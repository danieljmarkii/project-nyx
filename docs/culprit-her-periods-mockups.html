01

Four columns always; the rest earn their place

Every table opens with the same four: period, days, episodes, a week. After them come breakdowns, each with a rule for when it can be read. A breakdown shows when the pet's record can answer it, and only then. It is never chosen because its numbers look interesting, so the table can't go fishing.

Vomiting's breakdowns

  • After a meal. Within 30 minutes of a meal, or later. Shows when at least one period has meals logged that can time it. A period with food down all day says "unreadable".
  • Seen or found. Shows when any episode in the range was found rather than seen. Found ones stay inside the total.
  • Each sign brings its own list (CUL-2738): stool might break down by consistency, cough by time of day.

04

Motion: the record moving, and nothing else

The table holds still. The one thing that moves is the record itself: log a vomit, come back, and the current period's count ticks and its bar extends. Press play to see it.

05

Calls left

R-1 · Short periods (team call, yours to reverse)
Count always. Under 7 days, no rate. From 7 to 14 days, the rate prints with an open bar. Why: Dr. Chen needs the current period readable early, and the Data Scientist needs a reader to see that it rests on fewer days. Hiding the rate under 14 days, as round 1 drew it, hid the row that mattered most.
R-2 · The comparison line on a period's sheet
Name the last period on the same main food · no comparison line at all. Recommend naming it, chosen by that fixed rule only. The Designer flags the risk: it's the one sentence in the feature that sets two numbers side by side for the owner.
R-3 · How many periods on Patterns before "Every period"
Four · all of them. Recommend four: about one screen on a 390 pt phone, keeping the panel inside the fold.
