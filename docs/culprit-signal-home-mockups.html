02

The spine — now ten rules

The standing rules every frame obeys. S5 is rewritten by the Change Contract; S10 is your selective-receipts ruling, generalized.

  • S1The register drop. Safety cards stay austere — text, rail, sample line, and (new, §03) at most one count-anchored chip. No evidence graphic on the card face, no motion, no decorative ground. As benign cards get richer, plainness itself signals severity.
  • S2The control-side rule. No evidence visual ships numerator-only. If the control margin doesn't fit on the card, the visual moves to the expanded state where it does.
  • S3No borrowed authority. No composite scores, contributor bars, meters, or percentages. The sample line is the honest confidence display.
  • S4No hero numbers. The sentence stays the headline; counts stay subordinate with their qualifiers attached.
  • S5Change is counted, never verdicted. Change renders as a time-ordered count pair with a unit — 2 → 5 days — per the Change Contract (§03). No direction words, no ↑/↓, no percentages; the reader computes the direction. Falling pairs render on the insight lane only, density-gated.
  • S6Quiet is labeled. A presence-gated surface renders absence as one explicit line, never as silent shortening.
  • S7The record stays in daylight. No night grounds on the Signal or any record surface; night remains the working-on-the-pet's-behalf register.
  • S8Grazer-honest intake visuals. Day-level, drawn against the pet's own baseline band, never event-level activity.
  • S9Evidence one gesture away. Card face = sentence + glance evidence; tap = the mechanism; the safety tap = the phone-call script.
  • S10A receipt must earn its place. A strip renders only when its shape says something the sentence can't — clustering, distribution, a compared pair. A finding whose evidence is fully carried by its sample line stays sentence-only. Judgment per finding, not a template.
03

The Change Contract — negotiated with Dr. Chen

Your directive: consumer-centric, work with Dr. Chen to a version we can live with. The negotiation closed on one insight: his own sanctioned sentence — "5 days this week, up from 2" — already is change-over-time. What he vetoed was the arrow, which asserts direction without magnitude. A time-ordered count pair asserts only the record: 2 → 5 flattens nothing (2→3 and 2→9 read differently), so it carries more information with less alarm. The "→" between two counts is time's arrow, not a verdict's.

Five rules govern every chip: it may only compress the card's sample line (no new claim at the glance layer); it renders counts, never verdicts; it names the axis that rose (days vs episodes); it is server-computed, one predicate (the engine compares against the prior cached finding set — the client stays dumb); and every string is test-asserted through the same guardrail screens as the rest of the surface, with the full sentence as the a11y label.

RULED (round 2 → v1.1): the pair-chips are out — the sentence carries the change. Your CC-2 pick (baseline, "more streamlined") + CC-1's density read resolve the presentation: everything semantic in the table below stands (engine-computed change, count-anchored language, the density gate, the vetoes — all inside Dr. Chen's sanctioned envelope), but it renders in the sentence, where the shipped templates already put it ("up from 2 the week before"). The chip inventory shrinks to two: the existing Early pattern tag and a small New — the one fact a sentence doesn't naturally carry. S10 now governs the meta row too: nothing renders there that duplicates the sentence.

Why the safety lane is safe now: a safety finding can only rise or stop rendering — a falling trend never fires the detector, so resolution is disappearance, and disappearance is silence, not a claim. The lane structure carries the asymmetry that made arrows dangerous. "Resolved" stays vetoed (an all-clear inferred from absence). The fading problem: falling pairs are count-anchored, never worded — and during an active diet trial the expanded state appends one sentence: "A quieter week partway through a diet trial isn't the trial's verdict — the full run is what makes it readable." The density rule: a falling pair renders only when week-over-week logging density is comparable (falling density flatters, so the flattering render is what gets suppressed); a rising safety pair is never suppressed; the disclosure line lives one tap deep.

Dr. Chen, to you: "I gave ground on three things: change states on the safety lane at all, a glyph on safety cards, and a rendered weakening state during a trial. All three are safe because nothing new is claimed anywhere — every chip is a lossless compression of a sentence I had already sanctioned… This is the version I can defend to a colleague reading the card cold." Residual vetoes (small, named): ↑/↓ and slope glyphs, percentages, verdict words, chips on photo flags, week-pairs on chronicity, "Resolved."
