Medication history — one course model, three tenses

The vet asks "what medications has she been on?" and today the app cannot answer it anywhere. Three shipped surfaces — the profile card, the vet-visit rundown, the report's medication section — all filter to status = 'active', so the moment a course ends, the app forgets it everywhere except the raw dose stream. This round proposes the shape of the fix across four surfaces, for PM reaction. Nothing here is locked.

01

The grain rule

History already has a Medication filter — that half of the idea shipped. What it answers is what happened: dose events, one row per tap, newest first. The vet-chair question is a different grain entirely — what has she been on — and scrolling dose events backwards under pressure in an exam room fails the 10-second test badly.

Two grains, two jobs

History answers what happened — the event grain.
Dose events, timestamps, the evidence layer. It stays exactly as it is; every course-grain claim links back down into it.
whereas
Medication history answers what has she been on — the course grain.
"Metronidazole, fourteen days in March, twenty-six doses logged." A derived summary over the same rows, never a second source of truth.
The load-bearing decision: courses are derived from doses, not from regimens. On the PM's own account there are 2 regimens and 0 with durations — the dominant real shape is ad-hoc doses (B-394's finding). A history view that reads only medications rows renders near-empty for real accounts. This is the same call the med strip already won as its D2: ad-hoc tolerant, everyone gets value. The grouping machinery exists (attributeDosesToRegimens, the report's orphan-dose pass); the build is one shared course-summary derivation in lib/ that all four surfaces read.
02

Surface 1 — the profile's Past medications

The frames use your actual situation: a cat mid-diet-trial, a Motozol course ending, ad-hoc Zyrtec earlier in the record. Left is today; right is the proposal.

  • Two end registers, and the pill tells them apart. Ended renders only from an owner action (status IN (completed, stopped)) — a fact someone asserted. A course that simply went quiet gets No end recorded and a last-dose date. The app never promotes silence into an ending.
  • Section collapsed by default. Past courses are reference material, not daily state — the active cards keep the screen. The count on the label says there is history without spending the space.
  • Ad-hoc courses render. Zyrtec never had a regimen; it appears because the derivation groups doses by medication item. This is most of the real data.

No length, no schedule, no end — because none was recorded. The screen states first and last dose and stops. "No regimen set up" is a fact, not a scold; if B-302's promote-to-regimen nudge ever ships, this line is where its doorway would live.

The rundown block

Rows an owner can read out loud, in order

offlinedeterministic12-month window — a call to make

Every row leads with the drug name and gives dates the owner can say to a person across a desk. Current first, then past, newest first — the order the vet will ask in.

The one open design variable is the window. "Past 12 months" keeps the list speakable for Sam's chronic cat; the profile section (§02) carries the full lifetime. But a vet intake question is often genuinely lifetime ("has she ever been on steroids?") — the window and its label are a PM/Dr. Chen call, not settled here.

05

Surface 4 — the report's lifetime listing

The report's medication machinery mostly exists — Appendix D renders per-drug regimens and an adherence narrative, and the orphan-dose pass surfaces ad-hoc doses. But all of it is scoped to the report window: a 30-day report will not say "prednisolone in the spring." The proposal is one compact table that deliberately ignores the window, sitting beside — not replacing — the windowed adherence detail.

The table leads with the drug and the dates — the two things a vet scans for — and the footnote carries the disclosure that keeps it honest: absence of an end date is absence of a record, not evidence of continuation. The same two-register rule as §02, in clinical prose.

This frame is a proposal for a Tier-2 edit to nyx-vet-report-requirements.md (§3.8 / Appendix D), flagged for PM approval — the mock does not change the spec. Dr. Chen's lens says lifetime is the standard for referral records; the counter-consideration is report length on a long record, which is why this is one compact table and not a second appendix.

06

What these surfaces may never say

Inherited from the med strip's table and the diet trial's stale-active lesson, restated here because every one of them binds a history surface differently than a today surface.

RuleNeverInstead
H1 Completed — from silence "Last dose logged Jun 9." An ending renders only from an owner action. Nothing auto-completes a course, so stale-active is the steady state — a history view that promotes silence into an ending fabricates a clinical fact.
H2 An adherence percentage or grade Counted facts — "26 of 28 planned." A percentage judges the owner; the report's adherence narrative (honest about unconfirmed doses) already carries that register for the clinical reader.
H3 Did it help? ★★★☆☆ Nothing — or, someday, the computed symptom trend across the course dates. The C5 ruling stands: outcomes come from logged events, never from an owner-scored grade.
H4 A third course predicate One shared derivation in lib/, read by all four surfaces. dosesTowardTarget and lib/medStrip are the existing predicates; this feature reads them, it does not rival them.
