§00

What a vet recommends after the trial

The research first, before any app frame: what veterinary guidelines and the studies behind them recommend after an elimination diet trial, in plain English. Every claim comes from the frozen research brief, where each source was fetched and checked; each citation here links to its source.

Skin: itching, skin or ear infections

A test that confirms the diagnosis

The old diet comes back for up to two weeks. A flare, then improvement again on the trial diet, confirms a food allergy. It is "the sole means of confirming a diagnosis of food allergy" (AAHA 2023).

Gut: vomiting, diarrhea

A slow move off the diet, no test needed

Improving on the diet already confirms food-responsive gut disease. Feed it for at least 12 weeks before trying to move off it. Dogs with protein-losing enteropathy, a severe form, stay on it long term (ACVIM 2026).

Either

Stay on the diet: a complete outcome

A Cornell handout on gut disease: "If your dog's clinical signs have resolved, you have achieved success, and your dog can remain on this diet plan long-term" (Cornell). Many owners and vets stop here, and that is a finished answer, not an unfinished trial.

Itchy skin from a food allergy: the protocol, step by step

Elimination dietabout 8 weeks
Itch medicine stoppedabout 2 weeks before; far longer after an injection
Old diet backup to 2 weeks, or until symptoms return
Back on the trial dietif symptoms returned: up to 2 weeks to settle
Single foods (optional)1 to 2 weeks each, plus at least 7 settled days after any flare
Day 0Week 8Week 12About 6 months

Drawn to scale for a dog whose symptoms return near the end of the old diet's two weeks and who then tries five single foods. A pet that flares sooner moves on sooner; most stop before the single foods.

  1. Confirm the improvement. The vet rechecks after about 8 weeks on the trial diet; more than 90% of food-allergic pets respond by then (CAT(1)).
  2. Get ready. Stop itch medicine first: "generally 2 weeks" before, longer after a long-acting steroid or Cytopoint injection (Merck Veterinary Manual); a Cytopoint injection stretches a trial past 60 days (AAHA 2023). Keep a fresh bag of the old food: stored dry food can carry storage mites that cause a flare of their own (CAT(8)).
  3. Bring the old diet back. "The same food items (including treats and table scraps) as before" for up to two weeks, or until symptoms return (Sofou 2024).
  4. Read the result. If symptoms return, go back to the trial diet; if they clear within about 14 days, food allergy is confirmed, and if they don't, the vet checks for a skin infection. If nothing returns by day 14, food allergy is ruled out and the vet looks at environmental allergy instead (AAHA 2023).
  5. Optionally, find which food. One food at a time, added on top of the trial diet, for one to two weeks each ("not … more frequently than every 2 weeks", Merck Veterinary Manual), with at least 7 symptom-free days before the next (Udraite Vovk 2026). Start with what the pet ate before the trial. In older data the usual culprits are beef, dairy, chicken and wheat in dogs, and beef, fish and chicken in cats, though those counts may just reflect what pets were fed (CAT(2)). Most cases stop after step 4: about two thirds of reported food-allergic cats were only ever identified as reacting to their old commercial diet (Scott & Miller 2013).
  6. Long term. Avoid whatever flared. Tolerance can change: "dogs can develop new allergies to foods they previously tolerated" (VCA).

Gut disease: a different job

  • No diagnostic test. At least two weeks of response to the diet "confirms the food-responsive disease phenotype" (ACVIM 2026).
  • Twelve weeks before moving off it. The consensus gives no method; the one published protocol mixed the old food back in over a week (Glanemann 2021).
  • Protein-losing enteropathy stays on the diet. In one study, blood protein fell within four days of bringing a food back (Vaden 2000).
  • Not relapsing doesn't mean the diet didn't help. The benefit can come from the diet being more consistent, lower fat and highly digestible, not from avoiding an allergen (Tufts 2023; Simpson 2023). Relapse after a return to the original diet ranged from none to about 80% across dog studies.

Cats

  • The same skin protocol, two weeks long. Every cat source that states a length uses 10 to 14 days, or up to 14 (Noli 2021; Bryan & Frank 2010). The study behind the often-quoted 7 days says signs usually return within 7 days, "with rare cats not reacting until 14 days" (Scott & Miller 2013).
  • Never test a cat that isn't eating. Call the vet "if your cat refuses the food or only eats part of his meals" (CAVD); hospital guidance acts no later than three days after a cat stops eating (ISFM 2022).
  • Back to the old food to get eating is not a test. A vet may switch a cat back "for a short period to get him eating well again" (CAVD). In a food log it looks exactly like a test, which is why the reason has to be recorded.

The one rule every source shares

If symptoms return, stop that food, go back to the trial diet until settled, and only then try the next.

"Do not start another challenge with a new ingredient until the flare-up has disappeared completely" (FirstVet; also Coyner 2023). Everything else is the prescribing vet's call: outside the skin timing data, no default has evidence behind it.

Safety, and what to watch

  • Don't test: dogs with protein-losing enteropathy, or cats that have stopped eating (ACVIM 2026; CAVD). The consensus starts a diet trial only in a dog that is stable and eating, and the same caution plausibly applies to a test.
  • A history of hives or facial swelling: "the gradual introduction of food items, perhaps under direct medical supervision, may be indicated" (Sofou 2026).
  • An emergency, not a wait and see: no dog in the challenge studies had a severe reaction, but food is among the most common triggers of hives in dogs (Rostaher 2017). Dr. Chen's provisional rule: hives, facial swelling, trouble breathing or collapse during a test means a vet now.
  • Watch the gut as well as the skin: in one study, 7 of 10 dogs that reacted to a food had gut symptoms during the test, one of them gut symptoms only (Udraite Vovk 2026).
  • Settling time has never been measured. The only figures are protocol rules, from 7 symptom-free days to 2 to 4 weeks (Udraite Vovk 2026; FirstVet; Wilson & Datz 2022).

What the evidence can and can't tell a vet

Improving isn't the diagnosis.6 of 19 dogs (32%) and 8 of 17 cats (47%) that improved on the diet did not flare when the old food came back (Sofou 2024; Noli 2021). Season, flea control or a medicine can draw the same improving curve.
One flare on one food is weak evidence.In the only placebo-controlled study, 6 of 12 food-allergic dogs met flare criteria on a placebo week, and placebo weeks flared about as often as food weeks (Sofou 2026). The whole old diet is the better-supported test.
A flare is judged against the pet's own baseline.Fixed itch-score cutoffs would have read 25 to 92% of the dogs' real flares as negative, depending on the cutoff (Sofou 2026). The vet compares before with after.
Why two weeks for cats too.The often-quoted 7 days rests mostly on one study that only enrolled cats that had relapsed, with no stated watch length. Without it the cat data can't separate 7 days from 14 (a re-analysis of CAT(9)'s own tables), and every cat source that states a length uses 10 to 14.
Few owners ever do it.In one survey of dog owners before a first dermatology visit, 8 of 77 had fed the old diet again, and only a third remembered their vet raising it (Tiffany 2019).

§11

The rules every PR builds under

The brief's nine rules, after five isolated reads. Three are new (R10 to R12), and every one is a test a build can fail.

R1 · The vet plans; Culprit records.Culprit never proposes a food, an order or a length. Every plan surface shows where it came from (From your Sep 30 visit). The evidence default is a labelled choice, only where the owner has no number, and never "up to".
R2 · A planned food is never a slip.One reason function decides every feeding: permitted, then planned, then off-plan, in that precedence, and both the screen and the report switch on it. A slip inside a window stays off-plan. Feeding the food on after the window is its own population until the vet's word, never a slip and never a scolding.
R3 · Staying on is a finished answer.In two sizes: for good, and for now until the vet's date. Nothing nudges toward bringing a food back (D8 decides whether one line routes the owner's own question to the vet).
R4 · The result is the vet's.The owner's read is recorded in the owner's words; the vet's word as an instruction, attributed and dated. No verdict anywhere: not confirmed, safe, tolerated, passed, cleared, allergic, no reaction, fine. The brand name is never a verdict either: no "find the culprit".
R5 · Presence may speak; absence is never drawn or stated to the owner.No grid at the window-end question, no days-since counter, no count of days without a symptom, no "no symptoms" in an accessibility label. The report describes a quiet window only under its conditions (D6).
R6 · Stopping is one tap, on the trial screen.The owner confirms the first day they saw it; the logged day is kept beside it and never overwritten. Settling ends when the owner says so. No timer.
R7 · Safety leads, and has its own branch.Escalations, intake and the safety Signal lanes are never muted, and the refusal lane follows the phase. Facial swelling, hives or trouble breathing is a vet now. Window context never enters an AI prompt.
R8 · One food at a time, and the household runs both ways.A card the partner and the walker can act on; the two-cat line in both directions; results that name the pet.
R9 · Free.Pets > $: this is care. No entitlement check anywhere in the flow or the report block.
R10 · Nothing is final before the vet has spoken.The night-before hold; the question asked after the visit, never gated on Better; one answer counts on every surface.
R11 · A cat switched back to eat is not a window.While the record says a pet may not be eating, no window is offered; the switch is recorded with its reason and routed to health.
R12 · Owner words.Symptoms (the app's word). The stop button names the trial's own trouble: Itching's back, Stomach trouble's back, Symptoms are back. Never challenge, provocation, reintroduction, test or rechallenge on an owner surface; the report uses Dr. Chen's register.
