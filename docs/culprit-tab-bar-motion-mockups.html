A tab that answers the tap

Four live tab bars at phone size, one per option. Tap the tabs, or play the tour to run all four together. Slow motion stretches every gesture four times so you can study it; Reduce Motion shows what an owner with that setting gets.

What bends, and what still holds

The rules this round bends

No badge, no motion.
Tab bar spec §1, ruled 2026-08-22 (CUL-599)
Motion happens when the record changes state and nowhere else.
Principle 9, design principles v2.0, 2026-10-05

What they protect

  • Calm chrome. The bar never asks for attention.
  • Nothing loops. The tick stays the app's only loop.
  • Motion that means something, never motion that could be any app's.

What every option here keeps

  • Plays once, only on your tap. Never on its own, never on a second tap of the tab you are already on.
  • Done in half a second. The screen swaps instantly, as today.
  • Still under Reduce Motion: the new tab is simply on.
  • Silent. A tab switch carries no news, so no haptic.

The four bars

Each bar is drawn at the app's real size: a 26pt icon slot, a 22pt glyph, a 4pt dot. Biscuit has no photo here, so the Pet tab shows the initial, as the app does while a photo loads.

Your device asks for reduced motion, so the bars start still. Switch Reduce Motion off to see the gestures.

Option 3, one gesture at a time

Magnified so you can see each move. Every gesture lands with the same settle the Signal's fold uses: one overshoot of about 5%, never a second bounce.

R-1 · Which bar do we build?

DecidingWhether the tab bar answers a tap with motion, which amends the tab bar spec's "no motion" line and gives Principle 9 a carve-out for one-time tap feedback.
Options
  1. Still, as today.
  2. The dot travels to the new tab with one settle.
  3. Recommended. The dot travels, and each glyph makes its own gesture. The dot does most of the work; the clock running back and the ring drawing in are the two moves only this app would make.
  4. A generic bounce. Drawn as the foil: this is the motion that could be any app's.
DissentMotion & IA would take option 2: the dot alone already answers the tap, and four gestures are four things to tune on a real phone. The Designer holds out for option 3 because the glyph gestures are where the delight lives.
ConsequenceOptions 2 to 4 are a Tier 2 edit to the design principles and the tab bar spec, then the build. Option 1 closes CUL-2251.
