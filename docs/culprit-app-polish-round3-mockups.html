§02 · The Trend graphic, deliberately

Three ways to draw fourteen days

Fair call — the grey-and-rose bar strip is the least designed pixel on Home. The strongest move available: the Signal's evidence receipts (B-721) already established a house chart grammar — the dot lane and the stacked compare. Drawing the Trend zone in that same grammar makes Home speak one data language from top to bottom, instead of a receipt language on the Signal and a bar language two cards down. All three variants keep the ruled v2.1 furniture: the count-anchored sentence, today marked, the trial rule, accent-ink door, whole-card tap-through, tabular digits.

T-A · Fourteen days, as daysTeam rec

Two calendar weeks of day cells; an episode is a rose dot, a two-episode day is two dots — the receipts' dot-lane grammar, laid on a calendar. Days read as days, a bad day reads as a cluster (not a tall abstract bar), and the empty cells state the quiet honestly. The feeding mode uses the same grid with teal-filled days — one graphic system for both Trend modes, and a small kinship with the Patterns calendar.

A two-episode day carries an inner tick (shown Sat last week) so fill stays honest without dots.

The Oura-style stat tile: the number is the graphic, with a quiet 14-day micro-chart as its witness. Fastest possible read; the chart recedes to texture.

Data Scientist note: the micro-chart stays bars, never a line — a line would interpolate between discrete daily counts. Biggest register risk of the three: a large lone number can read as a score.
