What it costs to put Culprit on an iPad

Three possible answers, drawn to scale. The recommendation is the smallest one — because for this product, the iPad is where you read, not where you log.

Doc nyx-ipad-support-requirements.md v0.1 Backlog B-451 Measured 2026-07-26 on main Related B-269 · B-415
01

Where we actually are

Two facts frame this. First, ios.supportsTablet: false was set deliberately two days ago — PR #430, on the advice that "zero iPad QA has been run; add iPad when it's designed, not defaulted." This is a request to un-defer that properly, not a new idea.

Second, Culprit already runs on iPad today, in iPhone compatibility mode — a phone-shaped window floating on a 13-inch screen. The decision in front of you is not whether iPad owners can install it. It is whether what they see looks intentional.

The codebase is in unusually good shape for this: 198 flex-based layouts, 124 style blocks, only two hardcoded widths in the entire app. The port is not the expensive part. Keeping two form factors honest, forever, is.
02

What breaks when you just flip the flag

Setting supportsTablet: true is a one-line change, and that is exactly the trap. Nothing crashes. Every screen still works. It simply looks like nobody looked at it. Below is Home at real iPad-portrait proportions, beside the phone it was designed for.

Jordan logs while the dog is being weird on the kitchen floor: phone, pocket, ten seconds. Sam reads trends on the couch. Dr. Chen wants the report on a big screen. For Culprit, the iPad is a consumption device, not a capture device. That is the whole argument for spending an S here and not an L.

04

Scope A, drawn

The content column caps at 560pt and centres. The margin either side is the brand night ground — not grey. Culprit already reserves night for the moments the app is working on the pet's behalf, so the iPad margin reads as the product's own world rather than an unstyled letterbox. The tab bar caps with the column. The FAB anchors to the column's edge, not the screen's.

05

The one surface that improves for free

The vet report is already server-rendered HTML at viewport width=794 — A4. On a phone that is scaled down to fit 393pt. On an iPad in landscape there is enough room to render it at roughly 1:1, the size the vet will actually print it. No render changes. R9 is a verification requirement, not a build one.

06

What the L would buy you

For contrast, not for costing. This is the shape Scope B takes: the tab bar becomes a sidebar, the canvas becomes two columns, and lists gain the detail pane they have been asking for. Every one of those is a per-screen design decision across 39 screens, which is precisely why it is an L and not an S.

07

Requirements — Scope A

Each is verifiable on device. Full text in docs/nyx-ipad-support-requirements.md.

R1
ios.supportsTablet is true. Takes effect at the next native build-cut, not via eas update OTA.
R2
constants/theme.ts gains breakpoint tokens — a regular breakpoint at ≥700pt and contentMaxWidth (proposed 560pt). Tokens only.
R3
Above regular, primary scroll content constrains to contentMaxWidth and centres. Margins use colorBrandNight, not grey.
R4
The tab bar caps at contentMaxWidth and centres.
R5
The FAB re-anchors to the content column's right edge, never the screen's.
R6
Real bug, any scope. PhotoViewer and PhotoCarousel move from Dimensions.get('window') to useWindowDimensions(). Both compute a paging width; Dimensions.get does not re-render on resize, so both page at a stale width after rotation.
R7
All 13 Modal sites checked at ≥700pt — pageSheet presents as a centred card on iPad, not full-bleed.
R8
Full-bleed grounds (NightHeroGround, PullToRefreshSky) verified to fill, not tile or clip, at iPad aspect ratios.
R9
Vet-report WebView verified at iPad width. No render changes expected — verification, not build.
R10
app.json stays "orientation": "portrait". Shipping portrait-locked is legitimate; shipping a landscape layout nobody designed is not.
R11
Blocked — needs verification before build. Recent iPadOS windowing may allow arbitrary window widths regardless of the orientation key. If that holds against the current App Review baseline, R10 is insufficient, Scope A must handle arbitrary widths, and R6 becomes load-bearing rather than cosmetic. I have not confirmed this — check live guidelines.
