What it costs to put Culprit on an iPad

Scopes drawn to scale, then costed. iPad is on — so the question stopped being "whether" and became "how far, and what's the PR that turns a large into an extra-large."

Doc nyx-ipad-support-requirements.md v0.3 Backlog B-451 Measured 2026-07-26 on main Ruled D2 · D3 — D1 narrowed
01

Where we actually are

Two facts frame this. First, ios.supportsTablet: false was set deliberately two days ago — PR #430, on the advice that "zero iPad QA has been run; add iPad when it's designed, not defaulted." This is a request to un-defer that properly, not a new idea.

Second, Culprit already runs on iPad today, in iPhone compatibility mode — a phone-shaped window floating on a 13-inch screen. The decision in front of you is not whether iPad owners can install it. It is whether what they see looks intentional.

The codebase is in unusually good shape for this: 198 flex-based layouts, 124 style blocks, only two hardcoded widths in the entire app. The port is not the expensive part. Keeping two form factors honest, forever, is.
02

What breaks when you just flip the flag

Setting supportsTablet: true is a one-line change, and that is exactly the trap. Nothing crashes. Every screen still works. It simply looks like nobody looked at it. Below is Home at real iPad-portrait proportions, beside the phone it was designed for.

04

The narrow case, drawn

Correction — this section's claim was wrong

An earlier draft said these two figures were "what B− renders in portrait", on the basis of a 900pt sidebar breakpoint. That was an error. iPad portrait widths run ~744–1024pt, so three of five iPad models — including the 11" Pro at ~834pt — would have fallen below 900 and shown this centred column: the phone layout with margins. The breakpoint is now 700pt, and every full-screen iPad gets the iPad layout in both orientations. See §07.

So what these figures actually show is the compact fallback — a narrow Split View pane — and what Scope A alone would have shipped. The content column caps at 560pt and centres. The margin either side is the brand night ground, not grey: Culprit already reserves night for the moments the app is working on the pet's behalf, so the margin reads as the product's own world rather than an unstyled letterbox.

05

The one surface that improves for free

The vet report is already server-rendered HTML at viewport width=794 — A4. On a phone that is scaled down to fit 393pt. On an iPad in landscape there is enough room to render it at roughly 1:1, the size the vet will actually print it. No render changes. R9 is a verification requirement, not a build one.

06

What the L buys you

This is the shape: the tab bar becomes a sidebar, and the canvas becomes two columns. Everything in this frame is inside B− — it is PRs B2 and B3, and it is the part worth having.

What is not in the frame is the thing to notice. There is no detail pane. Tap an event here and it still pushes a route, exactly as it does on the phone. That is the whole difference between the L and the XL, and §07 explains why it is the line to hold.

07

What the L actually costs

First, the breakpoints — corrected. The plan originally put the sidebar at 900pt, which would have left three of five iPad models on the phone column in portrait, and would have handed an iPhone Pro Max a sidebar in landscape (it's ~932–956pt wide). Width alone doesn't identify a tablet.

TokenValueEffect
bpRegular700ptSidebar replaces the tab bar. Catches every full-screen iPad, mini through 13", both orientations
bpWide1000ptContent goes two-column. 11" portrait (834) → sidebar + one column; 11" landscape (1194) and 13" portrait (1024) → sidebar + two
contentMaxWidth560ptNow the compact fallback only — a narrow Split View pane, and iPhone

Both breakpoints are additionally gated on Platform.isPad, so width alone never promotes a phone. And landscape unlocks on iPad only, via the separate UISupportedInterfaceOrientations~ipad Info.plist key — a global orientation flip in app.json can't express that.

Now the cost. Risk isn't spread across 39 screens. Seven screens over 900 lines carry it — and four of those seven are capture flows.

What B− excludes, and why that's the whole point. No History → event pane. No Foods → food pane. A detail pane converts navigation from push-based to pane-based — event/[id] (1035 lines) and food/[id] (920) are currently routes, so making them panes means either rendering a route component inline or maintaining two presentations of the same screen. Every iPad port that has ballooned, ballooned here. Full B is 11 PRs plus a navigation-model change, and it's the navigation-model change that doesn't stay contained.

Calibration: B− at ~7 build PRs is roughly one standard feature track for this repo — the widget track was W1–W6, diet trial is PRs 1–7, Ask is A1–A8. It is not a new category of undertaking. Full B would be the largest track the repo has run.

08

Does this compound toward a vet login?

A vet-facing login is on the eventual roadmap, possibly desktop. Whether that makes B− a down payment or a detour turns on a fork worth naming now, because it's cheap to note and expensive to discover late.

If the vet surface is a separate web app — the React Native responsive work does not transfer. RN layout primitives are not web layout primitives. What does transfer is what already exists: generate-report emits real HTML at A4, which is already a web artifact and already the right shape for a vet portal.

If the vet surface lives in the same Expo codebase — B1's breakpoint tokens and B2's responsive shell are directly reused, and B− becomes foundation work for Scope C rather than iPad-only spend.

Neither answer changes the recommendation — iPad is justified on its own. But the second materially raises its value, and the fork should be decided before Scope C is scoped, not during it. Not a decision this doc asks for.

09

Requirements — Scope A foundation

Each is verifiable on device. Full text in docs/nyx-ipad-support-requirements.md.

R1
ios.supportsTablet is true. Takes effect at the next native build-cut, not via eas update OTA.
R2
constants/theme.ts gains breakpoint tokens — a regular breakpoint at ≥700pt and contentMaxWidth (proposed 560pt). Tokens only.
R3
Above regular, primary scroll content constrains to contentMaxWidth and centres. Margins use colorBrandNight, not grey.
R4
The tab bar caps at contentMaxWidth and centres.
R5
The FAB re-anchors to the content column's right edge, never the screen's.
R6
Real bug, any scope. PhotoViewer and PhotoCarousel move from Dimensions.get('window') to useWindowDimensions(). Both compute a paging width; Dimensions.get does not re-render on resize, so both page at a stale width after rotation.
R7
All 13 Modal sites checked at ≥700pt — pageSheet presents as a centred card on iPad, not full-bleed.
R8
Full-bleed grounds (NightHeroGround, PullToRefreshSky) verified to fill, not tile or clip, at iPad aspect ratios.
R9
Vet-report WebView verified at iPad width. No render changes expected — verification, not build.
R10
Amended under B−. app.json stays "orientation": "portrait" through B1–B6 — shipping a landscape layout nobody has designed yet is not legitimate. PR B7 unlocks it, once there is a designed landscape to unlock into.
R11
Blocked — needs verification before build. Recent iPadOS windowing may allow arbitrary window widths regardless of the orientation key. If that holds against the current App Review baseline, R10 is insufficient, Scope A must handle arbitrary widths, and R6 becomes load-bearing rather than cosmetic. I have not confirmed this — check live guidelines.
