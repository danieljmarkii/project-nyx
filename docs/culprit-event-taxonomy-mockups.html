02 · The confirm — B-745 register inheritance

Cough and sneeze confirm: deliberately boring, with two honest deltas

§14 predicted this frame "should be boring," and it is — the one-surface confirm, the summary-pill save, the teal register, all inherited untouched. The per-leaf contract shows up as two absences: no photo row (a cough has nothing to photograph — §6's per-leaf hasPhoto, so the zone never renders), and no Saw it / Found it chips. A vomit can be found on the rug at 5 PM; a cough cannot be found — it is heard or it is nothing, so the row writes witnessed by construction and one chip pair disappears rather than asking a question with only one honest answer. That is the CUL-606 lesson applied forward: withholding an affordance is cheap; one that stamps a claim the record cannot hold is not. Ruled at round 1 (now D10): the split is adopted — artifact leaves keep Saw it / Found it, witnessed-by-construction leaves drop it, and each leaf's contract row carries its confidenceModel.

The three change leaves — the one confirm that cannot be the boring inherited one Round 3 · frame 6

"Pee changes," "Drinking change," "Vocal change" are trend assertions captured as point events — Sam's question from the review names the problem: "my cat's been drinking more for about a week — do I log that today at 5:33 PM? Every day? Once?" The answer these frames encode: log it once, when you notice it. The row's time is the noticing moment — never rewritten — and the span lives beside it as an optional "since when" chip, stored as a per-family column per D14, so the record holds "noticed today, going on about a week" without pretending the change happened at 5:33 PM. Everything stays optional (Principle 1): a bare "Drinking change · today" is a valid save, and every chip only sharpens the summary pill. Logging it again later is re-noticing — "still going on" — not a duplicate. All three are observation-grade, data-only leaves at their wave (§9): no lane fires on them, so no chip here feeds an escalation rule.

04 · The breath counter — a new capture primitive

Thirty seconds, one tap per breath, in the calm register

respiratory_rate is the spine's one new primitive: a guided measurement (the Cardalis shape), structurally the weight_check pattern — an events row plus the measured value in a child table. It is a measurement, not a symptom: neutral tint, no rose anywhere, and the moment is designed calm — Sam counting a sleeping cat's breaths is a quiet ritual, not an emergency drill. The app does the arithmetic; the owner only taps. Canceling discards cleanly — no partial rows.

Priority flag from round 1 (PM): is this counter worth its W2 slot? Routed to the real vets — the §15 question sheet's Q4 is exactly this ("do you teach sleeping-breath-rate counting; would owner-logged counts be something you'd trust and use?") and is now marked the priority question for the next visit or message. The evidence pack's interim answer, for what it's worth: the sleeping rate is the single most-validated owner-collected measurement in the pack (four primary studies, the <30 threshold in both species, an existing tap-counter precedent in cardiology practice) — but validated ≠ prioritized, and the trio can ship as strain + labored first with the counter following the vet answer. The §17 fact-check pass on the <30 anchor is now discharged (2026-08-26): the number held, in both species — while asleep, which v1.3 made a rule rather than a copy preference (rule 11a; the numbers cite the Rishniw-group studies, never ACVIM, which is deliberately numberless and is the authority behind "use your vet's number for {pet}").

What this section does not draw this round: the sustained state (the second high count stating the finding and routing to the vet), the "Count again" path on 4d, and the rising-series surface. All three are rule-11 territory, and §9b holds rule 11(a) and 11(c) in open dispute — finding 2 says the sleep anchor may lock the tachypneic cat who cannot settle and the CHF dog who pants at rest out of the sustained state entirely, and finding 3 rejects the series rule's strict monotonicity. They are frames 4 and 10, held for CUL-684 (§00). The four frames below stand as the calm-register design authority they have been since round 1.

06 · Q7 — RULED: tracked events, as adopted (R6)

Per-pet, homed in Profile, safety always on — the mechanism as it will build

Q7 is closed (2026-08-26): tracked events adopted — and this round redraws the frames to the ruling rather than the exploration Round 3 · frame 9. The ruled shape: per-pet, homed on the pet's Profile (never account Settings — the grid is already per-pet via species, and a household's chronic cat is not its healthy dog); built no earlier than the wave that pushes a species grid past the fold (W3–W4); never offered at onboarding and never as a prompt — curation is a post-discovery act, found by someone who went looking, because offering it at setup asks a day-zero owner to predict the illness; the reveal is per-family, where the owner is already looking; and safety leaves are default-on and not hideable in v1 (P2, Dr. Chen ruling) — not-hideable is the conservative branch and does not wait on the §9a cooldown. The ground rules stand beneath all of it: hide ≠ delete (a hidden leaf changes the picker only — rows, detection, History, the report and §8's degradation contract never change), everything defaults on, and the grid never reorders itself (§6).

The alternative, kept as the record — the frequent row (round 1's §06, the Designer's exploration). Q7's ruling closed this one out loud: the row is permanently parked — including by its proposer. The tracked-events setting accomplishes the row's job at the root — it shrinks the grid itself rather than adding a shortcut above an unshrunk one — without spending a permanent row of vertical space on every open. The frame stays on the page as the recorded fallback if real use ever shows owners curating badly.

The owner personas on the density question (asked for at round 1)

Jordan (diet-trial dog, the 10-second test): at 3am with a vomiting dog she needs Vomit, Stool, or Cough — all inside the first two groups, in the same place they were yesterday, because the grid never reorders. The tiles below the fold cost her nothing she can feel; her risk was never the grid's length, it's anything that moves. Verdict: the full spine scales for her as drawn — symptoms-first order and spatial stability are the load-bearing parts, and both are rules, not hopes.

Sam (grazing cat, fussy-vs-sick): the long tail is where the app proves it speaks cat — Overgrooming and Hiding as first-class types is vocabulary she didn't have, and she would never have typed either into Other. The richness reads as competence… until it reads as homework: 28 tiles could feel like 28 obligations if anything ever frames the grid as a checklist. Verdict: scales, with one condition — the spine must always present as available vocabulary ("log what you notice"), never as coverage to complete. The tracked-events setting helps her twice: her chronic cat's grid stays rich while the healthy dog's stays short.

Where they land together: neither persona asks for a shorter default. Both ask for stability and framing — which is why the team's R6 recommendation is the toggle at full-spine density rather than trimming the spine itself.
