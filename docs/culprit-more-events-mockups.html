02

The vomit glyph — three candidates and one honest constraint

Why this is harder than poop: the poop emoji is universally recognizable because culture standardized one silhouette for the subject — the swirl. Vomit never got one. Its emoji (🤮) is a face — the recognizability lives in the face, not in any shape of the substance. So "universally recognizable without a face" has a real ceiling, and the three candidates below take three different positions on that trade. Each is shown plain, tinted in the rose wash circle, and at 16px (the History-row size).

V1 — The splat (blob + flecks)
The comic-strip "something landed here" splat: a lobed blob with two flecks. Energetic where round 2's puddle was placid. Faceless, so it stays clinical — but at 16px it can read as a flower or a stain.
G1b candidate
V2 — The spew (arcs + puddle)
Three arcs fanning down into a puddle — the motion of coming up and out, which is the recognizable half of the emoji minus the face. Reads directionally even at 16px; the risk is "wind lines" out of context.
G1b candidate
V3 — The profile (face + stream)
A minimal profile with a stream to a small pool — the emoji's actual recognizability, drawn in the house line. The register cost is real: this is the first face anywhere in the product, and it anthropomorphizes a symptom the app otherwise treats clinically.
G1b candidate — register risk
Team lean: V2, by elimination with conviction. V1 is a shape without a subject (nothing about a blob says vomit rather than mess); V3 buys recognizability with the product's first face — Dr. Chen and the voice rules both push back. V2 is the only candidate whose subject is the event (ejection → puddle), which is also what the owner actually witnessed and is logging. If none of the three convinces on the frames, the fallback stays round 1's Droplet — quiet, wrong-ish, shipped everywhere today.
03

Rung 2, rebuilt — the one-surface log

Round 2's sheet was rightly called out: presentation plus context lines is polish, not a stretch. The rebuilt Rung 2 changes what the flow is, not just where it renders: a simple event completes entirely inside the sheet. Tap Vomit and the picker morphs in place into that event's confirm — time (with Saw it / Found it), photo, note, Save — and the completion moment lands right there. Home never leaves the screen. Today the same log is FAB → modal screen → tile → second screen → Save; rebuilt, it's FAB → sheet → tile → Save. Meal, medication, and weight still route to their own screens — those flows are legitimately bigger — but the four symptoms and Other, the events this screen actually exists for, never leave the sheet.

Calibration, since that was the ask: this is two PRs of real work. PR A = the sheet presentation + the extracted picker (with the split stool tile and the pet switcher). PR B = restructuring app/log.tsx's step machine so the simple-event steps render as in-sheet states rather than screen swaps, keeping every write path, the completion moment, and the B-010 time affordance intact. That's the "achievable stretch" bar: a genuine flow rework you can still see the end of.

04

Rung 3, replaced — the capture bar

The dial is withdrawn — the challenge was fair: radial FAB fans peaked with Path in 2012 and no benchmark app ships one. The replacement swing comes from patterns the best-of-class apps do ship, including the one this repo names as its own engineering benchmark:

  • Linear's Cmd-K — one bar; ranked, context-aware suggestions first; typing optional. The fastest-feeling interaction in the product we benchmark engineering against.
  • Fantastical's natural-language entry — "lunch with Sam tuesday 1pm" becomes a structured event you confirm. The canonical "capture magic" precedent.
  • Things' quick-entry sheet — capture lands in a small sheet over your context, never a full screen.

Applied to Culprit: the FAB opens one small sheet with three layers, fastest first. Ranked suggestion chips the record can already predict (the active regimen's evening dose, the usual dinner, the Sunday weigh-in) — one tap each, they're confirmations in the B-614 sense. Then the type row — the full Rung-1 set, compressed. Then, for the moments neither covers, type what happened — "threw up after breakfast" parses into a structured event you confirm before anything is written. Same three-layer logic as Linear: suggested → browsable → typed.

Why this is the honest fences: it's the only rung that changes what logging is (from "navigate to the right form" to "say what happened, confirm"), and every piece has a best-of-class citation — Linear's ranked bar, Fantastical's parse-confirm, Things' sheet. It contains Rung 2 (the sheet + grid are its middle layer), so nothing built earlier is discarded. Still a prototype-first track: the parser needs a real lexicon, the suggestions need the B-614 confirmability gate (no pre-fillable dose ⇒ no chip), and multi-pet needs the switcher rule.
