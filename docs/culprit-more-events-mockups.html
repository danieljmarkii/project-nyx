02

Why the redesign is principled, not just prettier

Round 3's stage 2 was drawn as a form — sober rows, black-filled chips, a black Save bar. The reaction ("I hate the black design") points at something the system already knows: stage 2 isn't a form, it's a confirmation. By the time it renders, the app can fully describe the row it's about to write — type, pet, time, confidence. The B-614 ruling drew exactly this line: a control that opens a form is a second door; a control that writes a row the app could already describe is a confirmation. And the app's shipped confirmation register is the teal one — colorAccentLight wash + colorAccentInk text, the language of the completion cards and the med strip's one-tap. So the capture bar's parse-preview pill was never just a nice visual: it was the confirm register applied to capture. Stage 2 now speaks it natively:

  • The summary pill is the save. A live sentence — "Vomit · today at 5:33 PM" — in the teal wash, with Log it on it. Adjust a chip and the sentence updates; what you read is exactly what gets written. The only dark element left is the small Log-it pill, mirroring the FAB — the one "commit" mark on a light surface.
  • Chips select in teal, not black. Saw it / Found it selected state = teal wash + teal ink, the ChipGroup selection language, not a black fill.
  • Rows become light pills. Photo and note are bordered pills (photo keeps the dashed "additive" treatment — the documented exception, now on the surface it always meant).
  • The summary is confidence-honest. Flip to "Found it" and the sentence changes register: "found — sometime since this morning." The B-448 time-confidence model, spoken in the pill instead of hidden in a picker.

06

Round 5 — the title row carries the pet

Round 4 drew the sheet title as plain text plus a chevron. Everything else in the app that names the active pet leads with the photo — the Home header, the FAB's Logging for chip (whose own comment calls the name “the wrong-pet safeguard”), and every row of the switcher the owner just tapped. This is the one surface where the wrong answer writes a health row, and it is the one without the photo.

The reason it bites here and not on Home: the eight tiles are pet-independent, so they do not move. After a switch the only thing on screen that changed is one word, at the top of the sheet, with the finger still resting where the switcher row used to be.

The disc is a live state either way. PetAvatar renders the tinted initial as its floor and layers the photo over it, so a pet with no photo, a cold start, and a 404 all land on the same designed disc rather than an empty circle. Nothing here is a fallback branch.

06.1

The one fork — does a single-pet household see the disc?

The multi-pet spec §3.1 suppresses multi-pet chrome for single-pet owners — but it names the chevron, not the avatar, and the Home header already draws the disc for a one-pet account. The frames below are the two readings.

R5-1 · The single-pet disc — RULED B, 2026-08-29
Does the “Log for {pet}” row show the avatar when the household has one pet?
Deciding
One conditional in EventTypeSheet. Multi-pet accounts get the disc either way — this is only about the one-pet case.
Options
B — always. RULED. Matches the Home header, which already draws the disc for one pet, and keeps stage 1 and stage 2 on the same 30pt leading disc, so the header stops sliding 38pt on every single-pet log. §3.1 suppresses the chevron, which stays multi-pet-only under both options.
A — multi-pet only. Mirrors the FAB chip, which renders nothing at all for one pet. Cheapest read of “no multi-pet chrome”, but buys the stage-1→stage-2 jump.
Consequence
B closes the header jump for free and needs no follow-up. A leaves it, and the fix would then be a separate change to stage 2 instead.

Out of scope, deliberately. The copy split (Logging for on the FAB chip vs Log for here) and the single-pet row's VoiceOver “dimmed” announcement are CUL-682, which needs a device check first. The switcher's dismissal stays instant and un-animated — that is a ruled choice, so the retitled row is revealed immediately rather than behind a fade.
