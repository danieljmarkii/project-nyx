**How the rule got right (three passes).** The first hold sampled 24 h back from `now` and anchored on the latest firm day. Pass 1: Home regenerates several times a day, so a five-hour ④ window anchored the hold from a morning read and never from an evening one, and the card flipped twice a day at one count; the property had read at one time of day, where it is true by construction. The rewrite replayed at the exact change instants and immediately surfaced the second flaw: anchoring on the latest firm instant broke "never 'a word' at a count it said 'book' at" when a new episode briefly raised the count before an old one aged out. Anchoring on any earned instant in the course is the only rule that keeps that promise over arbitrary reads, and it is the §4.6 row as approved. Pass 2 then found day bounds built over signs ④'s floor does not count (a morning cough hid an evening meal's instant) and a walk too slow for the 2 s Edge CPU budget; pass 3 found nothing.

**Lessons.**
- A rule over `now` is only tested by reading at more than one time of day. A property read at one clock time and a replay sampled on a 24 h grid from that clock time agree with each other by construction.
- When a promise is about every pair of reads, derive the anchor from the promise: the latest-firm anchor looked like the obvious reading of "until the count falls" and was the one rule that failed it.
- A superset of instants is harmless; a superset of BOUNDS is not. Widening a day's first/last set replaces the true first event rather than adding to it.
- A stateless replay is costed before it ships: the first exact walk was 2.9 s at heavy logging against a 2 s CPU budget.
