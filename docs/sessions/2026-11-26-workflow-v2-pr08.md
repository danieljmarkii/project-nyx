## Teach

### Reversibility: a gate that errs costs a word, a gate that misses costs a pet (P2, L1)
Some decisions are two-way doors: if you were wrong, you walk back through and lose a little time. Others are one-way: once through, you can't undo what happened on the other side. Spend your caution on the one-way doors, and let the two-way ones be fast.

**Like:** a smoke alarm. A false alarm over toast costs you thirty seconds of fanning. A silent alarm over a real fire costs the house. So alarms are built to be oversensitive, and nobody calls that bad design.

**In today's work:** `scripts/steward/selfMerge.ts`
`export const OPEN_PRODUCT: Record<string, string> = {};` An empty open list means every product change waits for you. A wrong hold costs you one typed `merge`, a two-way door. A wrong open lets a clinical change reach an owner's screen unread. A later PR can revert the code, but not the night a warning didn't show.

**Why it matters to you as PM:** the replay says this gate would add about 140 words a month. The question to rule on is not whether that is too many words. It is which of those 140 were two-way doors all along, and that is exactly what naming a file in `OPEN_PRODUCT` decides, one door at a time.

**Check:** a change swaps the font size on the Settings screen. Under this gate it waits for your word. Is that hold a one-way or a two-way door, and what would make it safe to open?
