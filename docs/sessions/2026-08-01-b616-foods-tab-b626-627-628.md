## B-626 — restore per-pet context that doesn't scroll away

`app/(tabs)/foods.tsx`. A persistent `{Pet}'s library` subtitle now renders under the "Foods" title (the header `titleBlock` + `subtitle` style). The mock's screen A carried it; the build dropped it, and the only always-visible pet context was the trial strip — which scrolls away and is absent entirely with no trial running.

Three layers of this tab are scoped to the **active pet** over a **per-account** library: the reliable-favorites shelf, the per-row intake notes, and the B-616 trial chips + strip. Without a persistent anchor, the prior review read a bare `TRIAL DIET` chip on a shared library as ownerless, and a zero-chrome tab under an active pet (correct per D7 when that pet has no trial) as indistinguishable from a bug. The subtitle gives all of them one owner, and makes an empty trial band read as "this pet has no trial" rather than a dropped one.

Shown whenever there's an active pet — **not** gated on multi-pet or on a trial. The favorites/intake layers are per-pet on a single-pet account too, and a subtitle that appeared only with a second pet would make the header's context flicker as the household grows. `flexShrink` on the title block truncates a long pet name rather than pushing "Add food" off the edge.

Deliberately narrow: it does not name the *other* pet's trial on this pet's tab (that would be noise), and it does not change any off-trial behavior — it is pet context, present regardless of trial.

## B-627 — name the foods, don't count them

`lib/trialLibraryChrome.ts` + `components/foods/FoodsTrialStrip.tsx`. The strip's second line went from `3 foods on the trial list` to naming them: `{lead}, and N more` (e.g. `Royal Canin Hydrolyzed Protein HP, and 2 more`). The cold reviewer's reaction to the count was literally *"Three. Okay… which three?"* — they scrolled the library before tapping through. The identity of the three was one tap away by design, but the wedge's own surface was pointing at the 10-second answer instead of giving it.

- New pure helper `trialStripFoodsLine(foods)`: leads with the `primary_diet` row (the most identifying food, and the one the question is usually about) regardless of stored order; a permitted-only set (no primary — legal, rare) falls back to the first food. One food → just the label, no tail.
- `"and N more"`, not `"+2"` or `"and 2 others"` — it reads as a continuation of the named food (the vet's list continues), where a `+2` badge reads as the count this line is replacing.
- The strip's two `Text`s are now `numberOfLines={1}` — long product names truncate rather than wrapping the strip to three or four lines (context becoming content). Truncation is the accepted cost of naming (B-627 named it); the full list is one tap away on §2.2.
- The empty-set guard is unchanged — `buildFoodsTrialStrip` still returns no strip at all rather than a line about zero foods (a claim about the record).

## B-628 — frame whose call a mid-trial add is

`lib/trialFoodsScreen.ts` (`ADD_TRIAL_FOOD_CAPTION` + model field) + `components/profile/AddTrialFoodSheet.tsx`. A quiet caption now renders on the confirm sheet:

> Extras are your vet's call — Culprit just records the dates.

The add flow framed legitimacy from **neither** entry point. FR-9's vet line ("If your vet okays an extra, add it here") lives only on §2.2's empty "Also allowed" group — gone the moment a first extra exists, and never seen at all from food detail (FR-14). That left an anxious owner with nothing to hang legitimacy on at the moment of the write.

Placed on the **confirm sheet** because both entry points share it (`app/trial-foods.tsx` and `app/food/[id].tsx` both build the model via `buildAddTrialFoodSheet` and render the same `AddTrialFoodSheet`), so one line covers every path to an add — including the §2.2 *second*-add case the empty state has already vacated. This is the backlog's option (c); it changes both entry points, which is the point, not a cost.

It is **not** a wisdom-check, and that distinction is the whole design. D5 and Dr. Chen's mock-C note forbid "are you sure this fits the trial?" — second-guessing the vet's call judges the owner for following it. This line does the opposite: it states that extras *are* the vet's call (legitimacy, not interrogation) and that Culprit's job is only to date the record (reinforcing the "Earlier feedings — keep the reading they already have" fact and the C6 disclosure, and keeping the app out of the arbiter role). It never asks whether *this* food fits, never blocks, never marks anything off-diet. It is framing, not a fourth fact — the FR-11 "exactly three facts / two actions" contract is intact (asserted).

"Culprit" (not "Nyx"), matching the sibling C6 line in the same module.
