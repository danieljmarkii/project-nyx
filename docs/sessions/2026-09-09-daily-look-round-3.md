## The rulings the team made inside the PM's (each vetoable)

- **T-14 the day model:** a word marked twice in a day counts once; a day is *answered* with ≥ 1 look; an *observed-absence day* only when every look that day is `nothing_unusual`; the day's classification decides every glyph and count; within one look the energy poles clear each other.
- **T-15 the today list:** the two newest entries, the rest folded behind *N more today ›*; a quiet entry is quiet; Undo fades before the chevron takes its slot; the footer is a line, not a door.
- **T-16 the coverage line's three forms:** the ratio below full coverage · the window alone at 28 of 28 · nothing below fourteen answered days; only under a today list; after a withheld period it returns as the window alone.
- **T-17 the pairing receipt lives on Patterns and the report**, both sides answered days, the unanswered vomit days said, never a zero or a one on the left, ≥ 3 answered vomit days and ≥ 10 answered other days.
- **T-18 receipts** are derived at render, earned by symptom-class words only, absent below the floor; the count form uses the footer's window and its own ≥ 7 floor; the first-day form says *before it*.
- **T-19 one day, stored once:** `looks.local_day`, written on the device, counted by every surface, because the client and the report bucket days on two clocks.
- **T-20 the withheld state** is triggered by a positive intake fact, never by ignorance; the entry says why and points at the record; one predicate for Home, Patterns and the report's line.
- **T-9 amended:** the returned meal nudge names the meal. **§5.1 1b:** the Recap lead never says *nothing logged as a symptom* beside *off* (Q-17, vetoable).

## Appendix A — the product read, verbatim (`pm-feature-review`, isolated, run on round 3 as first drawn; every blocking finding folded)

> Round 3 is the strongest version of this feature yet — the arrival beat is genuinely good, the two-taps-and-done path clears the 10-second test on both personas, and the receipts do something no other surface in the app does: they pay an owner back on the morning it matters. But three things drawn on this page work against each other, and all three are owner-visible. **The coverage footer defeats the receipt's own low-coverage floor** — §03's caption promises "a worried owner on day four never reads a coverage message" and the frame directly beneath it reads *"Answered 4 of the last 28 days."* **The new selected state re-creates the blocking finding the last product read got fixed**: the check glyph widens a chip by ~13px inside a wrapping row, so the chips after it move as Jordan reaches for her second word. And **the meal nudge comes back asking the look's question** — "Nothing logged yet — how's Mochi doing?" renders on a frame with a look already on the lane, which is factually wrong, breaks the shipped rule that the nudge vanishes the moment anything is logged, and is the exact "two asks stacked on Home" that T-9 exists to prevent. Underneath those, one quieter inversion deserves a PM ruling on its own: §3.3 says *"the bold word is the act, never the outcome"* — earned because bolding the reassuring half made it the headline for 56 mornings — and round 3's entry now renders the outcome alone, in the largest type on the card. On a quiet day the biggest words in the Noticed card are "Nothing unusual." The concept ships; five frames need another pass.

### Wedge & brand

This serves the wedge better than anything else currently drawn for Home v2. Jordan sent home with a diet trial has a record full of what Mochi *ate* and nothing about how he *seemed*, and the receipt — *"First day Mochi has seemed off — in the 118 days you've answered since May 3"* — is precisely the sentence a vet asks for and an owner cannot reconstruct. Sam's case is served on the compact card too: "Hiding" costs the same tap as "Nothing unusual," which is the safety asymmetry inverted correctly. Pets > $ holds — nothing on this page is gated, and floor item 13 says so out loud. The brand risk is not monetization, it's **accretion**: R1 puts this card on every account forever, and round 3 grew the answered card from one collapsed row into a four-to-six-row stack that never shrinks. Principle 3 lists "a log feed" among the things that are not on the home screen; a newest-first, timestamped, uncapped list of today's entries inside a Home card is a small one. It is defensible at one or two looks a day and stops being defensible at five — and Sam, the grazing-cat owner, is the persona most likely to make five.

### 🎨 Design / principle / voice gaps

- **[10-sec | regression] §02 — the check glyph moves the next tap target.** `.chip.on::before` adds a `✓` at 10px plus `margin-right: 5px`, and `.chip.on` bumps `font-weight` 500→600 (`:130–131`) — roughly 13px of added width per selected chip inside a wrapping row. §3.1a binds the opposite: *"the head words hold their positions while the card is open, and nothing re-sorts under the owner's thumb (the product read's first finding)"* (`docs/nyx-daily-look-requirements.md:108`), and the previous `pm-feature-review` logged "chips reorder on selection so the next tap target moves" as blocking (`:512`). Different mechanism, same owner experience. Cheap fix: reserve the check's width in the unselected state (a transparent glyph), so selection changes colour and never geometry. **Needs the device render to confirm the re-wrap.**
- **[P3] The answered card grows and never shrinks, with no collapse rule.** Two looks → entry, entry, ask, footer; three looks → five rows plus a receipt. The med strip has a §7 collapse rule for exactly this reason and this card has none. §3.3's option (c) — the quiet row shrinking over time — was retired, and the opposite shipped. At minimum: a cap with a "2 more today" fold.
- **[P5] Patterns has a door from day 1 and no drawn empty state.** "Patterns ›" sits on the card label in every state including day 1 (`:635`), by design (§3.3). Nothing in §06 draws what an owner finds behind it on day 3, and DL-5 has no gate for it. Principle 5 says the empty state is the feature; this is a live door to an undrawn room.
- **[voice] Three registers for one record.** The footer says the owner **answered** (`:513`), the report says she **marked** (`:769`), and the receipt says the pet **has seemed** off (`:511`). "Answered" and "marked" are adversarially earned (G9: the app speaks the act it can vouch for, never the observation). The receipt is the one string where the app speaks *for the pet*. §3.3 asks for "the pet as the subject" and §7/§8 ask for the act — the spec disagrees with itself and the mock renders both. Pick one; I'd keep "answered/marked" and rewrite the receipt as *"First day you've marked Mochi off — you've answered on 118 days since May 3."*
- **[voice] The compact hint's third clause is app-mechanics.** *"Tap what you saw · more than one can be true · any time of day"* (`:356`). The slot used to carry the pip legend; with L-9 retired it got backfilled with a scheduling fact an owner doesn't need at 7am. Either drop to two clauses or say something about the pet.
- **[P3 | §3.3 inversion] The outcome is now the headline.** §3.3 rules *"the bold word is the act, never the outcome — the row reports what the owner did (Jordan: bolding 'nothing unusual' made the reassuring half the headline for 56 mornings)"* (`:140`). Round 3's entry is `ring · "Nothing unusual" · 7:12` in serif 15px (`:653`), the largest type in the card, with the act demoted to the section label and a glyph. §08 states this as intentional ("the verb sits on the label and the words stand alone"), which is a defensible design — but it reverses a rule the spec says was earned from a named review finding, and it should be ruled, not absorbed.
- **[legibility, probe a] The four-line stack mostly works, with one seam.** Serif entry / ruled indented receipt / sans ask / tabular footer do separate by family and by rule — the "nothing wraps into anything else" goal is met, and that was a real round-2 complaint well answered. The seam is that the ask row's "▾" and the footer's "Patterns ›" are two teal right-edge affordances ~8px apart doing completely different things (open the chips in place vs. leave the screen), with `padding: 8px 0 2px` and `padding-top: 8px` facing each other (`:163`, `:165`). Under C-5 that gap does not cover two hitSlops. Also, in the prototype the *whole* ask row is tappable (`data-act="open"`, `:1028`) while only the glyph is tinted, and `.cov` carries a `data-k` with no handler — so whether the coverage row is a door or just has one is undecided.

### Counterexamples tried

#### 1. The pairing receipt (R12c) — three independent breaks, the worst thing on the page

**(a) The control margin uses two different eligibility populations.**
Counterexample: 28 days; owner answers on 20 of them; vomit logged on 3 days, of which only **1** was answered (a vomit day is a chaotic day — the owner does not stop to tap chips); lip-licking marked on that one answered vomit day and on 4 of the 19 other answered days.
Rendered, per the mock string at `culprit-daily-look-mockups.html:594`:

> `Off on <b>2</b> of the <b>3</b> days a vomit was logged · and on <b>1</b> of the <b>20</b> other days you answered.`

…i.e. for my fixture: *"Lip-licking on 1 of the 3 days a vomit was logged · and on 4 of the 19 other days you answered"* → 33% vs 21%, reads as nothing. Answered-restricted truth: **1 of 1 (100%) vs 4 of 19 (21%)**.
**BROKE.** The left denominator is *all vomit days*; the right is *answered non-vomit days*. An unanswered vomit day can enter the denominator and can never enter the numerator, so every logging gap on a bad day pushes the association toward null. This is "didn't log" scored as "didn't happen", and the failure direction is reassurance. Same defect in the spec text at `nyx-daily-look-requirements.md:438` (§6.11: *"subdued on 3 of the 4 days a vomit was logged · and on 3 of the 20 other looked days"*).

**(b) It can render a zero and reassure.**
Counterexample: 3 answered vomit days, lip-licking on **0**; 20 other answered days, lip-licking on 5. The receipt is *earned* by tapping lip-licking today, so it renders: *"Lip-licking on 0 of the 3 days a vomit was logged · and on 5 of the 20 other days you answered."*
**BROKE.** That is an absence claim at n=3 rendered on Home on the day the owner was worried enough to tap the word. Floor item 5 ("a look… may only raise") and the standing *n=1 never reassures* invariant both bind here and neither is expressed as a rule that stops this render.

**(c) It is an uncorrected multiple-comparison family, selected on the outcome, sitting beside gated engine claims.**
Counterexample: 26–28 words × {vomit, diarrhea, stool} co-occurrence, over 20–24 answered days with 3–4 vomit days. The receipt renders for **whichever word the owner just tapped**, i.e. the comparison is chosen by the exposure. 2/3 vs 1/20 is p≈0.03 unadjusted and ≈0.8 across a 26-word family. `detection.ts:141–146` explicitly treats family size as load-bearing ("REMOVING a type from the `correlation` cell shrinks ①'s Bonferroni family… the remaining candidates' bar loosens"), and lane ① pays for exactly this shape with a correction, floors and a discordant-pair test. The receipt pays nothing.
And it renders **on Home, one card below a gated finding** — the mock's own §01 third frame (`:384`) draws the chronicity card *"Mochi has vomited on 14 days across 5 of the last 8 weeks"*; the R12c receipt would sit under it making a second, uncorrected claim about the same vomits. The precedent is in the repo verbatim, at `components/home/TrendZone.tsx:81`: *"An ungated duplicate of a gated claim is not a duplicate; it is a bypass."*
**BROKE.**

**v0.3 rule (all three):** the pairing receipt renders only when (i) **both sides are restricted to answered days** and the left denominator is stated as such (*"of the N days you answered that a vomit was logged"*), with unanswered vomit days disclosed in the same sentence or the receipt withheld; (ii) the co-occurrence numerator is **≥2 and non-zero** — a zero co-occurrence is never rendered, in any wording; (iii) a minimum of **≥3 answered days carrying the paired event** and ≥10 answered control days; (iv) it does **not render on Home at all** — it is a Patterns/report object, where a reader is deliberate and the counts sit beside the record they came from; (v) the sentence names its unit (`days with ≥1 vomit`, never the report's episode count — see G11). If the PM wants it on Home, it needs the same review the engine's ① lane got, not a "nugget" slot.

#### 3. The coverage footer under R1 (attack 1)

**(a) At full coverage it is a streak, and the stated mitigation is false.**
Counterexample: a healthy dog, owner answers every day for a month. Footer reads **"Answered 28 of the last 28 days."** The mock's own justification, `culprit-daily-look-mockups.html:620`:

> "It counts the owner's act in a fixed window, so on day 200 it reads like day 40 (**the number floats between 0 and 28; nothing grows**), and it is **never a streak (no consecutive count)**…"

**BROKE, twice.** The number does not float at the top of its range — it **saturates**, and a saturated rolling count of eligible days *is* a consecutive count: 28 of the last 28 days is, definitionally, 28 consecutive days answered. The one owner for whom the mitigation is claimed (the reflex tapper, ledger row 12) is precisely the owner who lives at the saturation point. On the C-3 question: C-3's suppression clause was written for a denominator the app controls, so it does not *bind* here — but C-3 is not the rule that rescues this line, and v0.3 must not cite it as if the carve-out settled the hazard.
**v0.3 rule:** name the full-coverage string explicitly and prove it is not a score. The only forms I can defend: state the window without a ratio at saturation (*"You've answered on every day of the last 28 — Patterns ›"* is worse, not better), or **render the footer only on Patterns and the report** (overruled), or **cap the render**: the footer states the ratio only while it is below the window, and at full coverage states the window alone (*"Counted across the last 28 days"*). Whichever the PM picks, R11's mitigation sentence must be corrected in the spec — it currently asserts something the arithmetic contradicts.

**(b) The day the number drops is a scored loss, and the withheld state makes it worse.**
Counterexample: owner at 28/28; pet gets sick; owner spends the day at the vet and does not answer; next day the footer reads 27/28. Now compound with R11's withholding: the intake concern fires → the footer **disappears** for the duration of the concern → when the concern clears, the footer returns reading **21 of 28**. The sequence an owner reads is: *perfect · gone · punished*, all driven by her pet being ill.
**BROKE (design-level).** **v0.3 rule:** the footer never renders a change, a delta or a return-to-view as an event; and the spec must state what the owner sees on the *first* render after a withholding period — silently returning a lower number is the app scoring her for a hospitalisation.

**(c) Home and Patterns disagree about the same withholding, one tap apart.**
R11: the footer is *"withheld together with the words under an intake concern (floor item 12)"*. `nyx-daily-look-requirements.md:447` (§7): *"So the line always prints; the un-looked count is implied by the pair, never stated as a failure."* And the withheld Home card **keeps its `Patterns ›` door** (`culprit-daily-look-mockups.html:670`).
**BROKE.** Withheld on Home, printed on Patterns, with the door to Patterns sitting inside the withheld card. This is the B-789 lesson inverted (one predicate, two consumers, `dietTrialCard.ts:745`). **v0.3 rule:** one withholding predicate, consumed by Home, Patterns *and* the report's look line, stated once; if Patterns may print it and Home may not, say why in the spec, because a reader will find the number in one tap either way.

**(d) The footer's render condition is contradicted by the mock.**
R11 says the footer prints "once ≥1 look exists in the window". The prototype prints it only when **today** has an entry (`culprit-daily-look-mockups.html:1001` `var answered = state.today.length > 0;` → `:1030` `if (answered) html += '<div class="cov">…'`), and §04's skipped-day/day-1 frames carry no footer. The two readings have opposite hazards: *print on a skipped day* = a score on the resting card, breaking §3.3's "a skip costs nothing on screen"; *print only when today is answered* = the number is a reward for answering today and is invisible exactly when a lapse would be informative.
**BROKE (unruled).** **v0.3 rule:** pick one and state the reason; and state that **Undo of the day's only look removes the footer** (the prototype does this at `:1116–1119`) so an Undo does not read as a failed save.

**(e) Local-midnight arithmetic, travel, and "Change time".**
- Two day counters, not one. R11 cites "B-421's one counter", but `lib/analytics.ts:876–879` states in terms: *"The vet report keeps its own counter (`generate-report/report.ts`), which is NOT this one — see B-442."* The report buckets on `user_profiles.timezone` with a **UTC fallback** (`generate-report/report.ts:686–689`), the client on the device zone. Counterexample: owner in UTC−7 with a null `user_profiles.timezone`, looks logged at 6–8 PM local. Home: "Answered 28 of the last 28 days." The report, bucketing those looks into the *next* UTC day: a different answered-day count over the same rows. **BROKE.** **v0.3 rule:** either the look row stores a **local day key at write time** (and every surface counts that key), or the spec states which counter each surface uses and that they may differ — and the report's line must never be described as "the same count Home shows".
- A timezone change mid-window silently merges or splits answered days with no owner act, moving a number the copy attributes to her act. **BROKE (unstated).** Same rule.
- A look at 11:58 PM moved across midnight by "Change time": if yesterday held only that look and today already holds one, the answered-day count **drops by one** because the owner corrected a timestamp. Honest under the day model, unstated in the spec. **v0.3 rule:** say it, route the edit through `sourceAfterPointEdit` (C-10), and pin it with a timezone-honest fixture anchored to `Date.now()` (C-29 / CUL-831) — a fixture on an absolute date will fail on a calendar boundary rather than on a change.

#### 4. The first-day receipt (R12a) below the floor — stripping the denominator *strengthens* the claim

Counterexample: owner installs in May, answers on 4 days out of 60, then taps *Off*. Q-13's provisional floor (14 answered days) drops the denominator, and the mock renders (`culprit-daily-look-mockups.html:601`):

> `First day Mochi has seemed <b>off</b>.`

**BROKE.** A novelty claim implies the absence of prior instances, and the record can support that implication only to the extent of coverage. With 4 answered days in 60, "first day he's seemed off" reads as "he's been fine until now" — reassurance by implication, produced by removing the one clause that qualified it. §3.3's stated rationale ("so a worried owner on day four never reads a coverage message") optimises the wrong thing: the coverage clause is not noise, it is the claim's warrant.
**v0.3 rule:** below the floor the first-day form either names the coverage in a non-rate form (*"The first time you've marked off — you've answered on 4 days so far"*) or **does not render at all**. It never renders naked. Note also that R12's own provisional says the sub-floor form is "a bare date"; the mock draws no date. Reconcile.

**Sub-attack — the denominator's boundary is stated on the report and dropped on Home.** Report line (`culprit-daily-look-mockups.html:769`): *"off on 6 (first Sep 2; answered on 118 days **before it** since May 3)"*. Home receipt (`:574`, `:995`, and §3.3 at `nyx-daily-look-requirements.md:140`): *"in the 118 days you've answered since May 3"* — no "before it". **BROKE (string-level):** the Home form is ambiguous about whether the day the word appeared is inside its own denominator, and if the implementation includes it the sentence is self-contradictory. **v0.3 rule:** the denominator is answered days **strictly before** the first-marked local day, and the string says so on every surface.

**Sub-attack — Undo / delete / backdate.** The prototype models it correctly (`:1118`: `if (gone.firstOff) state.firstOffSeen = false;`) but the spec never states the derivation. **v0.3 rule:** every receipt is **derived at render from the record as it stands** (soft-deleted rows excluded, backdated rows placed by `occurred_at`'s local day), never stored on the look row and never latched in state — so an Undo re-arms it, a later backdated earlier *Off* moves the "first day" and the old claim stops being rendered anywhere.

**(b) The bars hide onset, and the two figures cover different windows.**

**(c) A blank cell under a ▲ is a logging gap read as an observation.**
