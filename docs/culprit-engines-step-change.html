The ask never resolves

"Worth booking a vet visit" was right: vomiting every two to four days is moderate to severe on the vets' own index. But it is a latch with no reset. The engine reads neither visits nor treatment, so after Sep 16 it had nothing new to say and kept saying the old thing.

The step change

Six moves, in the order they depend on each other

Measure

Replay the shipped engines over synthetic pets with known truth, every day of a simulated year. Report how often a healthy pet sees a card, how fast a real problem is caught, and how many evenings carry a vet ask.

EN-1 · CUL-508 · EN-2
Calibrate the read

Four tiers instead of one warning. Count episodes the way vets do, check every vomit with or without a photo, ask whether she has eaten instead of guessing, and tell the vision model what counts and what fools it.

EN-3 to EN-7
A care state

Say a concern once. Hear that the vet has seen it. Then watch for change: more vomiting, weight loss, a new warning sign. Stand down only when the owner says so.

EN-9 · EN-10
See the whole animal

Weight becomes a lane, with each reading's source. Visits and treatments sit beside the trajectories as dates and counts, never as causes. A problem list composes it for the vet.

EN-8 · EN-15
Honest inference

No culprit card without a test. A lifetime budget for chance cards, measured on synthetic pets. A backwards-in-time check that catches owners changing food because the pet vomited.

EN-11 to EN-13
Close the loop

One tap to say you called, and one follow-up: "What did the vet say?" The answers are how the engine learns which warnings earn their place.

Why this is the moat

Anyone can copy a detector. Nobody has the loop.

Every method in this plan is published, and a competitor could ship a vomit-photo classifier next month; one already exists, unvalidated. What cannot be copied is the record behind each warning: the photo, the structured read, the context, the tier the owner saw, what the owner did, and what the vet said. No pet product publishes outcomes for its alerts. The one that knows, per warning, whether it was right is the one vets can trust, and owners keep listening to.

The evidence for the order: symptom checkers and language models over-triage by default, repeats drive people to ignore alerts, graded alarms beat binary ones, and consumer alerts that kept their users were rare and confirmed first. Sources are in the evidence pack.

How this was done

Replayed, researched, and attacked

  • Both engines replayed over the real record with a fidelity check: the Signal matches the live cache on Sep 23, and the read's context rule reproduces all 43 stored flag sets.
  • Six isolated research lanes: two source maps, a ledger of every earlier recommendation, the statistical methods frontier, veterinary vomiting evidence, and AI triage and alarm fatigue.
  • An isolated adversarial pass failed two of the first-draft proposals: counting episodes could merge a retching dog into one, and ignoring unrated meals would have silenced the only intake signal during the weight loss. Both are amended, and three overstated claims are softened.
  • Every clinical threshold is still set by the Dr. Chen persona, not a veterinarian. That was your call this round, and no public "clinical-grade" claim should rest on it.
