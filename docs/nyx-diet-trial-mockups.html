The two rules — they govern every screen below SPINE

Everything here is drawn against two rules that cannot be traded off against layout.
  • R1 — the negative claim is deleted from the product. “No off-diet foods logged” renders at no coverage level, on no surface. Every claim is in positive form about the record, carries both denominators, and the blind-spot qualifier is inline on the claim — never a page legend. The exposure count is a floor, never a total. Two-sided: below the floor Nyx also raises no absence-based alarm. (G2, ruled as a rule rather than a threshold.)
  • R2 — no blended coverage/adherence metric, in any form. Not a string, not a ring, not a badge, not a grade, not a colour, and not a bar width. Any progress bar on any screen below encodes getDietTrialProgress().fraction — day progress — and nothing else. (D2; and §6.9: Nyx never scores the owner, so coverage is never a percentage, bar, streak or badge anywhere.)

Held constant in both variants — the C5 render

C5 rejected owner-scored severity and replaced it with disclosure: the symptom trend is rendered against logging density over the same window, so a vet can see whether a falling symptom count tracks a falling total-log count. An owner-logged stream decays with attention — highest at trial start, lowest by week 6 — so a falling itch count is biased toward apparent improvement, and rendering the two series together makes the bias visible instead of correcting it with an instrument the app has refused on every event type.

Held constant across every screen

  • No negative claim, at any coverage, on any surface — positive form about the record, both denominators, blind-spot qualifier inline on the claim, exposure count is a floor never a total. Two-sided: below the floor, no absence-based alarms either.
  • No blended metric in any form — string, bar width, ring, meter, badge, grade or colour. Every bar is day progress and nothing else.
  • Nyx never scores the trial, and never scores the owner. Coverage is a statement about the record, never about the person — no percentage, streak, badge or grade anywhere. The owner reports the outcome; the vet decides what it means.
  • Record and continue. Every exposure surface carries a continuation statement. No copy implies the trial is voided, compromised or must restart — and no quantified reassurance, ever.
  • An oral-route exposure is never a reason to skip a dose. It names the fact and points at the vet for a substitution. A missed critical dose is the worse outcome.
  • Every flag is tappable to its reason, naming which rung fired. Disclosure includes explainability.
  • Non-blocking at the moment of the event (Principle 1) — nothing here warns, verdicts or colour-codes at log time. A pre-decision surface may verdict; a record surface may not.
  • Refusal routes to the health lane, not preference — and while an intake-decline flag is live, the card is structurally incapable of rendering an adherence line.
  • An abandoned trial is a clinical fact, not a failure state.
