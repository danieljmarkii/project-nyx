Six screens. The eleven “states” are one card with one line that changes.

Where the real complexity is, and it isn’t the UIIt is lib/dietTrial.ts — the one shared predicate behind every number on every surface (PR 5). That is genuinely intricate and it is load-bearing: four ordered rungs, dated membership, food identity by brand+product rather than UUID, treats in one denominator but not the other. It is also invisible — no amount of UI simplification reduces it, and no UI here can be made honest without it.

If you want the lever — a smaller v1

Everything below can be cut without breaking a safety rule or stranding schema. Listed in the order I would cut them.

  • Cut the exposure list + the reason sheet (PR 5’s UI half). Keep detection, keep the counts on the card and the itemised list on the vet report. Cost: §5.3’s “every flag is tappable to its reason” becomes a fast-follow, and until it lands a flag the owner can’t interrogate is a weaker promise. Saves: two screens.
  • Cut the milestone’s outcome sheet (PR 6’s second step); keep the action-first milestone. Cost: the vet report loses the owner-reported outcome. Saves: one sheet plus the outcome write path.
  • Cut A-3’s mid-trial state. Pure addition; nothing depends on it.
  • Cut the free-fed and multi-pet card variants — only if you also accept that the card renders no adherence line for those pets rather than a wrong one. The variants are cheaper than the suppression logic, so this one probably saves nothing.
What cannot be cutThe intake-decline replacement and the refusal-terminal-state rule. Both exist so the card cannot render a clean trial over an animal that isn’t eating — the failure §5.2 was rewritten around, and the one the Jordan review caught shipping in round 1. They are conditionals, not screens, so they are close to free anyway.

Do the trial calculations use secondary proteins? ROUND 4 — UNBLOCKED BY SHIPPING ALL OF B-351

Yes, once B-351 lands. And the FAB case is already covered — but there is a different hole one step over.

The PM’s instinct is the exact premise B-351 was built on. Migration 039’s own header says it outright: “the ‘duck’ novel-protein food that also lists chicken by-product meal is the textbook elimination-trial contaminant.” So yes — the lurker proteins are the ones that break trials. Here is the honest state of it:

LayerState todayUses secondary proteins?
Schema — food_items.proteins TEXT[]✅ shipped (migration 039). Prominence-ordered canonical keys; proteins[0] is the primary and is hoisted so a “duck” formula whose panel lists chicken first still reads duck as primary.Yes — the whole set is stored
Capture — AI extraction✅ shipped (B-351 PR 2). extract-food-from-photo writes the full set via deriveProteinSet.Yes
Capture — the manual picker⬜ B-351 slice 3, not shipped. A hand-entered food still gets one protein.No
Local mirror — SQLite cache✅ shipped. lib/sync.ts round-trips the array.Yes
Signal correlation engine⬜ detection.ts still keys on the singular m.protein.No
Diet-trial predicate — lib/dietTrial.ts⬜ does not exist yet — PR 5 builds it.It must, and that is this track’s call to make
Round 3 rules it in, because PR 5 is writing the predicate from scratch anyway.
  • The trial’s sanctioned set is the union of every protein of every primary_diet food — the full proteins array, not proteins[0]. With two kangaroo products that is both foods’ full sets unioned. §5.5 already said “sanctionedProteins derives from primary_diet rows”; this pins that it means the arrays, not the primaries.
  • Rung 2 flags on any protein in the fed food’s array that isn’t in the sanctioned set — so the duck food that also lists chicken flags on the chicken. That is the whole point of the rung, and reading only proteins[0] would silently miss precisely the contaminant class B-351 exists to catch.
  • An empty array is silence, never an all-clear (B-351 D10, already ratified). A protein-unknown food falls to rung 3 — “not recognised” — with hedged copy, never “clean”.
What this costs, and what it doesn’tIt does not block PR 1, 2, 3 or 4. Rungs 1, 3 and 4 (the explicit allowed set, unrecognised, and the oral route) are a complete closed-world detector on their own — that was ruling C1. Rung 2 is the arm that adds protein verdicts, and it can only ever add one. So the track builds in order and rung 2 sharpens when it lands.
The one real dependency, namedThe manual picker (B-351 slice 3) is still single-protein. Until it ships, a hand-entered food carries one protein and rung 2 can only see that one — so a hand-entered contaminant is invisible to the protein arm (it still falls to rung 3 and is recorded). Worth pulling slice 3 forward if the diet trial is the next build, since a diet-trial owner is exactly the person hand-entering an unusual food.
Shipping all of B-351 removes the dependencySlice 3 (the manual multi-protein picker) was the one named gap — a hand-entered food carrying a single protein. With all of B-351 shipped, every food carries its full array however it was captured, and PR 5’s rung 2 has no external dependency left. What B-351 still cannot do is see an undeclared protein — one that is in the food but not on the label. 33–83% mislabeling is documented specifically in the novel/limited-ingredient products these trials use, so that residual is permanent, and it is exactly what §5.5’s trial-level standing fact exists to disclose rather than detect.
And the food_item_id question resolves itselfThe PM asked whether the legacy single diet_trials.food_item_id should consider secondary proteins. It shouldn’t consider proteins at all — no computation reads that column. It is a display-only legacy pointer for the seven existing readers; every protein decision reads diet_trial_foods → each food’s full proteins array. Write the first-picked primary food into it and never compute from it.

“I FAB-log a rabbit food that isn’t sanctioned and it has hidden chicken” — traced

It does not fly under the radar, and it doesn’t need rung 2 to catch it. The chain is closed-world: the allowed set is the only permit path, so anything not on the list is off-diet by construction. What secondary proteins change is not whether it flags — it is what the vet is told.

StepWithout B-351 (rung 2 dark)With all of B-351
1 · Allowed setNot in diet_trial_foods → not permitted. The chain continues. This is the step that catches it, and it needs no protein data at all.
2 · Protein armDark — no verdict added.Reads the food’s array: ['rabbit','chicken']. Chicken is not in the sanctioned set → off_diet_protein.
3 · UnrecognisedFires → off_diet_unrecognised.Not reached.
What the owner sees“It isn’t on Biscuit’s allowed list, so Culprit recorded it as outside the trial diet.” Honest, hedged, correct.“This food lists rabbit and chicken. Biscuit’s trial is meant to be rabbit only.” Names the actual problem.
What the vet getsThe feeding is in the off-diet list with a date. The chicken exposure is not counted in the antigen tally.The feeding is in the list and a poultry exposure lands in the antigen tally — the line a dermatologist actually reads.
So the cost of a dark rung 2 is attribution, not detectionNothing is silently permitted. The exposure is recorded and dated either way. What is lost is the antigen — and on a hydrolyzed or novel-protein trial, “4 poultry exposures” is the finding, not “4 off-diet feedings.” That is why rung 2 is worth having, and also why it was safe to sequence it after PRs 1–4.
The real hole your question exposes is one step over — and it needs a rulingRung 1 says “in the allowed set → permitted, stop” (§5.3), and §5.5 computes the contamination standing fact from role='primary_diet' rows only. Put those together: a permitted extra with a hidden protein is permitted, counted, and never protein-checked. Your vet says the rabbit jerky is fine, you add it, it lists rabbit and chicken fat — the chain stops at step 1 and neither you nor your vet ever hears about the chicken. This is the same failure you described, moved from the unsanctioned food to the sanctioned one, where it is more likely to persist because nobody is looking. Decisions D-A and D-B below.

The two rules — they govern every screen below SPINE

Everything here is drawn against two rules that cannot be traded off against layout.
  • R1 — the negative claim is deleted from the product. “No off-diet foods logged” renders at no coverage level, on no surface. Every claim is in positive form about the record, carries both denominators, and the blind-spot qualifier is inline on the claim — never a page legend. The exposure count is a floor, never a total. Two-sided: below the floor Culprit also raises no absence-based alarm. (G2, ruled as a rule rather than a threshold.)
  • R2 — no blended coverage/adherence metric, in any form. Not a string, not a ring, not a badge, not a grade, not a colour, and not a bar width. Any progress bar on any screen below encodes getDietTrialProgress().fraction — day progress — and nothing else. (D2; and §6.9: Culprit never scores the owner, so coverage is never a percentage, bar, streak or badge anywhere.)

Rec: extend it.   2. D-B (new) — should a permitted feeding still count toward the antigen tally when it carries an unsanctioned protein? Mechanically: does rung 1 keep its stop, or does it return permitted and record the antigen? Rec: record the antigen, keep the verdict. The feeding stays permitted — it was vet-approved and must never be flagged as the owner’s slip (§6.9, never score the owner) — but the vet report’s antigen tally counts the chicken, because “6 poultry exposures, all from an approved treat” is a finding a dermatologist needs and cannot get anywhere else. This keeps the two facts separate: compliance is about the owner and stays clean; antigen exposure is about the animal and stays complete. Needs the adversarial-reviewer pass at PR 5 either way.
  3. The duration defaults — cat cells and dog cells. Ruled: both species participate, so the table needs all four cells. Proposed, for Dr. Chen to ratify or overwrite: Dog · skin 56d (Olivry/Mueller/Prélaud — 8 weeks is the >90% diagnostic-sensitivity band, not the low end) · Dog · gut 28d (ACVIM 2026: response typically 10–14 days, complete trial ≥2 weeks) · Cat · skin 56d (same 8-week band; the 40-cat arm of the same appraisal) · Cat · gut 42d — the one genuinely new number, raised from the dog’s 28 because cats reach only ~50% remission at 4 weeks against dogs >85% at 5. Every milestone says “the window you set”, never “complete”, so a short default is a prompt rather than a verdict.
  4. A-2 — is there a paused state? PM: “a trial is either active or not; paused is ending early and then restarting.” Drawn that way — no paused state, and PR 1 does not add the enum value. For Dr. Chen: the case it was raised for is a vet-directed hold — “stop the trial, she’s on metronidazole” — which under this ruling records as stopped early → the vet said to stop, then a new trial when the course ends. That is arguably more honest, because the paused days genuinely weren’t elimination days. The cost is that one clinical episode becomes two trial rows, and neither one is 56 continuous days. If he accepts that, A-2 closes for good and the enum never changes.

The coverage floor, in plain terms “NOT SURE I FULLY UNDERSTAND THIS”

Fair — round 2 explained it in jargon. Plainly: how much of the trial do we actually have records for, and how little is too little for the vet report to say the log means anything?

  • An owner who logs most days produces a record a vet can read. An owner who logged nine feedings across six days out of 23 produces one that mostly reflects when they remembered.
  • The floor is the line between those two. Below it, the vet report says “this log does not support interpreting this trial” — and that is all it changes. The counts still render, the card still shows what was logged, and no alarm fires in either direction.
  • Why there is no number yet: three defensible ways to define coverage read 100% / 84% / 19% over the same 70 days of real data, depending on whether treat-only days count and whether the denominator starts at the trial or at the report window. Picking the threshold before pinning the definition would be picking a number that means three different things.
  • So the order is: pin the metric in PR 5, then set the floor. It is not a blocker for PR 1–4 — the only screen that changes on it is the below-the-floor card, and that card is drawn.

Held constant across every screen

  • No negative claim, at any coverage, on any surface — positive form about the record, both denominators, blind-spot qualifier inline on the claim, exposure count is a floor never a total. Two-sided: below the floor, no absence-based alarms either.
  • No blended metric in any form — string, bar width, ring, meter, badge, grade or colour. Every bar is day progress and nothing else.
  • Culprit never scores the trial, and never scores the owner. Coverage is a statement about the record, never about the person — no percentage, streak, badge or grade anywhere. The owner reports the outcome; the vet decides what it means.
  • Record and continue. Every exposure surface carries a continuation statement. No copy implies the trial is voided, compromised or must restart — and no quantified reassurance, ever.
  • An oral-route exposure is never a reason to skip a dose. It names the fact and points at the vet for a substitution. A missed critical dose is the worse outcome.
  • Every flag is tappable to its reason, naming which rung fired. Disclosure includes explainability.
  • Non-blocking at the moment of the event (Principle 1) — nothing here warns, verdicts or colour-codes at log time. A pre-decision surface may verdict; a record surface may not.
  • Refusal routes to the health lane, not preference — and while an intake-decline flag is live, the card is structurally incapable of rendering an adherence line.
  • An abandoned trial is a clinical fact, not a failure state.
