How much of this is actually new UI ROUND 2 — THE COMPLEXITY QUESTION

Six screens. The eleven “states” are one card with one line that changes.

The round-1 deck looked big because it drew every data case as its own picture. That is a documentation choice, not a build cost. What actually gets built is six screens — and two of the six are plain lists.

The honest summaryOne new form, one changed card, one sheet, one milestone, two lists. Everything else in this deck is copy and conditionals. The reason the states are drawn separately is that the two that were never drawn — below the floor, and the day after a slip — are the ones that decide whether an owner finishes six weeks, and a spec that only draws the good week ships the wrong card for the bad one.
Where the real complexity is, and it isn’t the UIIt is lib/dietTrial.ts — the one shared predicate behind every number on every surface (PR 5). That is genuinely intricate and it is load-bearing: four ordered rungs, dated membership, food identity by brand+product rather than UUID, treats in one denominator but not the other. It is also invisible — no amount of UI simplification reduces it, and no UI here can be made honest without it.

If you want the lever — a smaller v1

Everything below can be cut without breaking a safety rule or stranding schema. Listed in the order I would cut them.

  • Cut the exposure list + the reason sheet (PR 5’s UI half). Keep detection, keep the counts on the card and the itemised list on the vet report. Cost: §5.3’s “every flag is tappable to its reason” becomes a fast-follow, and until it lands a flag the owner can’t interrogate is a weaker promise. Saves: two screens.
  • Cut the milestone’s outcome sheet (PR 6’s second step); keep the action-first milestone. Cost: the vet report loses the owner-reported outcome. Saves: one sheet plus the outcome write path.
  • Cut A-3’s mid-trial state. Pure addition; nothing depends on it.
  • Cut the free-fed and multi-pet card variants — only if you also accept that the card renders no adherence line for those pets rather than a wrong one. The variants are cheaper than the suppression logic, so this one probably saves nothing.
What cannot be cutThe intake-decline replacement and the refusal-terminal-state rule. Both exist so the card cannot render a clean trial over an animal that isn’t eating — the failure §5.2 was rewritten around, and the one the Jordan review caught shipping in round 1. They are conditionals, not screens, so they are close to free anyway.

The two rules — they govern every screen below SPINE

Everything here is drawn against two rules that cannot be traded off against layout.
  • R1 — the negative claim is deleted from the product. “No off-diet foods logged” renders at no coverage level, on no surface. Every claim is in positive form about the record, carries both denominators, and the blind-spot qualifier is inline on the claim — never a page legend. The exposure count is a floor, never a total. Two-sided: below the floor Culprit also raises no absence-based alarm. (G2, ruled as a rule rather than a threshold.)
  • R2 — no blended coverage/adherence metric, in any form. Not a string, not a ring, not a badge, not a grade, not a colour, and not a bar width. Any progress bar on any screen below encodes getDietTrialProgress().fraction — day progress — and nothing else. (D2; and §6.9: Culprit never scores the owner, so coverage is never a percentage, bar, streak or badge anywhere.)

Held constant across every screen

  • No negative claim, at any coverage, on any surface — positive form about the record, both denominators, blind-spot qualifier inline on the claim, exposure count is a floor never a total. Two-sided: below the floor, no absence-based alarms either.
  • No blended metric in any form — string, bar width, ring, meter, badge, grade or colour. Every bar is day progress and nothing else.
  • Culprit never scores the trial, and never scores the owner. Coverage is a statement about the record, never about the person — no percentage, streak, badge or grade anywhere. The owner reports the outcome; the vet decides what it means.
  • Record and continue. Every exposure surface carries a continuation statement. No copy implies the trial is voided, compromised or must restart — and no quantified reassurance, ever.
  • An oral-route exposure is never a reason to skip a dose. It names the fact and points at the vet for a substitution. A missed critical dose is the worse outcome.
  • Every flag is tappable to its reason, naming which rung fired. Disclosure includes explainability.
  • Non-blocking at the moment of the event (Principle 1) — nothing here warns, verdicts or colour-codes at log time. A pre-decision surface may verdict; a record surface may not.
  • Refusal routes to the health lane, not preference — and while an intake-decline flag is live, the card is structurally incapable of rendering an adherence line.
  • An abandoned trial is a clinical fact, not a failure state.
