Colour roles: each colour named for its job and its ground

Today a screen picks the teal and decides on its own whether it can be read where it sits. This round names colours by what they do (act, state a good fact, flag a concern) and where they sit (light or night), so the readable choice is the default. It starts from the ruling of 2026-10-03: indigo is the action colour, teal means a good fact.

Proposed wording · § Color

One dominant neutral (dark or light depending on theme). One accent — used sparingly, only for interactive elements and the primary trend line in charts. Never decorative. Never used to fill space.

One dominant neutral. One action colour, indigo, used only for what the owner can tap or change. Never decorative. Never used to fill space.

Each hue has one meaning, and a screen picks a colour by its job and its ground, never by its hue: the roles in constants/theme.ts (action, positive, selected, concern, attention, medication, destructive, text), each on light and on night, every one pinned to its ground by a contrast test.

§03 · The three choices

Ruled 2026-10-23

F1 · The action indigo on light grounds

Indigo needs one value a link can wear. The button disc's own indigo reads as black text at link size; the round 2 Home value (2026-10-03) reads as a link. Recommended: #3F35A0. It is the one new value in the layer.

F2 · What colour is "selected"

Today a chosen chip is teal. Under the ruling, choosing is acting, and teal would tell the owner that "Found it" is a good fact. Recommended: indigo, with a lavender wash that already exists as a token (the night text colour).

F3 · The action colour on night grounds

Indigo cannot show on the indigo night (1.94:1). Something else has to say "you can tap this" there. Recommended: moonlight, the night's own white; lavender is the softer option, and sits close to the muted text colour.
