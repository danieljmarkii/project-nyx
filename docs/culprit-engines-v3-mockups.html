"I've called", one tap away, and the note after it TD-4 ruled D

Home rows stay doors. The ask stays on the Home row in words, and the answer sits on the finding's screen, beside the evidence it's about. The same answers appear on the incident screen for a photo read.

After "I've called", nothing asks for more. The screen confirms what was recorded and offers an optional note. The note is written after the save, the way the daily look's note is. It's kept on the call's own record, which lives in the pet's Vet visits beside the visits (§03). Two days later there's one follow-up, "What did the vet say?", and its answer can be added to the same note.

03

The Signal and vet visits, as one loop

Today the two features barely touch. Get ready quotes the Signal's findings, and that's the only link. The Signal never hears that a visit happened, what was discussed, or when the vet wants to see her again. So it keeps asking.

The proposal connects them at every step, in both directions. Going down, the Signal opens doors into vet visits: book a visit, record a call. Going up, only owner answers and dates cross back: "Talked about it", "I've called", a recheck date. A visit never becomes a number in the engine. That line is AC 10 in the vet visits spec, and letting owner answers and dates cross it is the one amendment this needs (§06).

E-2 · What makes Home stop asking ruled A restated

Deciding: what acknowledges a concern, given that no visit records what it covered.

  • A restated (recommended): the owner's answer, per sign: a tick in the room, "How did it go?", or one question for a past visit. Why: only the owner knows what was discussed, and it's the loop you asked for.
  • A with a snapshot: also count the Get ready rows a visit displayed (dissent in §03).
  • C: the "My vet knows" tap only.

Consequence: unblocks EN-9's spec on a buildable source.

E-3 restated · Recheck-keyed now, calendar later ruled as restated

Deciding: how your "nail it or make it secondary" lands.

  • As restated (recommended): the vet's recheck date defers the concern to the appointment strip, with EN-9. The bare eight-week question becomes a secondary issue gated on EN-1's measurement. Why: the first repeats a date the owner entered, and the second has a measured noise problem.
  • Both now: ship the fallback with EN-9, unmeasured.
  • Neither: no recheck link either.

Consequence: drops the drafted eight-week re-raise of the old ask, and files the fallback as its own Low issue.

N-1 · Where a call's note lives ruled A

Deciding: the home for an owner's note about a call to the vet.

  • A, in Vet visits (recommended): a call is its own small record, listed beside the visits and in History, with the note on it. Why: it's where an owner looks for "what did the vet say", it keeps vet_visits meaning a visit, and it's the connectivity you asked for.
  • B, on the finding's screen only: no new record, and the note is lost once the concern stands down.
  • C, no notes on calls: words only in a full visit's "How did it go?".

Consequence: A adds one small table, with RLS, the wipe list, export and deletion (R-5), in EN-14's schema PR. The note is never read by a model and never counted.

AC 10 · Better than the rule decide · §03

The rule: AC 10 in the vet visits spec (2026-09-10) says a visit is provenance, never a source of numbers, and a guard pins which files may read the visit tables. It protects every count from a visit silently becoming data.

The better thing: let the engine's shell, never detection.ts, read owner answers ("Talked about it", "I've called") and appointment dates, to set the care state. No visit enters a count, a floor or a window.

Does the protection hold? Yes. Nothing numeric crosses, and the guard gains the shell as one named reader.

Recommend: amend it, as a Tier-2 edit to the vet visits spec in EN-9's PR. Without it the loop can't close.
