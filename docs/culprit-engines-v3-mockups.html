PMD-4 · does a vet-directed trial acknowledge? ruled A

Deciding: whether Jordan gets out of 1a through his trial.

  • A, through the owner's answer (recommended): while a trial runs, the finding's screen asks once, per sign, whether the vet started it for that sign; "yes" acknowledges until the trial ends (1b, 1c, 1h). Why: it's E-2's rule applied to the wedge, and nothing is matched on an indication.
  • A, from the record: a trial with vet_visit_id, a vet name or the vet-directed box acknowledges its indication's signs with no question. Faster, but the box means "the owner ticked it", and GI covers two signs the owner never named.
  • B: trials never acknowledge; "My vet knows" only.

Consequence: A adds a trial scope and an end to the acknowledgement row (PR-21), and one question to the finding's screen (PR-35). Courses follow the same rule, via "Did the vet start this for it?" on the course.

EN-9's re-raise tolerance ruled A

Deciding: how often a stable pet's owner may be asked again for nothing, now that the adversarial pass showed the drafted pair can't both hold. At 2 a week, no count test catches a doubling reliably within four weeks, so fast worsening is left to the burden card and the dense-day arm.

  • A · cap false returns at 5% of stable pets in eight weeks (recommended), and take the sensitivity that buys, reported per base rate. Why: noise is what sank the drafted trigger (59 to 81% re-asked), and the fast cases have their own nets.
  • B · cap at 10%: roughly twice the sensitivity at a low base, twice the noise.
  • C · set a sensitivity floor first (e.g. half of doublings caught within eight weeks) and accept the noise it costs.

Consequence: PR-16 measures the whole frontier on 1,000+ synthetic pets per scenario; you pick the point; PR-23 waits until the chosen setting meets it.
