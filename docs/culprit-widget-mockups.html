The widget's jobs — re-called by the PM ROUND 4 SPINE

The widget shows the day's record and opens the door. It doesn't take dictation.
  • Job 1 — answer "what's been logged today?" at a glance. Meals, treats, meds, symptoms — the kitchen-counter accountability question ("did anyone feed him? did anyone log it?") answered in under two seconds, without opening the app.
  • Job 2 — be the fastest door back into Culprit. Every element deep-links somewhere specific: the timeline to today in History, the headline to Home, the trial strip to the trial card, the ghost chip to quick-log. Logging happens in the app — the widget points at it and never impersonates it.
  • What this retires: round 3's capture tiles, the flip-to-picker interaction, and one-tap widget writes. "When in doubt, app it out" graduates from an escape hatch to the whole interaction model.

What the state-of-the-art research says 2026 SWEEP

Full brief covers Apple HIG/WWDC canon, iOS 18–26 platform shifts, and the widget-design canon (Flighty, Gentler Streak, Oura, Waterllama, Things, CARROT). The findings that shape this round:

Informational is a first-class jobApple's own trio — glanceable, relevant, personalized — and its three widget archetypes name "informational" explicitly: surface key data so the user stops opening the app for a repetitive check. The pivot isn't a retreat from interactivity; it's the platform's main lane. (WWDC20 "Design great widgets")
The best widgets are read-mostlyFlighty (ADA 2023), Gentler Streak, Oura, Copilot ship zero-to-minimal widget interactivity. The one beloved interactive widget — Waterllama — works because one tap = one unambiguous unit (a glass of water). A meal is not that: "which food, which slot, whose bowl?" is exactly the ambiguity the PM hit. The research validates killing capture.
A great informational widget is a state machineFlighty's widget has ~15 context-aware states — it decides what the most important line is right now and leads with it. Let state, not layout, do the work: a symptom day, a routine day, an empty morning, and a trial day are different widgets wearing one design.
The glanceable idiomsDay strip (slots fill as logged), 7-day mini-trend vs. baseline (Gentler Streak), dot-grid consistency calendars (Streaks), recency stamps ("last logged 2h ago" — ages gracefully), and sparse status-vocabulary color: neutral by default, color only when it means something.
Platform mechanics that shape layoutMedium widgets support per-element deep links — each row its own doorway (this is what makes Job 2 real). Small = exactly one link. Glance time is ~1–2s; never render a loading state; a small widget carries ~4 facts max.
iOS 18 tinted mode + iOS 26 glassUsers can force widgets monochrome (tinted) and iOS 26 puts them on system glass grounds. Hue-only encoding dies there — every event type needs a shape, and the system increasingly owns the widget's ground. Content over chrome, redundant encoding, no hand-painted backgrounds.
Widgets fail on staleness liesThe #1 real-world complaint: a widget showing yesterday's data as today's. Phrase content so it can't rot; timestamp facts, not claims. Our midnight-rollover honesty rule (never carry a ✓ across days) already ships — it generalizes to the whole surface.
Calm technologyAn ambient display should be ignorable on a routine day and instantly legible on an abnormal one (Weiser & Brown). That is Principle 3 in widget form: quiet neutral ground normally; the notable fact leads — and is never suppressed — when it exists.

Candidate E — "Today, on one line" RECOMMENDED

A day-axis timeline of the record, one honest headline, and doorways everywhere

The read, top to bottom: header (pet + trial context) · a 6a→10p timeline where every logged event sits at its actual time as a typed mark — meal , treat (smaller — same family), med , symptom — with hollow rings at learned meal windows not yet logged · a headline the state machine writes (counts + recency on a routine day; the symptom fact leads when one exists) · a footer that shows the record's last 7 days (or the trial's day strip when a trial is running). Filled = logged. Hollow = not logged. Shape carries type; color reinforces it.

The state machine (Flighty's lesson)One layout, and the headline decides what today's most important line is, in fixed priority: symptom logged today → symptom fact leads (never suppressed, Principle 3) · otherwise counts + recency · otherwise "Nothing logged yet today." The footer swaps by context: active trial → trial-day strip; no trial → 7-day record pips. State does the work; the layout never changes shape.
Every element is a doorway (Job 2)Medium widgets support per-element links: timeline → History filtered to today · headline → Home (the intelligence surface) · trial strip → the trial card · 7-day pips → calendar · "Log ›" → quick-log. There is no dead glass. The dashed ghost chip is round 3's app door, kept — it was the one part of the old design whose job survives.
Shape before color (iOS 18/26)Meal = large filled circle, treat = small filled circle, med = rounded square, symptom = rotated square (diamond), bowl = dotted ring, expected-window = hollow ring. In tinted/monochrome rendering every distinction survives on shape alone; in full color, the app's own event palette (colorEventMeal/Medication/Symptom) reinforces. Validated: CVD separation passes; teal's low contrast on white is relieved by the labels beside every mark.
Honesty rules, carried forwardFilled marks come only from real logged rows. Hollow rings at learned windows are round 3's "visible gap" rule re-drawn — never auto-completed by time passing (B-156 G1). At local midnight the timeline empties by construction (the dayKey staleness rule ships already). "Nothing logged yet today" is a fact about the record; no state ever reads as "all clear."

Held constant — the rules no direction change touches

  • Fail-safe honesty: a filled mark exists only where a logged row exists; an expected window is a visible hollow ring, never an assumed ✓ (B-156 G1 generalized). Midnight empties the day.
  • Absence is never wellness: "Nothing logged yet today" / "none logged" — no state reads as an all-clear, no praise, no streak language, no guilt language.
  • No AI, no Signal copy, no reassurance on a refresh-lagged surface (D9 stands).
  • Med register: counts use the confirmation register ("1 of 2 today") — never "missed," never "due," no compliance bars (the med-strip rules apply verbatim).
  • Pet-centric only: counts never decompose per person (T&S surveillance guardrail).
  • Per-widget pet binding at placement; never follows the in-app active-pet switch (B-086).
  • One accent: teal on live/interactive elements; rose is a semantic fact color, not decoration; no monetization state.
  • Copy gets the nyx-voice pass at build time — the strings here are working drafts.

PM decisions this round needs

R4-1 — Ratify the direction: the widget stops writing. Every tap becomes a doorway; capture retires from the widget entirely (the "Log ›" chip deep-links to quick-log). This reverses D1–D3 of the v1 spec. Recommended: yes — it is the PM's own re-direction, the research's strong consensus, and the largest reliability win available. (W4's intents stay for the Phase-2 Siri/Action-Button recipe, which remains capture — on surfaces where voice/hardware makes the event unambiguous.)
R4-2 — Symptom naming on the Home Screen. "Vomiting ×2" at a glance is Job 1 working — and new visibility for anyone who can see the unlocked phone. Options: (a) name it (as mocked), (b) neutral wording ("2 symptoms logged"), (c) an Edit-Widget "discreet wording" toggle per widget. Recommended: (a) with (c) as the escape valve if dogfooding says otherwise.
R4-3 — Expected-window rings: keep or cut. The hollow rings at learned meal windows are the one survivor of round 3's status column — they answer "has dinner been logged?" before the fact. Cutting them makes the widget purely retrospective. Recommended: keep (Jordan's 7pm question is the ambient job), with the existing no-stable-slot fallback (no ring rather than a guessed one).
R4-4 — Small sibling timing. Build G with the medium (one snapshot feeds both) or after the medium proves out on-device. Recommended: after — B-481 says prove one surface renders reliably first.
