Onboarding & the first week

B-251 built a clean funnel to an empty room. The constitution still says "onboarding is complete when Jordan logs their first event" — the built flow ends two screens earlier, the app never asks why the owner came, and the wedge feature is four taps deep on the fourth tab. Three nested options below, from a zero-schema copy fix to the full wedge concierge. Frames are deliberately low-fi: judge the shape and the words, not the polish.

02

The spine — rules every option obeys

Proposed as this track's ratifiable core (R-2). S1 resolves B-251's still-open D13 by extending the flow to the constitution's finish line rather than softening the constitution.

  • S1The finish line is the first log. Onboarding is designed toward the first logged event; the flow's last beat hands the owner into it.
  • S2Ask the job before decorating the room. One optional, one-tap mission question shapes the first week. Never typed, always skippable, changeable later.
  • S3Guaranteed value leads. The arc builds toward the clinic-ready record and report (guaranteed); the Signal is conditional upside, never a promised verdict. (Dr. Chen: "Promise the record, not the revelation.")
  • S4Honest floors, honest arc. Stage copy derives from the engine's real constants — capability framing ("timing patterns need about two weeks of meals"), never invented timelines. Kills "in a few days."
  • S5Teach one behavior. The one instruction the first week repeats: log everything she eats, including what you wish you hadn't fed. Completeness over precision.
  • S6No new furniture. The arc lives in Home's existing grammar — the building state, Today, the strips, the done screen. No checklist card, no tour, no coach marks.
  • S7Examples are labeled fiction. A named example pet, modeling good logging, pattern-shaped not food-accusatory, visually distinct from the pet's own cards.
  • S8A mission never gates. Every capability reachable on every mission and on skip; the mission only re-orders and re-words.

Option 3 — "One photo, four outcomes"

The wedge concierge. For the diet-trial mission, the last beat of onboarding is snapping the food the vet handed you. That one photo: seeds the library (Principle 2 restored) · logs meal #1 (S1's finish line) · becomes the trial's allowed list · pre-fills the trial sheet. Eng constraint that shaped this: the trial's allowed set needs a food to exist, so the sequence is fixed — snap first, then the pre-filled trial sheet. One flow, no dead ends, everything editable.

R-3Does the mission earn a screen — and which options does it list?
Deciding
Whether 2a exists, where it sits, and whether Sam's "what she actually eats" is first-class (drawn) or folds into "good records."
Options
Recommended: yes, one screen, after pet setup, four options + Sam's as drawn in 2a — the wedge user's intent is the product's reason, and one tap is the whole cost. Designer dissent stands: B-251's ratified bar was "every screen is a drop-off"; the lighter alternative is folding one question into the done screen.
Consequence
Yes → the schema column + report-header line get spec'd; no → Option 2 collapses into Option 1's copy variants.
