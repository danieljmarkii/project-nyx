Everyone who cares for Mochi, on one record

A partner gets their own login. A sitter gets a link that needs no app and no account. Anyone else gets the rules on a card. Underneath, one record knows who saw what, counts the louder reading when two people's entries may overlap, and checks doses across people. All of it free.

§01

Three ways in, one record

Each way in matches how much the person is trusted and how much they want to install. The research found three things no pet app or analog does, and this design does all three: sharing that knows about the diet trial, catching one event logged by two people, and a path for the record when people separate.

The card

Needs
Nothing. A text or a printout on the fridge or the food bin.
Sees
What Mochi can eat, what not to give, how to give the pill, who to call.
Does
Nothing logs back. Ships first.

The sitter link

Needs
A phone's browser. No account, no install. Bound to the first browser that starts it.
Sees
The rules, today's care with times and no names, doses due, the owner's numbers.
Does
One-tap entries; a hand-off to call or text when something needs a vet; a visit text.

§02

Inviting a partner

Settings gains a Sharing card. Only the pet's owner invites. Nothing syncs until the owner confirms the person by name, so a forwarded link reaches nobody. Brief D1, D2; team call on confirmation.

§03

Leaving a shared login

The first real case: two adults on one login, two phones. Today's sign-out would also sign out the other phone and wipe anything unsent, and a new account goes straight into making a second Mochi. This flow fixes both. Team calls; spec H4 and H5.

§04

The sitter link

Made by the owner or a partner, one per person, for a stay or for a regular walker or daycare. The page lives on getculprit.app, needs no app, and is bound to the first browser that starts it. It shows today only and names nobody in the household. Brief D3, D4; the page's layout is a visual choice (F9).

F9 · The page's first screen: two layouts

§05

Whose entry is it

Names answer "did you log the treat?" without becoming a scoreboard: others by first name, the reader as nothing at all, never on the off-diet list, never on a sitter's page. Home and History draw the same row, so a name shows in both or neither. Brief D7; where the name sits is a visual choice (F3).

F3 · Where a name sits on the shared row

§06

Doses, across people

Prevention lives where the next dose is given: the medicine's card says who gave the last one and how fresh this phone's copy is. The check works across people by medicine, so two people's doses always pair. Whether critical medicines also get a question before saving is the round's one persona conflict, drawn here side by side. Brief D5, D6.

F7 · The widget's dose tile with two people logging

§07

The plan, and the owner's side of a stay

A partner records what the vet said. Every plan change is a dated, named act both people see, and it can be undone. Owner-only actions explain themselves; they never show a dead control. Brief D2; the wall is a visual choice (F13).

§08

Ending access, and the vet report

Ending access stops the future and never reaches the past. The removed phone purges on its next sync; what the person logged stays, and they become a role. At a clean break, the person leaving can keep a copy (a fast follow). Brief D9.

§09

The decision round

Ten briefs. Nine carry the team's recommendation; one is a persona conflict with no recommendation. Rule them in the session, by letter (for example "D1 a, D6 b").

D1 · Adopt the shape, and its order Recommended (a)

Deciding: whether household sharing is built as three ways in over one record, and in what order.

  1. Recommended: the card, then readiness and the record's rules, then partners, then the sitter link (the riskiest piece, dark for your household first).
  2. The card and partners only; the sitter link later.
  3. Partners only, as August drew it.

Consequence: (a) unblocks the run order (§11). (b) defers the round's main opening, trial-aware help for the sitter. Forecast: 25 rows; push, "Same one?", transfer and keep-a-copy follow outside it.

Frames: §01 to §08.

D2 · What a partner can do Recommended (a)

Deciding: whether a partner can change the care plan, not only log.

  1. Recommended (every lens, with guards): start, end or extend a trial and add or change a medicine; each change dated, named and undoable; only the pet's owner archives, grants an account or transfers.
  2. Plan changes owner only (August).
  3. Two equal owners.

Consequence: (a) needs two rule changes first: a food added to a trial counts from the day it was added (B-456), and trial changes become append-only so two phones can't silently undo each other.

Frames: §07, F13.

D3 · Sitter links: two kinds Recommended (a)

Deciding: how long a sitter link lives and what its page shows.

  1. Recommended (Trust & Safety and the sitter consultant, reconciled): a stay link ends the morning after its date (one tap extends); a regular link has no date and ends after 30 days unused, when its maker loses access, or if you don't keep it at a 90-day check. Both: bound to one browser, today only, no household names, never "away".
  2. Stay links only.
  3. Links that never end.

Consequence: (a) a regular link's feedings count as exposures but never make a day "covered": a daycare's lunches can't speak for a home's dinners.

Frames: §04.

D4 · Safety without push in v1 Recommended (a), with a condition

Deciding: whether v1 needs server push so an escalation reaches you when someone else logs it.

  1. Recommended: no cross-person push in v1. The person logging sees every escalation; the sitter page runs the same rules and hands off by person; the visit text. Safety push follows as its own track.
  2. Build safety push in v1 (about 4 more rows, a review cycle, the push provider question answered now).
  3. No sitter link until push exists.

Consequence: (a) holds only while the sitter page carries the escalation and no copy implies you were told.

Frames: §04, the escalation and the visit text.

D5 · The record's rules with several loggers, as a set Recommended (a)

Deciding: how counts, intake and doses stay honest with more than one logger.

  1. Recommended: nothing depends on who is signed in; possible duplicates take the louder reading until someone resolves them (both count, the lower intake, every flag); doses checked across people by medicine, with no one-to-two hour clamp; sitter-only days never count as days symptoms were watched; "fed" never records how much was eaten.
  2. Partner rules now, sitter rules in the sitter phase.

Consequence: (a) changes the engine and the report, so each such build PR gets an adversarial review.

Frames: §05, §06.

D6 · A check before saving a second dose of a critical medicine Persona conflict

Designer: people log after the pill is in. A question before saving lands too late to prevent anything and adds a decision at the moment of event. Prevention is the card at rest.Dr. Chen: for insulin and drugs like it, one stopped double dose is worth the friction. Critical medicines only.PM decision needed: (a) no check before saving (Designer), or (b) a check before saving for critical medicines only (Dr. Chen)?

Consequence: (b) needs "critical" defined first, which is the open owner-toggle question on CUL-272.

Frames: §06, D6 options A and B.

D7 · Who shows by name Recommended (a)

Deciding: where a person's name appears.

  1. Recommended: names in the app (others by first name on the row; your own rows unnamed; an entry's detail always says who), never on the off-diet list or a sitter's page; the vet report in roles, initials only in its observation appendix; a person who leaves becomes a role.
  2. Names on the report too.
  3. No names anywhere.

Consequence: (a) answers "did you log the treat?" without turning the trial's off-diet list into a list of who slipped.

Frames: §05 F3, §08 the report.

D8 · AI reads on a shared pet Recommended (a)

Deciding: whose yes a photo or note needs before it goes to the AI.

  1. Recommended (Trust & Safety): a photo or note needs a yes from the owner, the sender and its author; the Signal needs the owner and the caller; a sitter answers once, no default; AI use is charged to you, with a small allowance per sitter link.
  2. Owner and caller only (the Oct 13 recommendation).
  3. Owner only.

Consequence: (a) ships before any account gains access to another's pet; the consent copy names the rule.

Frames: §04, Start.

D9 · When people separate Recommended (a)

Deciding: what each person keeps when a couple splits or someone leaves.

  1. Recommended (Trust & Safety and Data, reconciled): v1 ends access at once, offers you a password change, and lets the person leaving save the report. Next: "Keep a copy" at a clean break (names become roles, no future appointments, disclosed at invite, never marked on the original) and transfer. A pet in two homes keeps shared access instead.
  2. You keep the record; the other person exports only their own entries.
  3. Nothing leaves.

Consequence: (a) gives a person leaving a bad home their pet's history without telling the other person.

Frames: §08.

D10 · Price Recommended (a)

Deciding: whether any part of sharing is paid.

  1. Recommended (every lens, the pricing consultant included): all of sharing free forever, written into the boundary table; a future Premium covers everyone on a pet, decided by the pet; the 4-pet gate counts pets you own, never pets you can see; up to 4 accounts on every tier.
  2. Partners free, sitter links Premium.
  3. Free up to N people.

Consequence: (a) forecloses a per-seat family plan; (b) puts a purchase in front of an owner packing to leave.

Frames: §02, the invite's "free for both of you".

§10

Team calls already made

Logged on CUL-1935. Each is reversible; say so and it changes.

  • Wording: "Sharing", "share" and the person's name. Never "household", "caregiver" or "member" in the app.
  • Invites activate on your confirmation, by name, before anything syncs.
  • A person who leaves becomes a role on what they logged.
  • Owner-only actions explain themselves with an inert line (F13 A).
  • "Same one?" is never answered on Home (F5 B).
  • Visual choices follow the recommended options: F3 A, F5 B, F7 A, F9 A, F13 A.
  • A copy prints that it is a copy; the original never shows one exists. A pet in two homes keeps shared access.
  • An ended sitter page names no pet; the rules travel in the message you sent.
  • A partner can delete a photo they added, pending the spec-stage privacy review; otherwise only you hard-delete.
