Round 1 · directions, not designs — react coarse, not pixel-fine
§01

The data these frames stand on

One cat, one live elimination trial, and a record that separates cleanly when you ask each vomit how long since she last ate. The trial era re-run (Nyx-only, 2026-08-14) is the story every frame below renders a piece of:

The design problem in one sentence: today's surface shows a pooled chronicity card ("18 episodes across 6 of 8 weeks — worth booking a vet visit") while the decomposed record shows one phenotype stopped, treats collapsed, and meals doubled. The owner's lived sense and the surface disagree because the engine pools what the record separates. These mocks are the un-pooling.

Standing constraints every frame obeys (from B-721 + the guardrails)

  • Safety faces stay plain (S1) — the chronicity card keeps its text-only face; everything richer here is insight-class.
  • Change lives in the sentence, count-anchored and time-ordered — no ↑/↓, no percentages, no verdict words (better/worse/working).
  • No numerator-only visuals (S2); both sides of every compare are printed; a receipt renders only when it carries what the sentence can't (S10).
  • Never reassure on absence — every "none logged" is record-anchored, carries its window, and sits beside what continued.
  • No attribution — three things changed at trial start; the cards state counts and context, the vet interprets. RTM honesty lives in expanded states.

Direction C — the photographed record, composed (C4)

31 of her 36 episodes carry a completed photo read with structured fields the engine never touches. Composition, not new capture: joining what the photo said to when it happened. Descriptors, never labels — the regurgitation-vs-vomiting call stays with the vet.

Signal · expanded — empty-stomach card

In ~6 of the 7 photographed episodes that came 4+ hours after eating, the photo still showed partly digested food.

Why that's worth a mention

Food still recognizable hours after a meal is a specific observation about how her stomach is emptying — the kind of detail that changes what a vet checks first.

Report · appendix line (for R-4 context)

Rapid post-meal episodes (≤30 min, n=14): photographed content predominantly undigested food; no retching noted in owner logs. Features consistent with — determination deferred to examining veterinarian.

C1 — Content × timing, two registers Owner-facing (top): composed counts inside the timing card's expand, each with its plain-language "why it matters." Vet-facing (bottom): the descriptor bundle the regurgitation question needs, with the label explicitly left to the clinician. ~ figures are representative; the build computes exact ones.

What this direction never does: "hair in the photo" never softens anything (frequent hairballs in a shorthair are themselves a vet-worthy pattern, not an explanation) — no card here ever renders hair as reassurance. And no owner-facing surface prints "regurgitation" as a conclusion; the descriptors travel, the diagnosis doesn't.

§08

React — the round-1 decisions

Coarse reactions are the right altitude for round 1: kill directions, don't polish frames. Each brief is answerable on its own.

R-1

Deciding: how the timing story lives on the Signal — A1 (two cards, one lane each), A2 (one combined compare card), or A3's shape (one card per phenotype, clock + control evidence in the expand).

Team lean: A3's shape — one timing story per phenotype respects the zone's curation cap, and the clock reads better as evidence than as a rival card. A1 spends two slots on one theme; A2's headline has to carry two phenomena at once.

Consequence: sets the empty-stomach lane's card contract and closes the ⑤/⑥ suppression question as a design fact (the clock becomes evidence, not a card).

R-2

Deciding: whether B2's sentence — "No early-morning, empty-stomach episodes logged in the trial's 20 days … the right-after-eating kind has continued" — survives the guardrails, or the phenotype split must always render count-positive (B1's form with per-phenotype rows).

The tension, honestly: it's the most clinically useful sentence the record supports, it's record-anchored with its window and its counterweight — and it is absence-shaped, which G2 exists to police. Dr. Chen's screen is the gate, not this round. Options: (a) allow with the exact anchoring shown; (b) require the "0 · was 7" row form only, sentence stays count-positive; (c) vet-report only.

Consequence: (a) unlocks the hero frame on Home; (b) keeps the fact but blunts the lead; (c) defers the wedge's best sentence to the artifact vets read.

R-3

Deciding: which surface owns the trial-response read — a Signal card (B1/B2), the trial card (B3), or both with one as the door.

Team lean: both, split by register — the trial card carries the standing one-line count (it's the trial's home, and the §4.2 rule is satisfied: nothing here opens a form), the Signal carries it only when something changed worth surfacing (the B2 moment). Duplication is the risk to police.

Consequence: shapes C3's payload (standing fact vs event-driven finding) and which PRs touch which components.

R-4

Deciding: C4's owner-facing register — do composed photo facts (retained food, hair counts, bile) render in the timing cards' expanded states (as mocked), on their own insight card, or stay vet-report-only.

Team lean: expanded-states + report, no standalone card — the compositions are evidence about a timing story, not their own headline (S10: a receipt must earn its place).

Consequence: keeps C4 client-light (renderer additions only) and pushes its richest form into the report, where the regurgitation descriptors matter most.

R-5

Deciding: the sub-floor register — D1 (real progress toward each floor, hollow-dot lanes), D2 (the gap sentence only, no progress visuals), both, or neither.

Team lean: D2 now, D1 to a copy/Designer round — the gap sentence is honest at 3 episodes and escalation-shaped by construction; D1's fill-the-dots transparency is genuinely novel but flirts with logging pressure and "a card is coming" promise, which is a register question, not a data one.

Consequence: D2 slots into the E1/E2 empty-state work (B-721 SR-2 adjacency); D1 waits for its own round rather than blocking this one.

R-6

Deciding: the flag — mint signals_v2 (own beta-shelf row, own GA call) or ride signal_design_v2 (one shelf entry, one GA).

Team lean: own flag — this track changes what the engine says (new lanes, new sentences, a server deploy), not just how cards render; a separate kill-switch and a separate GA call keep the design uplift's rollout unhostaged. Cost: two shelf rows about the Signal.

Consequence: its own seed migration PR (the SR-0 pattern) and its own on/off QA matrix; the requirements doc writes FR-FLAG clauses accordingly.
