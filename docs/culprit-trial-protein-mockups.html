01

The shape in one rule

The sheet’s acceptance criterion is fifteen seconds, answerable standing in a clinic car park holding a bag of food. A required “what protein is this trial about?” question fails that test — some owners were told a protein, some were told a food, and a hydrolyzed trial has no answer at all. So the protein is never asked. It is shown, already filled in, from the foods just picked.

The register rule, applied here

A blank protein question is an entry — Principle 1 forbids it here.
It adds a decision to the golden path, and it is unanswerable for the hydrolyzed patient — the most refractory case, prescribed after a protein trial already failed.
A pre-filled “Trial protein: Rabbit” line is a confirmation — Principle 2’s job.
The app derives it from the picked foods and shows its work. Zero added decisions when the derivation is right, which is the common case. Tap to correct it, or clear it, or ignore it.
The stored protein never permits anything.
The food list stays the only permit path (§5.5 D-A). The protein is attribution and disclosure — it can add an off-diet naming to the record, never remove one. An empty or wrong value cannot make a contaminant “allowed.”

03

Tapping the row

The row opens a small picker. Derived options lead (with their provenance), common proteins follow, and the two escape hatches are first-class options rather than fine print — because for a hydrolyzed patient, “no single protein” is the correct answer, not a shrug.

The intro line carries the invariant in owner language: “it never changes what counts as off-diet.” That sentence is the §5.5 loophole guard, spoken out loud — an owner cannot launder a chicken treat into the trial by editing this field, and the copy tells them so before they try.

04

The day-0 catch — when the label disagrees

The strongest argument for storing intent separately from the food: it makes a wrong trial food detectable at setup. Today the target protein is read from the trial food, so the food can never be found to contradict it. With the owner’s “rabbit” on record, a picked food whose label leads with chicken becomes a finding — on day 0, where the clinical sources say trials silently fail, not on day 14 when the exposure detector finally has enough meals.

Two things this frame is deliberately not: it is not a modal (the owner is not interrogated at the moment of setup — Principle 1), and it is not an error (Start stays enabled; the record keeps both facts — the declared protein and the food’s label — and the vet report can disclose the tension rather than resolve it).

05

When nothing derives

A hand-entered food with no protein data, or a hydrolyzed diet — the derivation comes back empty. Two candidate behaviours; E2 is the provisional ruling (TP-1), the one call still awaiting PM confirmation — the mid-trial home (frames F–H) is what tipped it. Both keep Start enabled and neither ever guesses.
