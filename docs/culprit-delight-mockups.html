Delight from the record

You asked whether Culprit could hand owners something lighter: “your pet is really playful this week”, “your pet really likes this food”, nuggets they would never notice looking at a week at a time. The product team took it to Nyx’s own record.

The answer: delight that depends on how the pet is doing either lies or goes quiet for exactly the owners who need warmth. Delight the calendar or the record’s size schedules is true however she is doing. Build that kind first, in the app, and put a “has died” state underneath it before any of it ships.

Two kinds of delight, and only one is honest for a sick pet

Nyx is on day 75 of 84 of a rabbit elimination trial with ten vomits in September. Anything gated on her being well would almost never reach her owner. Anything the calendar schedules reaches every owner, and says nothing about her health.

Depends on how she is doing

Playful · likes · a quiet week

  • Ungated, it asserts wellness the record cannot see.
  • Gated correctly, it is silent for the wedge owner, so its arrival starts to mean “she’s fine”.
  • Lives in the app at most, as the owner’s own dated words, beside the concern words.
Scheduled by the calendar or the record’s size

Her birthday · the record’s age · day 84 · a finish rate with its denominator · the household’s rhythm, drawn

  • True however she is doing, so it can be light without being a verdict.
  • Renders the same on a bad day as a good one, below any safety card.
  • Spoken in the owner’s own nouns, not a cuter voice.

“Your pet is really playful this week”

The app receives no behavioural cues. All it has is what you tap in Noticed: 8 looks since Sep 22. Five say nothing unusual. Played appears on Sep 25 and Sep 26.

The week that push would have celebrated holds this evening: a vomit at 7:06, Played at 8:00, a second vomit at 8:12, Hunched or tucked up at 8:18. A pain posture between two vomits, in a cat on a GI trial.

  • “Really” claims an intensity against a baseline the app never computes.
  • “Playful” slides from an activity into a mood; lively’s never-say column forbids exactly that (“happy, a good day, better, herself again”).
  • On a lock screen it asserts record contents (G1) and a state (NV-G8 rung 4).
  • Sent only to healthy pets, it becomes delight that switches on with health, so its arrival teaches “she’s fine”. CUL-914 measured lines of this kind printing on 22 to 77% of pure-noise records.

“Your pet really likes this food”

Never “likes”. Preference is a rate over many meals, and “likes” is a state of mind the record cannot see. Intake decline is often a disease signal, so the house rule is that only the positive, multi-sample signal may be said at all.

  • The perfect finish records are the chicken treats the trial removed: Greenies 70 of 70, rotisserie chicken 11 of 11. Treats sit at a ceiling the code already refuses to call loved.
  • The shelf already overclaims. “Finished 29 of 29 meals” sits on a food logged 264 times, from ratings that stopped Aug 12. Filed as CUL-1685.
  • “Finished” counts Most, so “finished all 29” would be false in your own words (28 All, 1 Most).
  • The wet trial food (13 rated, none All) invites “not a fan”. It gets no taste line, anywhere.

Honest version: “finished, out of the meals you rated”, with the unrated count and the newest rating date, on meal foods only, in the app.

Decisions for you

Eleven briefs. DB-1 and DB-2 come first: every occasion idea waits on them, and they decide whether Nyx sees any of this. The recommended option is marked.

DB-1 · FIRST

Does a “has died” state ship before any occasion, and where?

  1. A profile row reachable for a sole pet that stops every notification and look-back for her, the recap included. The only shape the most common account can reach.
  2. A reason inside the archive flow (a sole pet can’t reach it).
  3. An activity proxy only (fires through the first two weeks of grief).

Unblocks every occasion idea. Own schema PR and a T&S read (CUL-1684).

DB-2 · FIRST

Does delight go quiet when she is unwell?

  1. Structural silence under shared quiet gates.
  2. Calendar facts render the same whatever her health, below any safety card; record-derived facts are gated. A calendar line that hides in a crisis teaches that its presence means she is fine.
  3. Everything below safety, with a disclosure when something is withheld.

Record-derived nuggets will rarely fire for Nyx, which is correct. Dir. of Engineering’s dissent stands for record-derived delight.

DB-3

Which channel does delight use in v1?

  1. The 9pm recap and in-app surfaces only. Spends no unsolicited budget.
  2. Plus an opt-in birthday and record-age push.
  3. A full opt-in “Nyx’s days” category.
  4. Spend the push on the dinner rating question (Dr. Chen).

(b) to (d) need the per-day arbiter (CUL-1688), the Principle 4 D1 wording, and a CHECK migration.

DB-4

Does delight get a second voice?

  1. One deadpan register; lightness comes from the subject.
  2. A keepsake register for calendar moments.
  3. A lighter voice everywhere.
  4. Lean: (a) plus one allowlisted literal, “Happy birthday, Nyx.”, identical every year.

Dissent: Owners and Market want (b); Data Scientist and Dr. Chen hold (a) without the literal. Drawn side by side in shortlist #1.

DB-5

What does Nyx’s day 84 (Sat Oct 17) get?

  1. Clinical prep only: the weigh-in line and the report door.
  2. A shareable keepsake at the target tap.
  3. (a) now, plus a plain record of the 84 days after you record the vet’s call. A keepsake before the vet rules reads as “it worked”.

The weigh-in line is buildable in nine days; an image keepsake needs a native build.

DB-6

Reopen B-763 for the target day on the lock screen?

  1. Keep it parked. The recap already says “Tomorrow is day 84 of the trial.” on Oct 16; a body cannot carry the keep-going sentence.
  2. The envelope “A note about Nyx’s trial.”
  3. “Day 84 of Nyx’s trial. Worth reviewing with your vet where it goes from here.”

Dissent: Owners and Market want (c).

DB-7

Trade a small ask for an honest finish rate?

  1. No ask; the food line lives with sparse ratings and says so.
  2. At most weekly, a recap door to the least-rated food of the day, nothing preselected, stands down after three ignored weeks.
  3. An app-sampled meal a day (the better statistical instrument, later).
  4. A dinner push at a declared window.

Any prompted rating carries safety weight: one Most or All quiets the vomit read’s intake arm.

DB-8

What format do shared cards take in v1?

  1. Text or PDF through the share sheet, with a preview of exactly what leaves and an “as of” date. A share is an export.
  2. Image story cards (native build).
  3. No shares in v1.

Unblocks the household trial card. Whether symptoms print on a shared look-back stays a conflict below.

DB-9

Does the record get a non-clinical photo class?

  1. Not yet. Resolve the public pet-photo bucket first (CUL-594); a portrait stored there is readable by anyone with the link.
  2. Owner-initiated, private.
  3. A photo door after any look.
  4. A monthly side portrait (Dr. Chen).

Gates every photo memory. Until then the record has no face.

DB-10

When is the rechallenge list released?

  1. Behind Get ready, when an appointment falls in the last 14 days of the target, grouped by protein.
  2. After elimination ends (v1 never writes that phase).

Either way it never appears on Home or in a notification.

DB-11

May any part of a look-back be Premium?

  1. All free. A look-back that carries health counts is trend visibility (Principle 7), and Strava’s 2025 paywall on Year in Sport drew backlash.
  2. Customising a shared card’s look may be Premium.

Under (a), the Premium feature list does not grow from this portfolio.

Proposed standing rules

What a delight portfolio would need written down before its first PR. Proposals only; none is adopted until you rule the briefs.

DELIGHT-1 · the inference test. A nugget whose interest depends on what it implies did not happen is an absence claim, whatever its grammar (“the last chicken treat was Jul 24”).

DELIGHT-2 · health invariance. A calendar fact renders the same whatever the record holds, below safety, never hidden. A record-derived fact is withheld while any intake arm, live safety finding or symptom logged today holds.

DELIGHT-3 · the armed twin. A positive intake or behaviour claim is promoted beyond its own screen only while the matching negative read can see, and prints its newest sample’s date.

DELIGHT-4 · the subject rule. A delight line’s subject is the pet’s record or a date, never the owner’s act: no “you logged”, no ratio of logged days, nothing consecutive, no badge.

DELIGHT-5 · bereavement before occasions. No birthday, anniversary, memory or year recap ships until a “has died” state the last pet can reach exists and silences the recap too.

DELIGHT-6 · instrument honesty. No count crosses a change of instrument (the trial boundary, one-tap logging, a new event type). Entry counts always say “logged”.

DELIGHT-7 · symmetric capture. The app never prompts for a photo, rating or word only after a positive.

DELIGHT-8 · a share is an export. Show exactly what leaves; an “as of” date; no date that can move; never a vet, clinic or indication.

DELIGHT-9 · prompted ratings carry safety weight. No default, no lock-screen action, Skip at equal weight, asked only after the meal could have been eaten.

DELIGHT-10 · fixed templates. Every template declares the words it may never sit beside and the facts it must sit beside. No model writes, selects or rephrases a nugget.

DL-1 · the lock screen. A delight body may name a calendar fact or nothing.

DL-3 / DL-4 · one arbiter. One per-account, per-day gate for unsolicited sends; safety outranks delight; delight never wakes the phone.

DL-8 · photos. No delight surface reads a photo from a symptom event; any non-clinical photo class is private, not in the public pet-photo bucket.

DL-9 · no offer in delight. No Premium mention or upsell in a delight body, and no look-back carrying health counts behind a paywall.

DL-10 · phase-aware naming. While an elimination trial runs, no delight surface names a food the trial removed.

DL-12 · judge on the real shape. Recap-borne delight is judged on a single-pet account; archive the test pet first.

How this was made

So you know what to trust.

  • Six lenses brainstormed independently against the same brief and the same data profile: Designer with Motion & IA, Data Scientist, the owners (Jordan, Sam, and a healthy-pet owner), Dr. Chen with the clinical guardrails, market research with live web search, and the Director of Engineering. 80 raw ideas.
  • A facilitator clustered them, keeping wild cards and every dissent.
  • Two red teams tried to break every idea: a clinical adversarial reviewer (biostatistics and Dr. Chen) and a trust, safety and voice reviewer. Each named the concrete counterexample it tried.
  • A synthesis lead ranked the survivors and wrote the briefs.
  • A verification pass then checked every load-bearing claim against the code and the database. It confirmed the shelf string, the sole-pet archive block, the single-pet recap gate, the follow-up naming, the budget, Ask’s screen and the teaching examples, and corrected four facts: 527 treat entries before the trial (not about 420), Central time (not Eastern), six foods on the trial list (not three), and rabbit before the trial (2 Instinct entries, not 17; first rabbit May 23).
  • The data is your own account only, read-only, aggregates and timestamps, no notes or photos read. It moves as you log; counts are as of the evening of Oct 7.
