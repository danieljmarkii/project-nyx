Meals with an intake answer, by month

The one pet logged continuously since May. Answered meals over logged meals; October is eight days.

Three things changed around August and the data cannot separate them: the card grew (a trial panel, then Undo), the owner had seen it several hundred times, and the routine may have changed. All three point the same way. The intake question is now answered only when something is off.

Three jobs, one form

The card does three jobs at very different frequencies, and all three wear the same dark card with the same check.

Every log · used ~1%

Confirm, and offer a way back

The write landed, here is what it says, Undo if the tap was wrong. Needed every time, acted on almost never. It should be the smallest, most specific thing the app can draw.

Meals · time 10%, intake 2%

Capture at recall

The time is the workhorse, yet it is the smallest control. The intake scale takes the most room and is now the least used, partly because a bowl just put down has no answer yet.

Rare · the one that matters

Carry an exception

A vomit read raised to a call, a food off the trial list, a second dose. It arrives inside the form the owner has learned to skip.

  • Habituation is fast and it transfers. The visual response to a repeated warning drops after the second exposure, and a warning that changes its appearance resists it; avoidance learned for one look carries over to whatever later wears that look (docs/research/2026-09-home-insight-fold-and-freshness-patterns.md §4). The exception currently wears the routine's look.
  • Principle 9's test fails today. Cover the words: a check on a dark card could be any app's. Nothing in the motion says which record changed or where it sits in the day.

The routine is a receipt, the exception changes form, and intake waits for the bowl it asks about.

  • Routine (about 97 in 100 cards): direction B's frame. Today's line with the new row landing on it, the time as the control, Undo. On most bowl-downs no earlier bowl is waiting, so the card is two rows instead of four.
  • Exception (the rest): direction C's frame. Its own ground, its own feel, held until closed for a call.
  • Not now: D collides with the Home write rule and doubles the forms. E is a cheap add on top of A, later.
  • Untouched: what every card writes, Undo's reach and the one shared reversal, the intake-is-not-preference and n=1 rules, the halo rule over a refusal. Nothing on the routine card may read as an all-clear, and the absence of an exception never does.

Why the check looks grainy

The badge's gold halo is an iOS layer shadow. The badge has no solid fill (6% white), so iOS traces the shadow from whatever is opaque inside it: a 1.5pt ring and a 3pt check. The gold blur hugs two thin teal lines, mixes to a muddy olive on near black, and gets re-rasterised offscreen every frame while the mark springs in. That fringe is the grain. Enlarged:

Now · shadow traced off the strokes
Proposed · one SVG, halo drawn, no shadow
  • The fix: draw the whole mark in one react-native-svg node: a solid teal disc, a dark check knocked out of it, and the gold halo as a radial gradient behind. No layer shadow, so nothing for iOS to trace. Vector at every scale, including mid-spring.
  • Celebrate vs. refusal stays exactly as ruled (CUL-894): the halo is a prop on the mark; a refused or picked-at bowl gets the disc without it.
  • The named and medication cards share the same badge style, so the same mark replaces all three. One component, one render path.
