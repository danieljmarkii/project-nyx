The medication strip on Home

Round 1 asked which of three shapes to build. The PM ruled C — both, and the reasoning turned out to matter more than the choice: it settles a standing contradiction between two ratified texts. This round is one design, every state, and it is the authority the build copies from.

An active medication lives only on the Pet tab. The owner sent home with a fourteen-day course — the wedge user, the highest-intent person this app has — opens Home every day and sees nothing about the one thing the vet asked them to do. That is the gap TrialStrip closed for diet trials, and this closes for meds.

D1 · what each card carries
Both
Course context and a one-tap. The tap is a confirmation, not an entry — see below.
D2 · sequencing
Ad-hoc tolerant
Renders from doses alone. On the PM's own account: 2 regimens, 0 with a duration. A regimen-only strip would render for almost nobody.
D3 · multi-med
One card per med
No ranking, no aggregation. Nothing gets to favour one med's data over another's.
01

Why C is not a violation

Two PM-blessed texts point opposite ways, and round 1 could not proceed without someone picking. The diet-trial card says logging is the FAB — a second door to the same room is not a feature, which argues for context only. The brand briefing's Care due row was adopted with a one-tap Log dose sitting in it, which argues for the action.

The ruling is not that one text loses. It is that they were never in conflict, because they describe different registers.

The register rule

A control that opens a form is a second door.
It asks the owner to make decisions the app does not yet have answers to. That is what the trial card forbids, and it stays forbidden.
whereas
A control that writes a row the app could already describe is a confirmation.
The app holds the drug, the amount, the route and the cadence — from the regimen if there is one, from the last dose if there isn't. The tap confirms a dose the record already predicts. That is Principle 2 doing its job.
The test any future Home action applies — and it is a test, not a precedent. The question is never did the trial card allow this? It is: can the app describe the row before the owner taps?

Two things fall straight out of the rule, and both are hard gates rather than polish. If the app cannot pre-fill the dose, there is nothing to confirm, so no button renders — round 1's Option A survives as a state, not as a rival option. And because D3 puts one card per med on a finite screen, a med whose cadence is already covered today collapses to a single line. The weight gets paid down by register, never by dropping or re-ranking a med.

Parts

Four elements, each earning its place

Header — the drug by the owner's own word for it (brand-preferred, drugDisplayName), then where the course stands.

Track — day progress and nothing else: daysElapsed / target_duration_days. Never a fraction of doses given. Meds without a duration get no track at all, because there is no honest fraction to draw.

Line — coverage as a counted fact about the record. Never a verdict about the pet.

Confirm — accent-light ground with accent-ink text, the contrast-safe pair. Present only when the app can describe the row it would write.

The app has no structured dose times. doses_per_day is a count; schedule_notes is free text. Nothing knows when a dose was expected, so nothing may say a dose is due.

State 3

Covered today — collapsed

collapse ruleno confirm

The cadence count is met, so the card goes quiet and keeps its place. This is how D3's fold cost gets paid: in the steady state — an owner who logs — most cards are collapsed most of the time, and a card takes its full dress only on the days it has something to say.

It also removes the commonest double-tap path. A med with no cadence (PRN) never reaches covered and so never collapses, which is right — repeat dosing is the point.

Ongoing, and ad-hoc

An indefinite med has no end to count toward, so it states recency. An ad-hoc med has no regimen at all — no length, no cadence, nothing to be behind on — so it states what was logged and stops.

State 6 & 7

Course length reached, and past it

never “you can stop”card persists

Ending a course is the vet's call. target_duration_days is a number the owner typed, not a prescription the app verified, so the line is a calendar fact plus a prompt to check — never permission to discontinue.

Nothing auto-ends a regimen, so stale-active is the steady state here just as it is for diet trials. State 7 is normal, not exceptional, and the card must keep rendering: the Pet tab is the only place a course can be ended, so hiding the card would hide the only route to ending it.

State 8

A refusal on the record

no confirmno coverage linewithholding

A refused or missed dose is a health signal, not a scheduling gap. The tidy coverage line is suppressed and the fact takes its place — the fact, never a verdict.

The confirm stands down too, and that is the deliberate part: a refusal means the next thing that happened is precisely what a one-tap given cannot express, and a confirm sitting beside a refusal invites the owner to paper over it with the cheaper tap. The card becomes a way through to the Pet tab, where the real adherence controls live.

One list holds every withholding reason — refused, missed, in-doubt, intake-decline — for the same reason the trial card ended up with one: patched one reason at a time, the next one still renders.

9 — the app cannot attribute the dose (no library item, no resolvable regimen), so it cannot describe the row, so there is no button. Context only. A button that guessed would be entry wearing a confirmation's clothes, writing a clinical record the owner never asserted.

04

The collapse rule, on a real screen

Sam's cat: three meds and an active diet trial. Round 1 showed this frame to make D3's cost visible before the ruling. Here is the same screen twice — before the day's doses are logged, and after.

  • Expanded before collapsed, then oldest course first, then by name. A stable order — a Home surface that silently re-sorts itself cannot be read at a glance.
  • No cap. No “show three, hide the rest”: that drops a med, which is the exact thing D3 was ruled to prevent, and it drops it in an order the app has no basis to choose.
  • The med cards sit below the trial strip when both are present. The trial is the wedge's primary object and runs eight to twelve weeks; a fourteen-day course is the shorter-lived guest. Fixed order, not a ranking.
05

What this surface may never say

Four invariants. Three are inherited; the fourth is new here. They are enforced by a test over the resolver's output, not by review — the words missed and due must not be reachable.

RuleNeverInstead
N1 Dose due · You missed a dose “No dose logged yet today.” No structured dose times exist, and un-logged ≠ un-given — someone else in the house is the commonest reason a dose is missing from the record.
N2 A bar bound to doses given Day progress only. The compliance-bound bar is the defect the trial-card rebuild deleted — and the Pet tab's regimen rows still carry one, so that card must not be copied here.
N3 A tidy coverage line over a refusal The refusal fact, and the confirm withdrawn. Refusal is a health signal, not a scheduling gap.
N4 All caught up · Fully covered Counted facts about the record — “2 of 2 doses logged today.” The app cannot know the pet is covered, and an affirmative here would be reassurance the record does not support.
06

Locked, and still moving

  • Locked by this round — the anatomy, all ten states, the collapse rule and its ordering, placement below the trial strip, and every case in which the confirm does not render.
  • Still moving — the exact words. Every string above is indicative and locks at M5 behind nyx-voice, clinical-guardrails and a pm-feature-review pass. State 8's register in particular is a clinical-guardrails call, not a copy preference.
  • Prerequisite — the day-math fix lands first. A date-only start read as UTC midnight counts a day too many for anyone behind UTC, and that number is the denominator of the adherence copy on the Pet tab. Two surfaces counting the same course is this feature's entire premise.
  • Deliberately out of scope — escalation on a missed critical-drug dose. The strip withholds; it does not escalate. That belongs to the Signal safety band and to an open question that already exists.
