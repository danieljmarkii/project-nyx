Correction 1 · the size of the loss

The mis-tap eats the sheet, not a draft

The issue reads “throws away the log you were in the middle of.” The code is narrower, and the difference matters to how hard this should be fought: the switcher only exists on the grid step — once a type is picked, the title is gone and there is nothing to open. So there is never a half-filled confirm behind it: no photo, no note, no time. CUL-612’s discard guard covers that stage and is not reachable from here.

What is lost is the open sheet — the owner’s place in the flow — and, on the completed-add path, the active pet.

Correction 3 · option (B) is bigger than it looks

“Return to the sheet afterwards” is not a routing change

The issue prices (B) as “a return path through an unrelated route plus its own QA.” It is more than that, and the reason is the thing that caused CUL-662: a pushed screen renders behind an RN Modal. The add-pet form cannot appear over the log sheet — the sheet has to close for it to be seen at all. So (B) is not routing; it is sheet-state restoration across a native modal: the FAB would have to remember that the sheet was open, at which stage, for which pet, and re-present it on return.

That is real work on the exact seam that wedged the app for every multi-pet account last week, bought to make a rare path two taps shorter. It is drawn nowhere in this round because there is nothing to see — the frames would be identical to (C), and the whole cost is invisible.

Decisions

Two rulings

D1Do the management rows render inside a capture surface?

Deciding: whether “Add a pet” and “Archived pets” appear when the switcher is hosted by a logging surface.

Options: (A) hide both — recommended. One optional prop on the panel; the surface becomes single-purpose at the moment of capture, and every door survives elsewhere. · (A′) hide only “Add a pet” — keeps the sheet-loss, drops only the pet change; half a rule is harder to hold than a whole one. · (B) keep them and restore the sheet on return — see above; not a routing change. · (C) keep as built — defensible only if the mis-tap is rare enough to accept, now that the loss is known to be the sheet rather than a draft.

Consequence: (A) ships this week and unblocks the CUL-663 flow-2 sign-off. It also makes CUL-662’s onNavigateAway wiring unreachable on its only consumer — kept, with the reason written down, so the next layer host does not rediscover the defect.

D2Does the ruling cover the FAB menu too?

Deciding: whether D1 is a fix to one beta surface or a rule about capture surfaces.

Options: (i) both — recommended. The FAB chip is a capture surface by the same test, its active-pet change is identical, and shipping the rule to one of two switchers a tap apart is the inconsistency that gets refiled as a bug later. · (ii) log sheet only. Narrower diff, and the FAB case is genuinely cheaper — the menu survives the trip and partly announces the switch itself. The cost is that the app then has two switchers with different row sets and no visible reason.

Consequence: (i) touches one more file (FAB.tsx) and edits a shipped, unflagged surface, so it wants the device pass. (ii) leaves that surface as-is and the inconsistency filed as its own issue.

Ruled (i). Both surfaces. The FAB menu is unflagged, so this half reaches every multi-pet account on the next build rather than only the beta cohort — it is the one part of this change that wants the device pass.
