Can an AI help read what you log?

The AI consent screen Apple's rule 5.1.2(i) requires, drawn as one screen used at all three doors (onboarding, the first open after the update, Settings), plus the Settings switch both ways and what a vomit or stool record says when reading is off. Next stop: synthetic panel 1 (CUL-1833) tests whether owners can teach it back; then you approve the final frames on CUL-1834.

What you are approving

  • The copy on the screen and its details sheet (§1). Every word ships in the 1.2.0 binary and is versioned as copy_version.
  • Where it sits in onboarding: right after Before you start (current) or after the pet is named (the option box). Brief A.
  • The Settings switch (§2) and the reading off states (§3), which CUL-1835 builds.
§1

A headline, three short lines, Allow and Don't allow side by side in the same style with nothing preselected, the details one tap away, and Not now in the corner. Allow and Don't allow each write a row. Not now writes nothing.

§2

The Settings switch

A row in Preferences opens the full screen, so the reviewer's guaranteed path is two taps from Home's avatar. Once answered, the answer becomes a switch that writes on each flip, with no confirm in either direction: taking consent back is exactly as easy as giving it. While unanswered there is no switch, because a switch has to sit at on or off and either one would look like an answer.

§3

Today read_disabled renders nothing (VomitAnalysisSection.tsx:689, StoolAnalysisSection.tsx:631). With the gate live, that blank would be every declined owner's photographed record, and a blank where a read should be reads as "nothing found". Each frame is a status, never a verdict: it says the photo wasn't looked at, carries the when to call guidance a photoless log gets, and offers the way back in one quiet line. The stool line stays event neutral, because the state renders for a normal stool too (the stoolCapCopy rule).

§4

Decisions for you

Brief A · where the step sits

Deciding: whether onboarding asks right after Before you start (A) or after the pet is named (A′).

Options: (a) after the disclaimer: sits with the other deliberate choices, the pet isn't named yet. (b) Recommended: after the pet is named. "Mochi's photos" makes the trade concrete, and a choice read next to a legal acknowledgment gets tapped through like one.

Consequence: (b) moves the step one screen later in CUL-1834 and threads {name} through three lines; panel 1 tests the version you pick. Neither touches the demo account, which meets the screen on first open, not in onboarding.

Brief B · the no line on the face of the screen

Deciding: whether "Photos won't be read, so watch for blood yourself" is one of the three lines or lives only in the details.

Options: (a) Recommended: on the screen. It is the one true cost of a no, and consent without it isn't informed. (b) In the details only: softer, and lets a no feel free when it isn't quite.

Consequence: panel 1 scores (a) for coercion. If owners read it as a scare into yes, it moves to the details before CUL-1834 starts, whatever the count.

Brief C · the engines evaluation line (CUL-1313)

Deciding: whether this copy, frozen into 1.2.0, also asks to use records to measure whether warnings were right.

Options: (a) Recommended: no, functional only (D18). One screen asking for one thing is what a reviewer and an owner can both read. (b) Add one line: stretches the screen past three lines and mixes two purposes under one yes.

Consequence: (a) leaves CUL-1313 needing its own opt in later; (b) needs that line drafted, reviewed and run through panel 1 before CUL-1834.
