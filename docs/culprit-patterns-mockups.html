Culprit · Patterns dashboard + detail Mock round 1 · 2026-08-01 Low-fi exploration — nothing locked

Patterns should answer questions, not display metrics

The dashboard shipped as a correct, honest card stack — and the PM, our own wedge user, doesn't open it. This round explores three directions (refine → restructure → swing), a shared detail-screen redesign, and one feasibility-gated exploration (ingredients). Round 2 raises fidelity on whatever survives this review.

01

Why the surface goes unvisited

Three findings from the research pass, matched against measured abandonment drivers (~30% of tracker abandonment traces to “static data,” the largest single cause):

1 · The dashboard looks the same every visit. Cards restate standing facts (“3 this month”) rather than leading with what changed. State of the art (Oura Today, Apple Health Highlights) leads with detected change and lets standing metrics sit one tier down.

2 · Every number is a dead end. Only symptom-count cards open a detail screen, and that screen is a number + sparkline with no events behind it, no food context, and no next action. The Whoop lesson: a metric with no consequence attached goes stale.

3 · The surface is blind to the wedge. A diet-trial owner's #1 question — “is this trial getting anywhere?” — is unanswerable here: Patterns renders no trial state at all, while Home, Profile, Foods, the widget, and the report all do. And eight detectors' worth of engine findings render on Home only; Patterns gets just the LLM summary sentence.

The three options below differ in how explicitly the surface is organized around those four jobs.

04

Option B — Lead with what changed

Restructure · moderate swing Engine findings reach Patterns Cards demoted to evidence tier

The dashboard becomes two tiers: “What changed” — a curated, capped set of detected changes (the same engine findings Home shows, plus card-level deltas that cleared their floors) — then “The record”, the full card stack collapsed into grouped, tappable rows. The surface is different every visit because it re-ranks on change; a quiet stretch says so honestly instead of re-dealing identical cards.

B1 · Dashboard. Change tier is capped (≤3, safety leads); the quiet state is a designed sentence, never blankness. “The record” rows open the existing card details.

Data Scientist — binding constraint Everything in the “What changed” tier must clear the engine's existing floors (established, n=1-never-colours, the G2 two-sided rule). The tier re-ranks on change but never mints a claim the detectors didn't make. The “new food entered the record” row is descriptive (a first appearance is a fact) — it is deliberately rendered in medication-slate, not symptom-rose, and sits below any safety finding, because adjacency to a symptom row must not imply causation the engine hasn't established.
05

Option C — Organize the surface around the four jobs

Swing for the fences Ask woven in (flag-gated) Sections adapt to the pet's situation

The dashboard is the four jobs from §02, as named sections that adapt to the pet: a mid-trial dog leads with the trial; a trial-less fussy cat leads with eating. Each section carries one narrated read, its evidence, and — where a question outruns what a chart can say — a suggested question that opens Ask, prefilled. The report is a first-class job, not a footer link.

D2 · The quiet state. Zero-after-N keeps the never-reassure framing that already ships — the redesign inherits it untouched.

What the annotations may and may not say Markers state facts with dates (“Jul 16 · new food”) and never draw the arrow — no “this caused that,” no highlighting a meal because it preceded a symptom. If detector ① has an established correlation, its finding renders as its own labeled row with counts, exactly as Home shows it. Visual adjacency is information; the engine's floors decide when adjacency becomes a claim.
07

Exploration — “what do the leftovers have in common?”

Feasibility-gated · not a round-1 commit

Sam's sharpest question. What we can answer today: commonality by protein (structured, canonical) and format (pâté / gravy / kibble — an enum). What we cannot: true ingredient-level commonality — ingredients_notes is a verbatim text blob with no parser (B-181 territory, its own extraction/structuring track). The Zoe lesson binds any version: verdicts with hidden evidence destroy trust — counts inline, always.
