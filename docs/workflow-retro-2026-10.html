Dispatch found more work than it finished

Engines v3, from dispatch's first run on 9/28 to the re-scope on 10/10: where the follow-ups came from, what they cost you, and a way to keep the flagging while only the findings that matter reach you.

In one minute

  • Dispatch did what its spec says, and its spec turns every finding into work. The run order went from 47 rows to 85. From 10/5 to 10/9, 19 of 24 Engines merges were follow-up rows, and only 6 of the 28 follow-up rows that shipped were on the path to v3.0.
  • The scarce resource was your decisions, not session slots. A traced follow-up row asked you for 3 to 7 typed decisions. Meanwhile the rulings that held the core aged 13 and 17 days.
  • You had built the valve; dispatch never read it. "Follow-ups go to Wave 7 by default" lived only as prose on the project page. No child prompt carried it, and dispatch's offer of new issues reads page text, never milestones.
  • The triage ran backwards. CUL-1438, a live false reassurance in the shipped Signal, has waited in Wave 7 since 9/30 while 22 off-path rows on mostly dark code shipped.
  • The wider workflow pays a fixed tax on every session. A dispatched child loads about 37K tokens before it writes code, and about 3.5% of that is its own row. All six reviewers load the full CLAUDE.md, and each change is written up four times. Separately, main has no branch protection, though CLAUDE.md says it does.
  • The fix is a sort, not another queue. Anything that could quiet a warning, leak or lose data is fixed now. A finding that blocks the goal becomes a row. Everything else parks and asks you nothing until pulled.

Why it happened

Seven mechanisms. None of them is a child misbehaving: hand-launched sessions filed at about the same rate. Dispatch multiplied the sessions a filing rule ran under.

M1

The add is mandated, and nothing offers "later"

Every child files what it finds in the project, and dispatch offers each one back as a row until you type add or skip. You approved 35 additions one at a time and skipped 19 in one reply, once.

M2

Your valve was prose

The 10/9 "Wave 7 by default" rule lived only on the page. Children see only their row's build note, and dispatch's offer never reads milestones.

M3

New slots went to what was ready

The cap rose from 3 to 6 around 10/6. The core was held on your rulings, so the new capacity went to follow-ups: 24 of 27 launches from 10/8 to 10/10 were letter rows.

M4

Each follow-up spends your decisions

PR-27g took a ruling, add, go, a plan go, apply 088 and merge, then filed the next link, added 12 minutes after its merge. Follow-ups filled the "waiting on you" limit (3 or 4 of 3) while W3 to W7 and D1 to D8 aged.

M5

Stored decisions grew one stale path per PR

may_wait was briefed as two PRs plus the render, with a louder option B on the table. It took nine rows and six migration files, about a quarter of the children's asks of you.

M6

Dark code got live-code rigor

The PR-23 summary cascade hardened a path its own record called "inert today", while a live reassurance defect waited. The two-pass cap limits passes per PR, not which PRs run.

M7

Done means absorbed the findings

The old Done means included "Wave 7 drained", so every finding moved the finish line, and the progress line hid it because the denominator grew too.

The sort: flag everything, schedule only what blocks

Each child tags a finding with the review's own words: QUIET if it can drop, soften or delay a warning; LOUD if it errs toward more warning. Untagged counts as QUIET. Then every finding goes through one of four doors.

Live harm

Could hurt an owner now

QUIET, or it can leak or lose data, in code that runs for any account: a deployed function, an applied migration or policy, a key with anyone on its allowlist, or code waiting for the next cut. Code counts as live until proven dark.

Fixed now

Its own fix, launched from any project. If it needs your ruling, a stopgap ships first: the quieter output or the leaking path switched off. Clinical and privacy questions reach you at once.

Dark gate

The same, proven dark

The dispatcher read every key it sits behind and found the allowlist empty in production, and no applied migration, trigger or policy is involved.

Gates every go-live it sits behind

A blocks relation and a Gate: label no prune can close. You decide it at the go-live: fix it, or cut the feature. Adding an account to the allowlist turns it into live harm.

Row

Blocks the goal

It stops a row on the Done means path from being correct, and says in one sentence what an owner would see.

A proposed row

Through a blocks relation to that row's issue. It waits for your typed go the first time.

Parked

Anything else

LOUD-only (even when false; your two-pass rule already lets it ship), polish, a hypothetical, a second-order guard, a process idea.

Counted, not offered

Backlog in the project's Found along the way milestone, related to its source and never a sub-issue of a row. One line in the digest; one weekly sitting.

A ruling travels with its work. A parked finding asks you nothing until it is pulled; its question then comes beside the proposed row, with the row's cost.

File the class, not the instance, and a forecast is a budget. A ruled feature that reaches its forecast plus one row comes back to you before more is built, and so does any row that collects a third follow-up in seven days. No alarm holds a safety fix, and while a fork is re-posed the quieter output stays off.

Free slots stay free, and every row shows its price. Off-path rows never launch on their own, each proposal says what it will ask of you, and when only off-path rows are ready the brief leads with the holds on the core.

Done means is fixed at kickoff, names its rows, and never includes a findings pile. The progress line shows scope growth: planned, now, added this week.

D1 · Sort findings by what they can do

Deciding: whether every finding is offered as run-order work, or sorted so only findings that could hurt an owner or that block the goal ask for your attention now.

D1 options

Consequence: A changes dispatch's steps 1, 5, 6 and 8, moves the no-row selection into the tested script, files findings in Backlog rather than Todo, and adds a Done means (rows) line to the page format. It narrows one clause of the auto ruling: an off-path row can no longer be auto.

D2 · A ruling travels with its work

Deciding: whether a parked finding asks for your ruling when it is filed, or only when it is pulled into a run order.

D2 options

Consequence: edits to /wrap step 4 and CLAUDE.md's backlog protocol; fewer arrivals in your queue (87 open against a cap of 30). Nothing leaves the never-list.

D3 · A forecast is a budget

Deciding: whether a ruled feature that outgrows its forecast comes back to you before more rows are built.

D3 options

Consequence: a Forecast: line in the decision-brief shape, a counter in the dispatch script, one line in the child prompt.

D4 · Freeze the process once this lands

Deciding: whether to pause changes to the workflow tools themselves (dispatch, /wrap, the steward, the groomer) for two weeks after this build lands. The work they run keeps going. The pause lets you see whether the changes worked before the next round of changes, the way you taste one batch before changing the recipe again.

D4 options

Consequence: the six open dispatch issues are sorted once; genuine bugs proceed. CLAUDE.md stays outside the weekly batch, since Tier 1 updates it the moment a decision is made.
