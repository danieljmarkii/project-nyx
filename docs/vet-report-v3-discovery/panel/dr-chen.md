# Dr. Alex Chen: panel read of the Culprit vet report

Small-animal GP, 12 years. I read the renders cold as page images (Cooper p1–p11, Nyx p1–p10, plus Mira p1 and the `everything-on` photo page for contrast) before reading anything about how they were made. Page numbers below are printed sheets.

---

## 1. My honest 60-second read (Cooper)

**0–5 s, masthead and patient block (p1).** Cooper, 6 y MN Lab, 31.8 kg (Jun 29), chronic pruritus, May 18 – Jul 2, 43 of 46 days logged. Good. That's my signalment line, and it's where it should be. My eye snags on the QR code and "getculprit.app" top right. They take the corner where I look for the date and the patient's ID.

**5–15 s, the SAFETY box.** I read "CHRONICITY: Itching spans 35 days" and think: yes, I know, he's itchy, that's why I put him on the trial. It's the presenting complaint, not a safety finding. Then "NOT TRIGGERED: Ran and did not trigger, which does not rule these out: loose stool getting worse (it triggers at 2 or more…" and **I stop reading mid-sentence.** That's a software log, not a clinical note. Ten seconds gone, nothing learned. Worse, the next time this box shows something that matters, I'll have been trained to skip it.

**15–25 s, the lead paragraph.** This is the gold: *day 46 of 56 · 4 of 154 feedings off-diet · the record shows Chicken in the diet during the trial*. If I read nothing else, this is what I'd use. It should be the first thing under the name.

**25–35 s, weight sparkline and the four tiles.** 32.4 → 31.8 kg on a home scale is fine; I note it and move on. Tiles: "17 / 46 d Symptom events" adds 16 itch logs to 1 loose stool, and that sum means nothing. "43 / 43" days with a meal: good. "4 / 154": good. "−0.6 kg": a repeat of the sparkline.

**35–60 s, the diet trial table.** "Switch onto the diet: … First day of an unbroken run of logged days with only foods on the trial's list: Jun 28." I read it twice. Does it mean the trial effectively started June 28? Is this day 5, not day 46? What it means is *last off-list feeding Jun 27*, and that's what it should say. Then the clock's up and I'm walking into the room.

**What I never reached in 60 s, and needed:**
- **Apoquel 16 mg SID running the whole trial, 0 doses logged** (bottom of p2, again on p5). For a pruritus diet trial, this is the single biggest interpretive fact on the record, and it's on sheet 2.
- **Pedigree Dentastix on the "allowed list" from Jun 8, ×25, contains chicken** (p2 "Label contamination"). That means the dog has had chicken nearly every day for the back half of the trial. On page 1 it sits behind "the record shows Chicken", but the *mechanism* (an "approved" daily chew) is what I need to say to the owner. Also: who approved it? I wouldn't have. The report says "vet-approved extra", but the list is owner-entered. That provenance matters.
- **The owner's own note on Jun 18: "flare after the chicken on Sunday"** (p6, Appendix A, row 14). That's the best clinical datum in eleven pages: an owner-observed, unprompted dietary challenge response, and it's buried in an event log full of em dashes.
- **NexGard started Jun 5** (p2, p3 marker). Flea control starting mid-trial is a real confounder for an itchy dog, and the itch bars do drop after it.

**What I'd actually use in the room:** the lead sentence, the 4/154 with the dated breaks (p8 table, the best table in the report), the itch weekly bars with days-logged under each week (p3, an excellent chart), the medication list, and the owner's note. Appendix B (diet history, WSAVA-style, p7) is what I'd go through *with the owner* on paper. That matches the real clinic's experience.

**What I'd skip:** the safety box as it stands, "Symptoms vs logging", "Interpreting this record" (p2, four sentences repeating "cannot establish that the elimination was clean" three ways), the loose-stool chart for one event (p3), the off-diet chart (p4, which repeats the p8 table less precisely), and the stool/blood & mucus panel for one stool (p4).

**Nyx (cat), for contrast.** The 60 s went better because there's less to argue with. The 13-week vomiting bars (p2) with the dashed "not read" weeks are honest and clear: 3/week, flat, for 7 weeks. The things I'd use: 23 vomits on 23 of 50 days, 4 in the last 7 days, no weight on record (said plainly, good), and contents food ×12 / bile ×5 / hairball ×1. Problems:
- The **foreign-material flag rests on an AI read of a photo that's "no longer retained"** (p1 flag, p4 May 23 row, p9). I'm asked to act on a picture I can't see. Either the flagged photo stays, or the flag says in the same line: *photo not available for review*.
- The pooled 12/5/1 bar hides the most interesting thing in Appendix A: contents **shift over time**, undigested food May 14 – Jun 6, then foamy bile Jun 9 – Jun 18, then unreadable. That sequence is a clinical story (fasting/bilious pattern, a change in the disease), and a bar that sums it loses it.
- Every vomit is logged at exactly 10:00 (fixture, I assume). In real data, the time relative to the last meal is what separates regurgitation from vomiting, along with **effort** (passive vs abdominal heave). The real clinic needed the photos for this. The app should capture "heaving: yes / no / didn't see" and the report should print it.

**Mira (p1)** shows the safety box done right: "DIET NOT EATEN, 38 of 38 rated feedings left unfinished … ≈7 % body weight … hepatic-lipidosis risk window." That's a real finding, with a denominator, in plain clinical language. Every safety flag should read like that one. Cooper's "chronicity" flag is the counter-example.

## 2. The never-lose list

These must survive any redesign, in this order of importance:

1. **Denominators next to every count, in the same sentence.** "4 of 154 logged feedings", "16 itch logs on 43 of 46 logged days", "18 of 23 photos legible". Not in a footnote. A count without its denominator is useless to me, and it's the first thing a clinic summarizer will strip if it lives elsewhere.
2. **Days logged per week, printed under each weekly bar** (p3 "4/7 d · 7/7 d"), and the dashed "too few days to read" weeks (Nyx p2). This is the single most trustworthy design element in the report. It tells me a zero is a zero.
3. **Trial day count, product and dates:** "day 46 of 56, RC Hydrolyzed Protein HP, started May 18, directed by Dr. A. Chen." Plus **date of last off-list feeding** (Jun 27). With a 7–14 day flare window, that tells me whether today's skin reflects the diet.
4. **Every off-diet feeding, dated, with product and protein** (p8 table). That's the clinical record of compliance. It's what I'd photocopy.
5. **The owner's free-text notes, verbatim, dated** ("flare after the chicken on Sunday", "kids fed him at the table", "ran out of the HP, one breakfast"). Owners' words are evidence. Promote them; never paraphrase them.
6. **Concurrent medications with dose, frequency, start date, and logged adherence**, including "0 doses logged". Especially antipruritics, steroids and anti-inflammatories during a pruritus trial.
7. **Photos, with date, time and event type, retained for anything flagged.** The real clinic used them to separate hairball and regurgitation from vomiting. They're among the few things in this product I can't get any other way.
8. **Weight series with dates and source** (home vs clinic scale), and an explicit "no weigh-ins recorded" when absent (Nyx p1 does this right).
9. **Provenance tags:** owner-entered vs read from label photo vs AI read vs vet-set. "AI READ · UNCONFIRMED" (Nyx p1) is exactly right. Extend it to "allowed list set by: owner" vs "set by clinic".
10. **Event markers on the time axis** (diet start, med start, food permitted). The p3 dashed lines are the right idea.
11. **The confounder statement**, said once: "3 changes overlap; no single one can be credited." Once, not three times.
12. **The appendices as a paper reference** (event log, diet history, off-diet table, med log), for the second visit with the owner. Keep them; just keep them out of my first 60 seconds.
13. **Black-and-white printability** (the textured stacks on p4 and Nyx p2). Clinics print in mono.
14. **The window and why it was chosen** ("since last vet visit", "since diet-trial start"), and when a custom window was hand-picked, the disclosure of events outside it.

## 3. Cut list (delete, or move off page 1)

- **Delete "NOT TRIGGERED" from page 1** (Cooper p1, Mira p1). Move the definition to the legend. A negative from a threshold detector isn't information I can act on.
- **Don't put the presenting complaint in the safety box.** Chronicity of the sign the trial is treating isn't a safety flag. Reserve the box for refusal, weight loss, blood, foreign material, worsening burden, and the owner having been told to call.
- **Delete the summed "Symptom events 17" tile.** Show one number per sign.
- **Delete the duplicate weight tile** (−0.6 kg repeats the sparkline next to it).
- **Collapse "Interpreting this record" (p2) and "Reading the trend" (p3)** into one confounder line on page 1.
- **Delete repeated hedges in body text.** I count "floor" 8 times and "not a diagnosis / worth confirming against the bag" 11 times in the Cooper text layer. Say each once, in the legend; the footer already says "owner-reported, not a diagnosis" on every sheet. Repetition doesn't make it more true; it teaches me to skim, and then I skim past the one hedge that matters.
- **Move off page 1:** the "Symptoms vs logging" paragraph, the allowed-list inventory with ×counts, "Antigen exposure" (and replace "Cereal" with the actual grain; "cereal" is meaningless for an allergen history), the one-event loose-stool chart, the one-stool blood & mucus panel, and the full Diet/Feeding/Meds table (p5). That table is mostly repeats.
- **Move the QR code and URL to the footer.** It's the app's marketing, not the patient's record.
- **Kill the dead space.** Nyx p1 is a quarter blank because a chart wouldn't break; Cooper p2 orphans its chart heading. Wasted paper reads as unfinished.
- **Fix the phrasing that misleads:** "First day of an unbroken run … Jun 28" → "Last off-list feeding: Jun 27 (5 days before this report)". "Elimination diet trial — Soy" → "hydrolysed soy (RC HP)". A hydrolysed trial isn't a novel-protein trial, and that distinction drives what I do at day 56.
- **Pick one feeding unit.** "154 feedings" (p1–2) vs "87 meals" (p5, p7): explain it once (meals + treats) or use one.

## 4. "Too clinical": do I agree?

**No, and the diagnosis matters.** It isn't too *clinical*. It's too **verbose and too defensive**. Clinical writing is terse. My note for this dog would be:

> Hydrolysed soy (RC HP) d46/56 for pruritus. 150/154 feedings on list. Breaks: table chicken Jun 14 (owner: flare after) and Jun 27, Pro Plan SS ×1 Jun 21; Dentastix (chicken) ×25 on owner's allowed list since Jun 8. Oclacitinib 16 mg SID throughout, adherence unlogged; afoxolaner from Jun 5. Itch logs 11 → 5 (halves), 43/46 d logged. Wt 32.4 → 31.8 kg home scale.

That's 60 words. The report says it in about 1,950 across 5 sheets. What the PM is reacting to as "clinical" is legal-defensive prose: sentences built to survive cross-examination rather than to be read. The fix is *more* clinical density with *fewer* words, not warmer language.

**What would make me trust it MORE as it gets more designed:**
- Alignment and tables: numbers in columns, units consistent, dates in one format.
- One shared time axis with exposures, meds and symptoms stacked as lanes (see §8). That's design that does analytical work.
- Small multiples with identical scales, and zero-based axes.
- Visible provenance badges (owner / label photo / AI / clinic).
- Photos as a dated contact sheet.
- Restraint: one accent colour for "flag", neutral everything else.
- Any number I can trace to a row in an appendix.

**What would make me trust it LESS:**
- Red/amber/green judgments on the owner's data ("Skin: improving ✓").
- Scores, gauges or percentages I can't decompose ("Trial adherence 97% A+"), and any composite "health score".
- Positive framing of the trial ("Great progress!", "Cooper is doing better").
- Illustrations, mascots, pet avatars, gradients, rounded "card" chrome that eats space.
- Truncated y-axes that dramatize a change of 1.
- Owner-voice copy ("Cooper's been such a good boy").
- Brand prominence larger than the patient's name.

**The line between designed and marketing:** every element on the page should encode data or help me navigate. If it's there to make the owner feel good, or to make the app look good, it's marketing, and on a clinical document it costs credibility. A test: photocopy it in black and white at 50%. If what's left is data, it's designed. If what's left is a logo and white space, it's marketing. And the owner-facing phone view (the screen-fold PNG shows my PDF shrunk to ~6–7 pt text) needs its own reflowed layout. Don't make the clinical page friendlier to solve the owner's reading problem; give the owner their own view.

## 5. An AI summary on the report

**Would I read it?** Yes. It'll be the first thing my eye lands on, which is exactly why it's dangerous.

**Would I trust it?** Only conditionally, and only after it has been right for me a few times.

**Under what label and conditions:**
- Label it plainly: **"Generated summary of owner-logged data · not reviewed by a veterinarian"**, not "AI insights" or anything cute.
- Three sentences maximum. Descriptive only: what was logged, what changed, what overlapped. No diagnosis, no prognosis, no treatment suggestion, and no reassurance ever.
- **Every number in it is computed by code and inserted, not generated.** The language model may choose order and phrasing; it may not produce a figure. Each figure must appear identically elsewhere on page 1.
- It must name confounders when they exist (concurrent meds, other changes).
- It must surface the owner's own notes when they bear on the question ("owner noted a flare after chicken Jun 14").

**What would make me throw the report away:**
- One number in the summary that doesn't match the table below it. One mismatch and I distrust every page.
- Diagnostic or causal language: "likely food allergy", "the diet is working", "chicken sensitivity confirmed".
- Reassurance: "no concerning signs", "Cooper appears healthy".
- A drug, food or date that isn't in the record.
- Advice to me, or to the owner, to change the plan.
- Anything that reads like it was written to please the owner.

**The 3-sentence summary I'd want at the top for Cooper:**

> Cooper (6 y MN Labrador, 31.8 kg) is on day 46 of a 56-day hydrolysed-soy trial (Royal Canin HP) for chronic pruritus. 150 of 154 logged feedings were on the owner's allowed list, but that list includes Pedigree Dentastix (contains chicken; fed 25 times since Jun 8), and chicken was also fed Jun 14 and Jun 27 (owner noted "flare after the chicken on Sunday"); the last off-list feeding was Jun 27. Oclacitinib 16 mg once daily ran throughout (no doses logged) and afoxolaner started Jun 5, so the fall in logged itch episodes from 11 to 5 between the first and second halves (43 of 46 days logged) can't be credited to the diet alone.

## 6. Questions: the owner's, or "what this record can't tell you"?

**Both help, in different ways, and both must be capped at three.** The owner's questions help me run the conversation; the gaps help me not over-read the data. Of the two, **"what this record can't tell you" is more valuable to me clinically**, *if* it's written as factual gaps, not as questions aimed at me. A list of AI-generated "questions for your vet" is the version I'd resent.

**Owner's questions for Cooper** (in the owner's words, chosen or written by the owner, not generated for them):
1. "He's been on Apoquel the whole time. How will we know whether the food is what helped?"
2. "The kids gave him roast chicken twice, and he seemed itchier after the first time. Do we have to restart the trial?"
3. "Are his Dentastix okay? We added them after the trial started."

**What this record can't tell you (Cooper):**
1. **Whether Apoquel was given daily.** The course is recorded (16 mg SID since Apr 30), but no doses were logged, so its effect on itch can't be separated from the diet's.
2. **How bad the itch is, or where.** The record counts logged itch episodes; it has no severity score, body distribution (paws, ears, flanks) or ear/skin-infection events, so fewer logs could mean less itch or less logging.
3. **Who approved the extras, and what's in the NexGard chew.** Dentastix was added to the allowed list on Jun 8 by owner entry, and the flavouring protein in the 2 NexGard doses isn't recorded.

**What would annoy me:**
- Generic questions ("Is itching normal for Labs?").
- Leading or diagnostic questions ("Could Cooper have a chicken allergy?"). That's the app diagnosing through the owner's mouth.
- Questions that instruct me ("Should Cooper have skin cytology?").
- Questions the report already answers.
- More than three.
- Gaps phrased as accusations of the owner ("You didn't log Apoquel").

## 7. Clinic AI

**My practice software doesn't summarize attachments today.** The PDF arrives by email; a CSR or tech files it to the patient record as an attachment; I open it on the exam-room computer or it's printed. Some colleagues use AI scribes for exam notes, and a few have tools that summarize incoming referral histories. I'd expect PDF summarization to be common within a couple of years. But I wouldn't redesign page 1 for a machine. Page 1 is for me. Make the machine layer work underneath it.

**What I'd want a clinic AI to extract** (and so what the PDF must carry as clean, labelled text):
- Signalment, owner, report window, generated date, report ID.
- Active problems and the question being monitored.
- Diet: exact product names, trial start date, day N of M, trial type (hydrolysed vs novel protein).
- Compliance: numerator/denominator, plus each break as date · product · protein.
- Date of last off-list exposure.
- Medications: drug, dose, route, frequency, start/stop, logged doses.
- Weight series: date · kg · scale source.
- Symptom counts per week with days logged per week.
- Flags, with provenance (AI / owner / computed).
- Photo index: date · event · AI read · retained or not.
- The owner's verbatim notes.

**Design implications:**
- **Write sentences that survive excerpting.** A summarizer drops footnotes and legends. "4 of 154 logged feedings off-list (a minimum)" survives; "4/154" plus "a floor, not a total" three paragraphs away does not. Put the qualifier *inside* the sentence that carries the number.
- Give every chart a one-line text equivalent with its numbers. Today the chart aria-labels don't reach the PDF, and value labels extract as junk runs like "2 2 1 1 0 2 – –".
- Add a structured data appendix (a plain table of the above, or embedded machine-readable data) so the extractor never has to read a bar chart.
- Use ISO-style dates and units on every value, and no abbreviations a model will misread.
- Keep the provenance tags in text, not only as visual badges, so "AI read · unconfirmed" survives extraction.

## 8. Page 1 as I'd design it

Letter, portrait, mono-safe, about 250 words of text plus one chart.

**Block 0, header strip (one line plus one line).**

Window May 18 – Jul 2, 2026 (since last visit) · 43 of 46 days logged · Generated Jul 2 · Report #ID. Culprit wordmark small, QR in the footer.

**Block 1, the question (one bold line).**
Diet trial for chronic pruritus: hydrolysed soy (Royal Canin HP), **day 46 of 56**, started May 18, directed by Dr. A. Chen.

**Block 2, flags (only real ones, max 3, one line each, boxed).**

- ▲ **Concurrent antipruritic:** oclacitinib 16 mg SID throughout (0 doses logged); afoxolaner from Jun 5.

**Block 3, summary (3 sentences, labelled "Generated from owner logs, not vet-reviewed").** As in §5.

**Block 4, key numbers (one row of 5 cells, each with its denominator).**
Trial day 46/56 · On-list feedings 150/154 · Itch logs 11 → 5 (first vs last 23 d) · Days logged 43/46 · Weight 32.4 → 31.8 kg (3 home weigh-ins)

**Block 5, the one chart: a trial timeline with swimlanes on a shared date axis (May 18 → Jul 2).**

- Lane 2: exposure ticks, one mark per off-list feeding, labelled by protein, with the owner-note marker on Jun 14.

**Block 6, off-list feedings (table, ≤6 rows).** Date · item · protein · owner's note. ("Jun 14 · Home roast chicken · chicken · *kids fed him at the table*"; Jun 18 note "*flare after the chicken on Sunday*" attached.)

**Block 7, medications (table).** Drug · dose · freq · start · doses logged.

**Block 8, two short columns side by side.**
- Left, **Owner's questions** (≤3, owner-written).
- Right, **Not on this record** (≤3 factual gaps, as in §6).

**Footer.** Owner-reported observations · not a diagnosis · Appendices: A event log · B diet history · C off-list feedings · D meds · E meals · F photos (N) · How to read · QR · Report ID · page x/y.

**For a vomiting case (Nyx), swap Blocks 5 and 6:**
- Block 5 becomes vomit weekly bars plus a **contents lane over time** (food → bile → unreadable), so the shift is visible.
- Block 6 becomes a **photo contact sheet**: 4–6 thumbnails with date, time and AI read, the flagged one first and always retained, or the flag labelled "photo not available".
- If weight is missing, Block 4 says "**No weight recorded, weigh before visit**" in the place weight would be.

**On saved reports attached to a vet visit: yes, please.**
- Freeze what was sent: same ID, same numbers, on every page.
- Show it on the visit with what I decided (e.g., "trial extended / challenge started").
- The next report opens with "**since report #ID sent Jul 2**", saying what changed since and noting any entries in the old window edited after it was sent. That closes the loop the real clinic did by hand, going through it with the owner on paper at the next visit.
