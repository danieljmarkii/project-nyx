# Skeptical GP read: top-of-sheet summaries (blind)

The reader: a small-animal GP with 15 minutes and a lot of "Dr. Google" printouts behind them. Read only `blind/vet-trios.json`. I check every number before I trust any sentence.

Arithmetic I checked and found consistent: Cooper day 46 = May 18 to Jul 2; Mira 0.3/4.4 kg = 6.8%, 30 refusals = 15 days x 2, the 4.6-9.0% rounding band is correct, Jun 19 + 13 days = Jul 2; Nyx May 14 to Jun 30 = 47 days. I found no fabricated number in any of the nine. What separates them is what each one leaves out, and where it loses track of what a claim covers.

---

## Cooper (dog, diet trial for itching)

**Rank: C09 > C03 > C06**

1. **C09, top of sheet.** It opens with the finding that matters: *"Chicken on 29 of 154 meals and treats: Dentastix (label lists chicken) daily from Jun 8, on the owner-entered allowed list."* This trial was contaminated from week 4, and C09 says so first. It is the only one that gives me the Apoquel dose and start date, the weight (−0.6 kg, −1.9%, 3 home readings), the loose stool on Jun 22 and the unfinished meal on Jun 24. It also explains *why* the owner's "flare after chicken" story can't be tested: chicken was logged only on Jun 2 before Jun 8, so any before/after comparison runs into the trial's own start. That is the conversation I will have to have in the room, and it has done the work for me. It is long, but each fact carries its own scope.
2. **C03.** It is honest and keeps the owner's quote with its rebuttal attached (*"this is not tested, as chicken was fed daily from Jun 8"*). It drops the Apoquel dose, the weight, the stool and the NexGard flavour caveat.
3. **C06.** It drops the owner quote, the weight and the GI entries. It also has a sentence that loses its subject: *"Itching has 16 entries … and is not a test of the diet."* The itching isn't a test. The *record* isn't one. That kind of slip is small, but it is how meaning drifts in prose.

**Distrust / softening:** *"on the owner-entered allowed list"* is necessary, but I want to know who put Dentastix on that list. If the sheet reads as if I approved a chicken chew, I'll be annoyed. *"Apoquel (doses not logged)"*: is he still getting it? If so, the itch counts mean very little, and all three should say that more plainly. None of them softens the contamination. Good.

**Missing in all three:** any itch severity or trend. 16 of 43 days tells me frequency but not direction. I'd also want lesion sites and ear involvement, whether Apoquel was actually given daily, and the NexGard product's flavour, which is checkable.

**Discard?** None of the three. C06 comes closest, by omission.

## Mira (cat, refused diet trial)

**Rank: M01 > M02 > M03** (M01 and M02 are close)

This is the one that worries me. An 8-year-old cat refused 30 feedings in a row and lost 6.8% in 18 days. I am reading this 13 days after the last entry.

1. **M01.** It is the only one that gives me the history that reframes the case: *"Recorded condition, owner-entered: chronic vomiting since Apr 10, 2026."* The vomiting came before the trial. That is probably why the trial existed, and it moves my differentials toward IBD or small-cell lymphoma rather than food. It also names the trial protein (hydrolysed chicken liver) and says medicines are *"none entered; not verified"*. Faults: it says intake wasn't observed twice in three sentences. And *"A safety flag is live: call the clinic today rather than waiting for the visit"* is an instruction to the owner, sitting on a document that is being handed to me *at* the visit. As an urgency marker I'll take it, but it belongs in the owner's app, not on my sheet.
2. **M02.** It is the most precise about uncertainty: *"2 home-scale readings rounded to 0.1 kg, so the true loss lies between 4.6% and 9.0%."* That is honest and doesn't soften anything: 4.6% in 18 days is still a problem in a cat. It is the only one that says how stale the record is: *"nothing is recorded after Jun 19, 13 days before this report."* It is missing the chronic-vomiting history and the medicines line.
3. **M03.** It has neither the 13-day gap nor the history. It ends on a dangling *"with no medicines entered, not verified"*: verified by whom, and what?

**Distrust / softening:** *"Intake not directly observed: a free-fed Purina ONE bowl was down"* is correct, but an owner will read it as "she was eating the Purina." With the weight loss, that is not reassuring, and none of the three says so outright. *"The trial was stopped"* is passive in all three: who stopped it, the owner or the clinic? I need to know.

**Missing in all three:** whether she ate the Purina ONE (and how much); anything since Jun 19 (appetite today, jaundice, lethargy); what the vomit looked like; body condition.

**Discard?** None, but **any** version that dropped the weight loss or the hepatic-lipidosis window would go straight in the bin.

## Nyx (cat, vomiting, possible foreign-material flag)

**Rank: N06 > N01 > N07** (N01 and N07 are close)

1. **N06.** It opens with the flag and labels it hard: *"· AI PHOTO READ · UNCONFIRMED · photo not retained."* Good. I don't trust an app's photo read, and I can't check one without the photo, so the label has to be that loud. N06 is the only one that tells me what the reads actually *said* over time: *"undigested food 12 (to Jun 6), bile or foam 5 (Jun 9 to 18), hairball 1 (Jun 21)"*. A shift from food to bile is clinically interesting. It is also the only one that names the diets, including a weight-care kibble for a cat whose weight is not recorded, and the ≥20 Temptations feedings. *"All 23 entries carry an owner-logged time between 10:00 and 14:00 (timing only, not an association)"* is useful, and it is properly scoped: it may just be when the owner is home. It is too long for the top of a sheet, but every line earns its place.
2. **N01.** It lists what isn't recorded (weight, meds, conditions, neuter status), which saves me questions. It is weakened by *"18 of 23 vomit entries have a legible AI photo read … and 5 have no usable read"*: it tells me the reads exist but not what they say, so the line is just noise. Worse, *"the record is logged on 43 of 90 days"* is misleading without context. Her first entry of any kind is May 14, so most of the empty days come before the owner started using the app. Read cold, "43 of 90" suggests a patchy logger.
3. **N07.** It is the only one that gives the context N01 lacks: *"4 of the first 45 and 39 of the last 45."* That tells me the logging ramped up, so I shouldn't read a rising frequency off it. But it drops the not-recorded list and anything about the photo reads beyond the flag.

**Distrust:** the foreign-material flag itself. It is one AI read of a photo nobody kept, from May 23, five weeks before the latest entry. I'll still palpate and probably take films, because escalating on a possible red flag is the right direction. I'd discard a version that stated it as fact ("plastic found"). I'd also discard one that used the later "hairball" read to reassure. None of the three does either.

**Missing in all three:** weight (critical in a cat vomiting for 7 weeks), appetite and activity now, any stool or diarrhoea, hairball and grooming history, and whether she has access to plastic, string or toys.

**Discard?** None.

## Which were written by a language model?

My guesses, for what they are worth:

- **Code: C09, M01, N06.** These are fragment-built with tags like *(chronicity flag)*, *(burden flag)* and *· AI PHOTO READ · UNCONFIRMED ·*, and fixed field labels like "Recorded condition, owner-entered:" and "Also logged:".
- **Language model: C03, C06, M02, M03, N01, N07.** These are flowing prose that open with "Name, an N-year-old…". They share exact fact phrasing with the code versions ("owner-entered", "at least 35 days"), so they look like rewrites of the same fact list. M02's rounding band could be either.

**Does it matter?** Not by authorship as such. It matters through one failure mode. Each slip I found is in a prose version: C06's itch-is-not-a-test, M03's dangling "not verified", N01's unexplained "43 of 90 days". Each prose version also *dropped* something the scoped version kept: weight, history, what the reads said. Prose buys readability by merging caveats into connecting phrases, and that is exactly where a claim drifts away from what it actually covers. In this set the code-built lines were more complete every time and never mis-scoped. The prose versions were never dishonest, but they were less complete.

## Overall: paragraph or scoped lines?

**Scoped lines, with a short headline above them.** I want one or two lines on top: the live flag, plus the single finding that changes what I do today. Under that, the scoped lines, each carrying its own count, date range and "owner-entered / unconfirmed / not recorded" label. As written, N06 and C09 are too long to call a *summary*. Shorten them by cutting lines, not by turning them into paragraphs. A "Not recorded:" line is mandatory; it is the cheapest time-saver on the page.

## Exact terms for accepting an AI-written paragraph on a clinical document

I'd accept one only if **all** of these hold:

1. **It is a rewrite, not a reading.** It is generated only from the code-built scoped lines on the same page, which stay printed beneath it. No fact, number, inference, differential, diagnosis or treatment suggestion may appear in it that isn't in those lines.
2. **Every number is machine-checked against the source before printing.** Any mismatch means the paragraph is not printed and the lines stand alone. There is no partial credit.
3. **Nothing safety-related is dropped.** Every live flag, every "not recorded" item and every "unconfirmed / owner-entered" label in the source appears in the paragraph, attached to the same claim, in the same sentence.
4. **It never reassures and never softens.** No "only", "mild", "likely", "probably", "picky" or "nothing concerning", and no absence of a finding framed as wellness.
5. **It is labelled.** Something like "Summary written by a language model from the entries below; verify against them." It carries the date of the last entry and the date the report was generated.
6. **It is fixed and auditable.** The exact text I read is stored with the report version, so if something goes wrong I can see what the owner was shown and what I was shown.
7. **It contains no instructions** to the owner or to me. M01's "call the clinic today" belongs in the app, not on my sheet.

Break any one of these and I'll read the lines and ignore the paragraph. Break #1 or #4 and I'll stop trusting the rest of the report too.
