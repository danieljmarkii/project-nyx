## Who wrote what

My guess: the scoped-line versions (**C09, M01, N06**) are code. They have fixed tags ("chronicity flag", "· AI PHOTO READ ·"), repeated stock phrases, and labels like "Also logged:" and "Not recorded:". The paragraphs (**C03, C06, M03, M02, N01, N07**) look like a language model rewriting those same lines. They share exact fragments ("Sunday being Jun 14", "her latest entries", "start not recorded") but merge clauses, drop items unevenly, and occasionally garble ("with no medicines entered, not verified"). M02 adds derived numbers not present in the lines (the 4.6 to 9.0% band, "13 days before this report"). Both are correct, and both are the kind of thing a model adds.

**Does it change how I treat them?** Yes. I treat a model paragraph as a *paraphrase of a source*, not as the source. I'll accept it at the top only if the scoped lines sit right under it so I can check any number against them. Any number that appears only in the paragraph, I verify before I act on it. I treat code lines as the record. They can still be wrong, but they're wrong consistently, and I can learn their failure modes.

## Overall

**Paragraph or scoped lines?** Scoped lines, ordered by what I'd act on, with the first line being the thing that changes today's plan. A short paragraph (two or three sentences) is welcome *above* the lines as the lead, never *instead of* them. Across these trios the best paragraph (M02) beat its lines on one patient by surfacing the gap. On the other two, the lines (C09, N06) carried facts every paragraph dropped. Paragraphs lose things, and you can't see what they lost.

**An AI-written paragraph is acceptable to me if:**
- every number in it is traceable to a record line printed on the same page, and any derived number shows its working (as M02's rounding band does);
- each qualifier stays fixed to its fact: owner-entered, home scale, unconfirmed AI read, photo not retained, not directly observed;
- it escalates on presence and never reassures on absence (no "stable", "improving", "no foreign material since");
- it states what is *not* recorded and how stale the record is;
- it's written to me, in the third person, with no instructions to the owner and no conclusion about whether the diet "worked."

**I throw the report out if:**
- a number is wrong, or exists only in the prose and doesn't check;
- it calls a refusing cat "picky" or describes intake as preference;
- it says "no off-diet foods" when the allowed list itself contains the allergen;
- it drops "unconfirmed" from an AI photo read, or treats a lack of later reads as clearance;
- it draws a causal or diagnostic conclusion ("chicken flare confirmed", "trial failed", "hairballs");
- it puts my name on a protocol I didn't write, without marking it owner-entered.
