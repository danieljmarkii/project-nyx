## 0. The question

The PM, after one real visit on the shipped beta: *"Should we have a 'get ready' OR 'how did it go?' state? It feels like this should just be a vet appt and we not have the complexity of the states."* And after round 4 drew that as a fork: *"G1b is at least an improvement… the screens having at least the same entry point helps cut down. I still have a sense that we're over-complicating this. Can we do some competitive research and take some design inspiration from others?"*

So the question put to twenty-eight products was not *what features do they have* but *how do they draw one appointment or visit across time*: how many states the user sees, how many doors, what is on the page at each moment, how notes and questions travel, how "what changed" is captured afterwards, and how long text is previewed.

**The one-sentence answer.** The products that do this well keep **one object with two faces (upcoming, past)**, let **the clock change the lead fact and the primary action** on that same object, gate **content, never doors**, treat the after-visit artifact as **something read, not a form filled**, and lose users precisely when an artifact moves to a second tab or screen. Nobody good has four phased screens.

## 1. Seven patterns, across all three lanes

| # | Pattern | Who embodies it | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **One object, two faces.** The same row is *upcoming* (details + a prepare door) and later *past* (summary + notes + attachments). Day-of is a time-boxed **state of the card** — a control that appears — never a page. | MyChart (Visits list; "visits list and the visit details page now match"; Mobile Check-In "up to 15 minutes before"), Kaiser, NHS App, Zocdoc; PetDesk / Banfield ("Join virtual visit" 15 min before) / Digitail (check-in opens T-3 days) / Vetster; Flighty, United, Uber (five statuses on one ride), DoorDash | `[E]` |
| P2 | **One named prepare door with a window.** "Get Ready" / eCheck-In, "Prepare for Appointment" — opens days before, closes at the slot; edits live inside it. | MyChart (Epic's own label is *Get Ready*), Zocdoc ("Prepare for Appointment → Modify Visit"; "during the scheduled appointment time, you will not be able to make changes"), Carbon | `[E]`/`[E†]` |
| P3 | **Time gates content, not doors.** The check-in instructions appear 48 h before; the map appears at "10 stops away"; the home screen *becomes* the trip at T-48 h. The entry point never moves. | Airbnb, Amazon, United | `[E]` |
| P4 | **After = appended to the same record, often time-bounded.** The receipt sits under the amount paid; a tip is offered for 72 h; a post-visit message channel lasts 48 h; past trips sit lower on the *same* list. | Uber, Lyft, Vetster, TripIt | `[E]` |
| P5 | **The after-artifact is read, not filled; "what changed" lands on standing objects.** No human-health product asks the patient to record what changed — the After Visit Summary is authored by the clinic; medication changes come off the clinician's record and update the standing medication list. Pet apps agree: Digitail's discharge notes update the Medications tab; Pawtient turns the visit into *one-tap suggested entries* ("weight, medications, follow-up tasks"); Kin turns it into tasks. "What's next" is a pointer to the next row, not a form. | MyChart, Kaiser, NHS, Carbon, One Medical; Digitail, Pawtient, Kin | `[E]` |
| P6 | **One note, three states.** Prep → scratchpad → summary on the same page; the inert calendar event (notes identical before and after) is the failure mode; a note that opens as a separate document elsewhere is the other. Questions typed before, opened during, notes after — one page (Medcorder). Chewy's clinics put the owner's questions on the exam-room monitor. | Granola ("open a meeting note, and you'll see some prep"), Notion Calendar, Medcorder; Apple Calendar / Fantastical as the counter-example; Chewy Vet Care `[E†]` | `[E]` |
| P7 | **Findability is the recurring failure.** Every extra tab or door moves the artifact away from the appointment: the Care Plan tab, the care plan in Messages, Delta's separate day-of tab, PetDesk's prep in a generic To Do list, myVCA's symptom text typed at booking and never shown again. | NN/g healthcare journeys ("Several participants had difficulty finding information or features that existed"), One Medical, Carbon, Delta, PetDesk, myVCA | `[E]` |

Two smaller findings the proposal leans on:

- **Long text.** Two or three clamped lines need no expander; anything longer gets *Read more* on its own line below, by a word-count threshold, with no *Read less*; lists never hide a single item; truncate "infrequently" (Thumbprint). Progressive disclosure at most two levels deep; what is needed "frequently, up front" is primary (NN/g). `[E]`
- **The day-of door is a nudge, not a surface.** Digitail pushes when check-in opens; TripIt's day-of arrives as notifications on the same object; Fi nudges "you were just at a vet — add them?" from location. The nudge lands on the object; it does not open a new room. `[E]`

## 2. What the best version looks like for Culprit `[A]` — one page, three faces

Everything below is the team's inference from §1, drawn as mock round 5 (`docs/culprit-vet-visits-mockups.html` §H). It keeps every ruling that still stands (G2 no fifth tab; the Pet tab is the home and Home is the moment; the report is the vet's artifact; one primary; Home carries three write classes and no form; a visit is provenance, never a count; no model call).

**One screen: the visit.** `Nyx's vet visit` exists from booking. Every door lands on it: the Pet-tab card, the visits list, Home's strip inside its window, History afterwards. The page has one header that states the phase, a fixed order of sections whose *content* the clock activates, and one primary that relabels.

| | Face 1 · **Booked** (from booking to the day) | Face 2 · **Today** (the appointment's day) | Face 3 · **Visited** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Header | *Tue Sep 16 · in 19 days · Completely Cat Clinic* | *Today · Completely Cat Clinic* | *Visited Tue Sep 16 · Completely Cat Clinic · Dr Miller* |
| **To raise** | One list: the record's rows (quoted, never re-derived, safety above the cap, the intake decline once) + the owner's questions. **+ Add** always. | The same list gains ticks (one tap = asked); a second, quieter tap opens a note under the row — a note open on every row would turn the checklist into a form. | *Raised 2 of 3*, each with its note. |
| **Notes** | A large field, a draft, saved as typed. Never a two-line preview: 2–3 lines shown in full, longer text with *Read more* on its own line. | **First screenful.** The field is what the room is for. | Shown in full under *What the vet said*; *Read more* only past a long threshold. |
| **Paperwork** | — | *Photograph or add a PDF* → Vet Files under the pet, linked to this visit. | Attached to the row (the NHS shape); *Add* stays. |
| **The plan** | — | Appears on the day, quiet, *after the room*: rows read the record first — a course as *Keep · Changed · Stopped*, the trial as *Keep · Extended · Ended · Switched*, *Started something new?* offering a past course back before a blank form, *Weighed at the clinic?*, *Next visit*. | Settled in the order a good After Visit Summary uses: reason → what was said → medications kept / changed / stopped / started → weight → what's next. **Add to this visit** reopens the rows. |
| **The rundown** | One line: *See the 30-day rundown* → the shipped rundown screen (Ask keeps it). | Same line, lower. | — |
| **Primary** | *Send the vet report* | *Save Nyx's visit* (nothing typed is fine; *Later* is every row's resting state) | none — this is the record; ⋯ holds *Edit* |
| **⋯** | *Change the appointment · Remove · Copy as text* (carrying To raise and the notes) | same | *Edit* |

**Home** carries the same object's lead fact by the clock — *Vet visit Tuesday · Get ready* → *Today · Completely Cat Clinic* → *Did Tuesday's visit happen? Yes · It moved · It didn't* — and every door opens the one page. Home gains navigation only; the three write classes hold. **The Pet-tab card** shows the next visit and *Open*. **The visits list** is one list, upcoming then past (TripIt); **History** shows the visited row.

**What goes away** (routes today → where it lands): `app/rundown.tsx`'s Get ready mode → Face 1; `app/vet-visits/at-the-vet.tsx` → the Notes and To raise sections of Face 2; `app/vet-visits/after.tsx` → the Plan section of Face 2 + *Save*; `app/vet-visits/[id].tsx` → Face 3; `edit.tsx` and `edit-appointment.tsx` stay as ⋯ destinations; the saved moment (D2) stays as the completion beat. Five screens become one page with three faces plus two editors.

**Engineering shape** `[A]`: the appointment and the visit stay two tables (G3 — every reader of `vet_visits` keeps meaning "a visit that happened"); the page is keyed by the appointment until the day's first plan action or *Save* mints the visit through the existing `logVisitFromAppointment`, then by the visit. No new schema for the faces. The only schema question is the one round 4 already carries: whether a verdict is written on the visit (REC 4) or only on the standing objects (§3 below).

**The trial extension track composes here**, not beside it. `changeTrialWindow` (CUL-1039, shipped 2026-09-17) is the one write; the extension track's PR 3 builds `TrialWindowSheet` with the *My vet asked for this* box. The plan's *Extended* verb opens **that sheet** with the box pre-checked and links the trial to the visit — one arithmetic home, one sheet, two doors. It sequences after PR 3, or PR 3 is built once with both mounts.

## 3. What the research says about the open decisions

| Decision | What the products do | Where that leaves us |
|---|---|---|
| **The fork (G1 one page vs G1b the screens with doors)** | P1, P3, P7 all point one way: one object, content gated by time, no artifact behind a second door. Delta's separate day-of tab is the counter-example everyone else avoids. `[E]` | The one page (§2). G1b's door fixes are contained in it — every door already lands on the same page. The Designer's warning that a long page buries the in-room field is answered by Face 2's ordering (Notes first) rather than by a separate screen. `[A]` |
| **Notes preview (the PM's G1 reaction)** | Thumbprint: no expander under three lines; *Read more* on its own line above that; never clamp a list. `[E]` | The field is large on every face; a cut-off preview is out. `[A]` |
| **REC 2 · questions with notes** | Questions travel as one flat list pinned to the appointment (Medcorder, AHRQ); Kin and Pawtient make them an agenda to work through; no verified product ticks them in the room, Chewy shows them on the room's screen. `[E]` | Keep the ticks (the PM loved them); make the note a second, quieter tap; keep the list flat and on the page. `[A]` |
| **REC 3 · one "To raise" list** | Pawtient's briefing is "the questions worth asking" and the trend summary in one; Kin builds one agenda. `[E]` | Supports the PM's reaction 3 as drawn in round 4 (G2). |
| **REC 4 · verdicts on the visit (R-D1)** | P5: changes land on standing objects; the visit shows a summary that is *read*; Pawtient's suggested entries are the closest shape to our plan rows. `[E]` | Our rows already write to the standing objects. The gap is only that *Keep* leaves no trace, so the visit cannot render a summary. Two shapes satisfy the pattern: a per-visit verdict row (round 4's REC 4), or a *reviewed at* mark on the course. Either way the visit page must read like a summary, not a form. `[A]` |
| **REC 6 · Files and Visits** | NHS keeps documents attached to the past row; Digitail's portal density around the visit is the anti-pattern. `[E]` | Files stays a sibling card; paperwork attaches to the visit row; no merge. `[A]` |
| **REC 7 · weighed at the clinic** | Pawtient's suggested entries name "weight" first. `[E]` | As drawn. |
| **The day-of door** | A nudge to the same object (Digitail T-3 d push, TripIt, Fi). `[E]` | CUL-253's reminders are the day-of door; no new surface. `[A]` |
| **The after window** | Vetster's 48 h, Lyft's 72 h: the *ask* expires, the record stays. `[E]` | Home already asks once; the plan rows stay reachable on the page for good. `[A]` |

## 4. What not to copy

- A separate day-of tab or screen (Delta) — dark most of the year for a quarterly visit.
- The inert calendar event — notes identical before and after; the visit must change.
- A care plan in another tab or in Messages (One Medical, Carbon) — the NN/g findability failure.
- PetDesk's Cancel that vanishes silently after the reminder — a time-gated control needs its reason on the card.
- Retrospective forms asking reason / professional / price up front (11pets, PetNoter).
- After Visit Summary length and jargon — long summaries go unread.

## C. Lane 3 — one object across time, outside health (verbatim)

- **Flighty** · one flight · phases shown by the card's *lead fact* changing, not by screens: "Your flight today is on time" → "Check In is now open" → gate → "Taxiing for 13m" → in-flight arc → "22m early" → "Baggage Claim, Belt 6"; past flights land in a free flight log [E] flighty.com; App Store id1358823008.
- **United** · one trip on the *home screen*: "contextualized home screen starting 48 hours before their flight which provides dynamic updates based on where a customer is in their travel journey" (2020-10-28) [E] prnewswire.com; Live Activity counts down to boarding, then to landing, then lists the carousel [E] afar.com. Takeaway: same entry point, the surface *becomes* the trip at T-48 h.
- **Delta** · the counter-example: "My Trips" plus a day-of tab ("Today puts everything you need for your day of travel in one convenient place") [E] delta.com/us/en/delta-digital/mobile. Even the split is one aggregating surface, not a chain of screens.
- **TripIt** · one itinerary; day-of arrives as notifications on the same object; past trips sit "below any upcoming trips" on one list [E] help.tripit.com.
- **Airbnb** · one reservation on Trips; check-in instructions appear "48 hours (local time) before" [E] airbnb.com/help/article/41; the 2025 redesign makes Trips "an advanced travel itinerary" that "welcomes you" on arrival [E] news.airbnb.com.
- **Uber / Lyft** · one ride with five statuses ("Upcoming", "Awaiting pickup", "In progress", "Completed", "Attention needed") [E]; the receipt sits under the amount paid [E]; Lyft's past ride carries time-bounded actions — "Tip Driver up to 72 hours after payment", "Find Lost Item" [E]. Takeaway: after = actions appended to the same record, with expiry.
- **DoorDash / Amazon** · phase = a status element inside the order; the live map appears when the clock allows ("10 stops away") [E]/[E†].
- **Apple Calendar / Fantastical** · one event, inert over time: notes identical before and after; past events merely dimmed [E]. The failure mode.
- **Google Calendar / Notion** · notes hang off the event through one door ("Take meeting notes"); Notion's page spans agenda → live notes → summary with action items [E].
- **Granola** · the cleanest exemplar: one note per meeting; before, a Brief ("open a meeting note, and you'll see some prep"); during, a scratchpad; after, "Enhancing notes" into a summary with decisions and action items [E] granola.ai.
- **Otter** · before is settings, during live words, after a summary [E]; summary email within 2 hours [E†].
- **Eventbrite / Ticketmaster** · one ticket; status changes arrive as notifications; upcoming-vs-past separation not verified [E].
- **Things 3 / Apple Reminders / Agenda** · Today is a query, not a place [E].
- **UX writing** · NN/g progressive disclosure (≤2 levels; frequent needs up front) [E]; Sims, *Just-in-Time Interfaces* ("home screens that reflect current activity and status") [E]; Thumbprint truncation (2–3 clamped lines need no expander; *Read more* on its own line; never hide one list item; truncate "infrequently") [E]; NN/g *Learn More* links [E].

Patterns (lane 3): a status enum on one object where the clock picks the lead fact; time gates content, never doors; after = actions appended to the record, time-bounded; one note, three states; Today is a view, not a screen.
