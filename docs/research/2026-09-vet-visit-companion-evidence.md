## 1. Competitive — owner-side vet-visit features, vet-side scribes, human-health analogues

Tag note for this section: `[E†]` = verified only through the search index's excerpt of the cited page (the page itself blocked a fetch).

### 1.1 Whitespace or commodity?
- **The parts are commodities; the loop is whitespace.** Recording and transcription are OS-level on iPhone (Notes since iOS 18, call recording since 18.1, iOS 26's SpeechAnalyzer for third-party apps). Clinic-side AI scribes already record the room in a meaningful share of US practices and several emit a *client summary*. No product found — owner-side or human-side — combines *prep before → capture during → the visit landing in the owner's own longitudinal record after*. `[E]` for every listing fetched; `[A]` that nothing unindexed exists.
- **Exactly one shipped owner-side incumbent, and it is tiny.** Pawtient AI (iOS + Android, first release 2025-12-09) store-verifiably ships "Record the vet visit, get an AI summary — with the vet's permission (Premium)" and a pre-visit "Vet Visit Mode" (owner-assembled talking points + trend summary) at $5.99/mo, with **one** App Store rating on 2026-09-09. `[E]` apps.apple.com/us/app/pawtient-ai/id6756168872 · generalrev.com/articles/record-pet-vet-appointment/
- **The adjacent giant positions on the same phrase without recording.** Fi Intelligence (2026-03-17, "available to all Fi members") calls itself "the perfect vet visit companion": documented collar-derived observations plus Q&A over uploaded vet records ("what happened at my last vet appointment?"). No audio capture, no transcript, hardware-tethered. `[E†]` businesswire.com (2026-03-17) · support.tryfi.com (Fi Intelligence; Vets tab)
- **"Vet-ready summary" is a crowded shelf; "questions from your pet's own record" is empty.** PetSage (30-day summary), Pet Check AI (symptom report), CompanAIn and PerkyPet (marketed; PerkyPet's store links still 404) all sell a summary to bring. Nothing verified generates suggested questions from the record — Pawtient's are editorial, VetCore ships a static "question pack", Fi answers rather than proposes. Nobody does anything live in the room. `[E]`
- **Consent is the design problem more than the legal one.** The owner is a party to the conversation, so in 39 states + DC they may record without asking; every vet-side scribe vendor, AAHA's owner-facing guidance, VIN and Pawtient itself still frame *asking first* as the norm, and 2025–26 human-health lawsuits turned on boilerplate consent text nobody heard. `[E]`

### 1.4 Lane C — human-health consumer analogues
- **Abridge's patient app is gone.** The 2019 consumer app (record a visit, transcript with medical terms bolded; UPMC 2020 required consent from both patient and physician) was removed from Google Play 2025-07-26; the company pivoted to clinician-side distribution (Epic integration, 300+ health systems): "CIOs and IT teams buy the system… Clinicians use it because they have to." `[E]` upmc.com (2020) · appbrain.com (removal)
- **Medcorder** (free, last update 2025-08-25): one-tap record, transcript, family sharing, a pre-appointment question list the family can add to, a reminder 15 minutes before — and it **"prompts you to confirm that you have your physician's consent before every recording."** `[E]` medcorder.com/faqs
- **Apple** plays a non-disableable spoken notice to all participants when a call is recorded; Notes transcribes on-device; iOS 26 exposes the engine to third parties. **Pixel Recorder's** on-device summaries drove daily use and a 24 % rise in saved recordings. `[E]` support.apple.com · android-developers.googleblog.com (2024-08)
- **SecondEars** (Peter MacCallum, hospital-endorsed patient app): 51 invited → 30 consented → 24 recorded → 22 listened; 100 % would use again; the clinician discomfort that was anticipated "did not eventuate in practice"; the recording went to the medical record before the patient. `[E]` formative.jmir.org (2019) · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7001044/
- **Dartmouth Open Recordings** (JAMIA Open, 2026-04-03): 30 of 32 patients recorded ≥1 visit, 28 listened, 52 % shared with care partners, recordings used to prepare for the next visit; an Arizona neurology practice gets a 10 % malpractice-premium reduction; barriers were workflow and forgotten devices. `[E]` academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/9/2/ooag033
- **The demand and benefit evidence:** 40–80 % of medical information is forgotten immediately and half of what is retained is wrong (Kessels 2003); across 33 studies 72 % (weighted) of patients listened to their recording and most studies found sharing with others (Tsulukidze 2014); 69 % of UK respondents want to record, 15 % had done so covertly (Elwyn 2015); 92 % of German cancer patients want recordings, 5 % have them (2024). Covert recording is driven by "a pre-existing lack of trust, a fear that recording would be denied"; permission-seekers "prioritise a trusting relationship". `[E]`
- **Clinician reaction is the adoption risk:** 56 % of surveyed UK surgeons uncomfortable, 23 % would stop the consultation, 62 % unaware of their legal rights (2022); covertly recorded clinicians felt "disrespected, uncomfortable, devalued". Where recording is open and endorsed the discomfort did not materialise. `[E]`

### 1.5 Consent norms that shape an owner-side design (`[A]`, grounded in §1.3–1.4)
1. Ask-first is the norm even where the law does not require it; the honest control is a real, un-pre-checked tap logged with a timestamp, never boilerplate (the Sharp allegation).
2. Copy the two precedents already in owners' pockets: Apple's non-disableable notice and Medcorder's confirm-before-every-recording.
3. Design "no" as a first-class path (every vendor falls back to notes and documents the refusal).
4. Audio only (AAHA's "no video" expectation); delete-audio-keep-transcript is the vendor norm.
5. Expect the room is already being recorded: "your clinic may already use an AI scribe — ask for their visit summary" turns a competitor artifact into an import.
6. The moment to offer recording is the prep card in the waiting room, not arrival at the exam room (SecondEars: one patient forgot to press record; Medcorder's 15-minute reminder).

### 1.6 Positioning — claims to retire, claims the evidence supports
**Retire:** "first app to let owners record their vet visit" (Pawtient, Dec 2025; Medcorder-class since 2018) · "the only pre-visit summary for your vet" (Fi, PetSage, Pawtient, Pet Check AI) · "vets don't record visits" (ambient scribes are mainstream) · "you need your vet's permission to record" (legally false in 39 states + DC — say *ask first* as a value, never as the law) · "the first AI summary of what the vet said" (clinic-side summaries already reach owners) · any implication that Fi records visits.
**Supported:** "prepared before, captured during, in your record after — at any clinic" (no owner-side product closes the loop) · "your vet's words next to your own log" (the longitudinal, owner-held, clinical-grade record is the moat) · "questions drawn from your pet's own record" (whitespace) · "ask first — built in" (a trust claim no incumbent makes prominently) · "transcribed on your phone" (if SpeechAnalyzer ships) · "bring the clinic's summary in, too" (Vet Files already has the surface). **Live in-room suggestions** are genuine whitespace and the evidence argues against leading with them: a decision at the moment of event, competition with the vet for attention, and 23 % of surveyed clinicians would stop a consult over a phone recording.

### 2.2 Risk posture in six bullets
1. **The owner is the legal recorder; Culprit is the tool. The law binds the owner first.** In-person recording by a participant is lawful without the other side's consent in 39 jurisdictions including Nebraska (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 86-290(2)(c)); it requires every participant's consent (or, in Oregon, that every participant is "specifically informed") in eleven states — CA, FL, IL, MD, MA, MI (a judicially recognised participant exception; treat as all-party for policy), MT (knowledge), NH, OR, PA, WA — plus Delaware as a caution. A closed exam room is a private conversation under every state's reasonable-expectation test; Massachusetts has no such test, the element is *secrecy*. `[E]`
2. **App-maker exposure is real, recent and design-controllable.** *In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation* (N.D. Cal., 2026-08-13; order PDF returned 403, secondary-sourced) let federal Wiretap Act, CIPA § 631 and BIPA claims proceed against a transcription vendor, drawing the line: *"A vendor that transcribes and hands the transcript back is a tool. A vendor that transcribes, retains, and trains is operating for its own commercial account."* Culprit stays on the tool side by never retaining raw audio server-side, never training, never using content for its own purposes, and binding every processor to no-train terms. A tool that always shows it is recording is outside 18 U.S.C. § 2512's "primarily useful for surreptitious interception" device offence. `[E]` statute; `[A]` application.
3. **Apple raises the floor independently of state law.** Guideline 2.5.14: "Apps must request explicit user consent and provide a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording… This includes any use of the device camera, microphone…". 5.1.2(i) (revised 2025-11-13): "clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." 5.1.1(ix): apps that "require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity… not by an individual developer" — a live question for a solo Nebraska individual with no entity. `[E]`
4. **Anthropic's API does not accept audio, so transcription is a new processor unless it happens on-device.** iOS 26's SpeechAnalyzer transcribes entirely on device and needs only the microphone permission; the legacy `SFSpeechRecognizer` sends audio to Apple unless `requiresOnDeviceRecognition` is true *and* the device supports it. On-device-first means zero new processors; the transcript then rides the existing Anthropic key under commercial no-train terms with 30-day deletion. If a server fallback were ever needed: OpenAI transcription is no-train by default and zero-retention-eligible; Google logs nothing by default; AssemblyAI keeps transcripts indefinitely by default; Deepgram's model-improvement program is **on unless `mip_opt_out=true`**. `[E]`
5. **Speaker diarization is the live biometric-litigation frontier — leave it out.** BIPA, Texas CUBI and Washington RCW 19.375 all name "voiceprint"; *Basich v. Microsoft* (W.D. Wash., 2026-02-05, Teams diarization), *Cruz v. Fireflies.AI* (2025-12-18) and Otter (BIPA claims survived dismissal) target exactly "who said what". AssemblyAI tells customers speaker embeddings "may be deemed biometric data" and the customer owes the notices and consents. No court has ruled whether within-recording diarization is a voiceprint. `[E]`
6. **Retention and the live feature.** Defensible default: raw audio never uploaded, deleted on device once the transcript is saved or on discard; the transcript an owner-deletable, pet-scoped row that cascades on account deletion and joins the local wipe. The 2017 FTC voice-recording policy (audio held only long enough to convert to text, now codified in the 2025 COPPA rule) is the closest regulatory articulation. **Live AI suggestions are the highest-risk item**: they stream third parties' speech to a server in real time — the fact pattern pleaded against Otter. `[E]`

**Two points that hold in every state, including Nebraska:** (1) the one-party rule protects only conversations the owner is a **party to** — audio bleed from a waiting room (other clients, another family) is *non-participant* interception with no consent exception anywhere, the single strongest reason to confine the feature to the exam room; (2) every consent exception dies if the recording is made "for the purpose of committing any criminal or tortious act" — the Otter court used that carve-out against the *vendor's* own training use, another reason Culprit must have no independent use of the content. `[E]` statutes; `[A]` application.

**2024–2026 changes:** no state moved from one-party to all-party for in-person conversations; Oregon's notice rule was upheld (2025); Michigan's participant exception reaffirmed (2021–22); Illinois amended BIPA damages to one violation per person and allowed e-signature releases (P.A. 103-0769, 2024-08-02); California's CIPA reform stalled; Otter (2026-08-13), *Cruz v. Fireflies* (2025-12) and *Basich v. Microsoft* (2026-02) opened the AI-notetaker front; the Nebraska Data Privacy Act took effect 2025-01-01 (small-business exemption applies to Culprit today `[A]`); Apple's third-party-AI clause landed 2025-11-13; the COPPA rule amendments (2025-04) added voiceprints to "personal information". `[E]`

### 2.4 The human-medical analogue
- **JAMA 2017 (Elwyn, Barr, Castaldo):** 39 states + DC one-party, 11 all-party; in one-party states "the doctor may ask the patient not to proceed, but the patient has the right to record… the doctor can choose to continue… or terminate the visit"; a patient-retained recording is not subject to HIPAA. `[E]` via americanpatient.org (the JAMA PDF returned 403).
- **Dartmouth Open Recordings:** 71 % of patients listened, 68 % shared with a caregiver (meta-review of 33 studies); Barr (2026-07): "I am a big advocate for open recording… secret recordings damage trust"; a 2026 JAMIA Open case study documents three US settings routinely giving patients recordings. `[E]`
- **Consumer apps' in-product consent:** Medcorder confirms physician consent before every recording; Abridge (consumer era) relied on editorial scripts ("Is it OK if I record this conversation? It helps me stay in the moment") before pivoting to clinician-side scribing; Pawtient AI asks permission first and prefers transcripts to raw audio; **Apple's iOS 18 call recording** plays an audible notice to all participants and replays it periodically. `[E]`

### 2.5 Veterinary-specific
No AVMA policy on clients recording appointments was found; the Principles of Veterinary Medical Ethics bind the *vet* to confidentiality (e.g. Cal. Veterinary Practice News (2018): a Pennsylvania practice owner faced criminal charges when exam-room cameras captured audio. The inverse trend — clinics recording clients with AI scribes and consent tooling (ScribbleVet's poster generator; a BC clinic recording exam-room audio with front-desk opt-out) — means front-line staff are increasingly used to a consent ceremony. ### 2.6 Apple App Store requirements
- 2.5.14 explicit consent + clear visual/audible indication; 5.1.1(i)/(ii) policy + purpose strings; 5.1.2(i) third-party AI disclosure + explicit permission (Apple's expectation is an in-app prompt, not only a policy link); 5.1.1(ix) legal-entity submission for sensitive information. `[E]` developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · developer.apple.com/news/?id=ey6d8onl
- **Purpose strings:** `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` required for any microphone access; `NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription` only "if your app uses APIs that send user data to Apple's speech recognition servers" — on-device SpeechAnalyzer needs the microphone string alone (verify at build). `[E]`
- **App Privacy label:** "Audio Data" = "the user's voice or sound recordings"; "collect" means transmitting off device beyond real-time servicing; "data that is processed only on device is not 'collected'". So on-device transcription with only the transcript uploaded → Audio Data stays **No**, the transcript is Other User Content (already Yes); any upload of audio → Audio Data **Yes**; a transient server-STT call is a third-party AI share under 5.1.2(i). `[E]` developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/

### 2.8 Biometric / voiceprint law
BIPA (740 ILCS 14): "voiceprint" is a biometric identifier; written release (e-signature suffices since 2024) and a public retention schedule before collection; $1,000 / $5,000 per violation, now one violation per person; private right of action. Texas CUBI: notice + consent, destroy within a year of purpose, up to $25,000, AG-only. Washington RCW 19.375: voiceprint is biometric, audio recordings excluded, duty attaches on "enrolling" a template. Nebraska DPA (2025): "voice print" is biometric when generated to identify an individual; small businesses exempt except for selling sensitive data. **Does diarization create a voiceprint? Unresolved and actively litigated** (Basich, Cruz, Otter). Culprit has no Illinois geofence because it collects no location. The defensible product answer is no diarization, no embeddings, no voice-derived attribution; if attribution is ever wanted, infer roles from content ("recheck in two weeks" is the vet) or let the owner tag lines — text operations. `[E]/[A]`

### 2.9 The consent UX contract the lane recommends (design input to the spec §6)
A single all-party posture everywhere (the app collects no location, so it cannot know the state; one rule satisfies the strictest state and matches every consumer precedent). **A.** A first-use full-screen sheet (never modal-on-modal): what recording does, that it captures everyone in the room, that audio stays on the phone and only the transcript syncs, that some states require everyone's agreement and the owner is responsible for asking; the "ask your vet first" script in the app's register. Copy constraints: never state or imply recording is legal for this owner; never "you don't need permission in your state"; never frame the vet's refusal as an obstacle; never promise the transcript is complete. **B.** Every recording: one un-pre-checked acknowledgment ("Everyone in the room knows I'm recording and is okay with it") stored as `consent_acknowledged_at`; "start once the exam-room door is closed"; no auto-start, no start from the widget. **C.** An audible announcement at start ("Culprit is recording this visit"), captured in the recording — the RCW 9.73.030(3) form of consent, Oregon's "specifically informed", the defeat of Massachusetts' secrecy element, Montana's warning, and Apple's own choice. *Designer dissent recorded: socially awkward in a small room; the tap already carries the consent. T&S: the tap is the owner's claim, the announcement is the record.* **D.** A persistent in-app recording pill on every reachable screen; foreground-only (no `UIBackgroundModes: audio` — locking the phone stops the recording and says so); auto-stop ceiling. **E.** Stop → review the transcript on device → keep or discard; delete lines before saving; "Summarize" is a separate explicit action with a first-use 5.1.2(i) disclosure naming Anthropic; never auto-summarise. **F.** Never: covert or screen-off mode, auto-start, background capture, voice speaker identification, a public share link for transcripts, any Culprit use beyond the owner's record, any "it's legal here" copy. **G.** A Terms addition (attorney-drafted): an owner covenant to comply with recording law; Culprit records nothing covertly; transcripts are automated and may contain errors.

### 2.10 Data posture the lane recommends
On-device transcription only, with an honest "not available on this phone" instead of a silent server fallback; raw audio in the app sandbox, excluded from iCloud backup, deleted when the transcript is saved or discarded (24 h ceiling for an unfinished session), registered in `wipeLocalSession`; no audio bucket in v1.x; the transcript a pet-scoped row with RLS default-deny cascading from `pets`, **hard-deleted on owner request** (an honest erasure story for other people's words beats the soft-delete convention that exists for events — flagged for the Dir. of Engineering as a second documented exception); transcripts in the export scope; summaries text-only, explicit, stored as derived rows deleted with the transcript; no biometrics; caps under the existing `ai_usage` framework, the transcript itself never gated; the privacy-policy edits enumerated (§1 microphone paragraph, §3 the Anthropic transcript sentence, §6 retention, §7 deletion bullets, a new "Recording other people" section); the App Privacy answers re-checked; the mic purpose string added via the audio plugin, with no background audio mode; verified on the built artifact (`Info.plist`, `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`) and by a packet capture showing audio never hits the network.

## 3. Technical feasibility — audio capture, speech-to-text, rich text, the LLM step (Expo managed, SDK 57)

### 3.1 Summary
- **The Claude API does not accept audio.** Messages content types are text, image and PDF/document; the SDK feature request for audio input (Feb 2026) was closed "not planned". Any server-side transcription is a second vendor. `[E]` platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/overview · github.com/anthropics/anthropic-sdk-python/issues/1198
- **On-device long-form speech-to-text is an iOS 26 capability.** Apple's `SpeechAnalyzer` / `SpeechTranscriber` benchmarks at 2.12 % clean / 4.56 % noisy word-error against 9.0 % / 16.3 % for the legacy `SFSpeechRecognizer`, ~3× faster than Whisper Small, long-form and distant-mic by design, models in the OS asset catalog (zero app-size cost). It is iOS 26-only, has no contextual-string vocabulary on the long-form transcriber, and the two community wrappers are immature (one is file-only and non-streaming; one is English-only and "best under a minute"), so a thin local Swift Expo module is the path. `[E]` lyonesse.app/blog/apple-speech-api-benchmark.html · blakecrosley.com/blog/speech-framework-vs-sfspeechrecognizer · react-native-ai.dev/docs/apple/transcription · github.com/DaveyEke/expo-speech-transcriber
- **The legacy on-device path (iOS 17–25) is officially unlimited and practically undocumented past one minute.** Apple's WWDC 2019 statement says the server limits on request count and audio duration "do not apply" on-device; the maintained community module (`expo-speech-recognition`, SDK 57 release 2026-08-30) says long-form files "will likely want" on-device recognition and chunks files to dodge an undocumented one-minute cap on URL requests; a 2026 third-party guide claims the limit applies to both — a conflict only a spike settles. File mode takes 16 kHz 16-bit mono WAV. An open issue reports "Recognizer is unavailable" on iOS 26.4. `[E]` github.com/jamsch/expo-speech-recognition (README, issue #145) · developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/256/ · picovoice.ai/blog/ios-speech-recognition/
- **Android on-device recognition is weak:** segmented results, 5–10 s silence timeouts and restart loops, no support on Android 12, Google's recognition service required on 13+. `[E]` github.com/csdcorp/speech_to_text/issues/619 · jamsch README
- **On-device Whisper (`whisper.rn` 0.7.4, active)** works under EAS Build without ejecting but needs manual native config, 16 kHz WAV input, and is slower and less accurate than SpeechAnalyzer at phone-sized models (tiny 7.9 % / 17.0 %, base 5.4 % / 12.5 %, small 3.7 % / 8.0 % WER; 75–466 MiB downloads; 273–852 MB RAM). Its one advantage is cross-platform. `[E]` github.com/mybigday/whisper.rn · github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp · argmaxinc.com/blog/iphone-17-on-device-inference-benchmarks
- **`expo-audio` records fine; background recording is young.** Background recording shipped in 55.0.0 (2026-01-21) behind the `enableBackgroundRecording` plugin (dev build / EAS); Android adds a foreground service with a persistent notification; the docs warn about battery. The predecessor `expo-av` has a documented history of dropped audio on screen lock, a mic muted after ~1 min in Android standby, and no resume after a phone call — the exact failures a 30-minute visit produces. No documented interruption-ended resume event for recordings. `[E]` docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/audio/ · expo-audio CHANGELOG · expo/expo issues #31964, #26131, #25977, #10501
- **File sizes at 30 minutes** `[A]` on the documented presets: 128 kbps stereo → 28.8 MB (over OpenAI's 25 MB cap); **mono AAC 48 kbps → 10.8 MB (the sensible default)**; 16 kHz 16-bit mono PCM (what the legacy on-device file path needs) → 57.6 MB. Android's `LOW_QUALITY` preset is AMR-NB 8 kHz — unusable for STT.
- **iOS shows its own recording indicator** (orange dot / Dynamic Island) whenever the microphone is live, including in the background; it cannot be suppressed. `[MK]`
- **Rich text:** Expo's own guide recommends starting with a native editor from Software Mansion; `react-native-enriched-markdown` 1.0.2 (2026-08-20) is native, claims VoiceOver/TalkBack, and stores Markdown — three weeks past 1.0. ### 3.4 Offline-first fit (repo facts `[E]`)
- Typed notes already have a home: `vet_visits.notes TEXT`, LWW, `synced` flags, pushed via `serializeQueuePush('vet_visits', …)` (`lib/sync.ts:1170`); the visit surface itself is the missing piece (the Vet Files audit's "write-only capture path").

- An on-device transcript is produced after `stop()` and stored as text with engine provenance; it needs no network to read or edit.
