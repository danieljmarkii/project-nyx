# Make the first log pay off

The evidence backs the PM's concern. Asking owners to keep logging for a pattern that arrives weeks later sets the product against base rates where **median 30-day retention for health and tracker apps sits between about 3% and 8%**. The people who do keep logging are tracking toward a bounded question and use their data **on the day they generate it**, not weeks later. Leading products do not fix the wait by producing early insight. They separate the **raw record**, shown from the first entry, from the **verdict**, which they hold back behind a named, finite state defined by how much data exists. They seed history from memory or imports, and they give value about the present that needs no history at all. For Culprit, the value of the first log is the record itself: a strip of the pet's real days that the vet will read. Add a bounded frame for the trial ("day 9 of 56") and an escalation route that is always there and never depends on how much has been logged, and the first day pays off without any pattern claim. Honesty about thin data can live in the visual encoding rather than in copy. "Not logged", "looked fine" and "something happened" are three distinct marks, and "not enough yet" is its own state with a number attached. That fits the PM's friendly, low-copy Home. The ghost of the Signal chart is the weakest element in the current mock. No study tests a persistent ghost preview, the design systems say decoration must never carry information, and in a health context a flat placeholder reads as "stable". Streaks are contraindicated: broken streaks cut continuation, most of all when the user blames themselves, and gamified gains fall from g=0.42 to g=0.15 once the game ends. The largest gaps in the evidence are no published retention data for any pet-health app, no study of ghost previews or progress-to-baseline indicators, and no study that measures time to first insight.

## Most loggers quit before a weeks-long engine can speak

The base rates are stark. Eysenbach's "law of attrition" treats dropout as the default outcome in eHealth ([JMIR 2005](https://www.jmir.org/2005/1/e11)). Baumel et al. used independent panel data on popular mental-health apps and found **median 30-day retention of 3.3%, with tracker apps at 6.1%** ([JMIR 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/E14567/)). Industry benchmarks put day-30 retention for health and fitness apps at 3% (AppsFlyer) to 8% (Adjust) ([Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks)). Food journaling, the closest relative of Culprit's meal log, is the canonical high-burden case. Only **3% of 190,000 downloads of The Eatery led to use beyond one week**, as cited in [Cordeiro et al. 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Cordeiro2015.pdf). At those rates, an engine that needs weeks of logs will produce output for a small minority of installs unless something else keeps owners logging through the first weeks.

Effort is the main reason people stop. In Epstein et al.'s study of former trackers, the cost of collecting data was the **top reason for stopping in every domain: 45.6% for activity, 42.9% for location and 57.1% for finance** ([CHI 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5428074)). Food journalers called logging "too much effort", "time-consuming" and "tedious". Their abandonment snowballed in a specific way: a forgotten entry made the app's totals wrong, and the wrong totals made them quit ("Every time I start I forget one meal or another so it becomes less accurate. Then I just forget completely") ([Cordeiro et al. 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Cordeiro2015.pdf)). Lowering effort helps, but only part of the problem. A photo-first food journal cut "skipped because too difficult" from **60% to 22%** and removed skipping caused by unknown ingredients, but forgetting stayed near universal (95% with prior tools, 93% with the photo journal) ([Cordeiro et al. 2015b](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jfogarty/publications/chi2015-decaf.pdf)). Effort and forgetting are separate problems with separate fixes. Confirming a usual meal instead of entering it addresses the uncertainty that drives skipping. A cue at the right moment addresses forgetting.

The literature's sharpest finding for Culprit concerns *when* people use their data. Rooksby et al. found that tracker data "appeared to be short term, usually on the day that the data was generated". Only one participant cared about long-term data, and "building a long term, accurate record was a minority pursuit" ([CHI 2014](https://pubweb.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Rooksby2014.pdf)). The same paper names a **"diagnostic" tracking style**: one participant logged medication and diet to find the cause of stomach problems, and this kind of tracking "did not need to carry on indefinitely, just until the person was satisfied that they have an answer". That describes Culprit's wedge owner exactly. The lived-informatics work treats lapsing, suspending and stopping once a question is answered as normal outcomes, not failures. It recommends planning for lapse and resumption, avoiding reminders that point out the lapse, and supporting "happy abandonment" when the goal is met ([Epstein et al. 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Epstein2015.pdf); [Epstein et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5428074)).

Bounded protocols are the counter-evidence to the attrition curve. TummyTrials ran a fixed 12-day self-experiment for IBS. **12 of 15 analysed patients reported 100% compliance**, and participants credited "the fixed duration, clear rules, and app reminders" ([Karkar et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5432136)). Parents tracking infants started "in response to health professionals' questions" and stopped once confident; few continued past six months ([Digital Child factsheet](https://digitalchild.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Key-Findings-Baby-apps.pdf)). Veterinary data show both how much the work matters and how fragile it is. Only **40.1% of owners in a dermatology-practice elimination-diet survey reported full adherence** ([Painter et al. 2019, via OUCI](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/works/98ZRd6a4/)). **75% of Ontario owners reported dietary indiscretions** during an elimination trial, and owners relied on recall for diet history ([Tiffany et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380261)). Owner-kept seizure diaries tend to undercount ([Frontiers Vet Sci 2023](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2023.1278618/text)).

Read together, these studies support an inference rather than a measured result. "Log for this 8-week trial so your vet can read it" is the bounded, diagnostic pattern people sustain. "Keep logging so a Signal might appear" is the open-ended pattern most people abandon. No controlled study compares the two framings of the same app. That comparison is the single most useful thing Culprit could measure.

## Leading trackers show the raw record now and hold back only the verdict

Across wearables, cycle apps, symptom trackers and pet trackers, the dominant pattern is a clean split. **Raw per-entry data appears immediately. The comparative verdict waits for a stated amount of data.** On Garmin's HRV Status, nightly HRV values show from night one, and the Balanced/Unbalanced band waits for about three weeks of sleep data ([Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-607F08F6-33FC-40BF-9727-84E54043D82D/EN-US/GUID-9282196F-D969-404D-B678-F48A13D8D0CB.html); [DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2022/07/garmin-previews-watches.html)). Tractive, the closest pet analogue, says "Activity, sleep, and scratch data appear from day one". Resting heart rate, respiratory rate and barking show **"Learning"** for at least seven days before a status appears ([Tractive](https://help.tractive.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011024119-What-health-features-does-Tractive-track)). Apple Vitals asks for seven nights to "establish your typical range" ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/120142)). Fitbit's Daily Readiness needs seven nights ([Google](https://support.google.com/fitbit/answer/14236710)), and an earlier version of its tile showed a countdown to the first score ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/what-fitbit-daily-readiness-score-and-how-do-i-use-it)). That countdown appears to be gone from the current help page, so treat it as historical.

| Phase | What the strongest products do | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Ask for memory backfill to seed the first output, or start with a stated prior that will become personal | Flo and Clue ask for the last period dates; Clue asks for "the dates of your last 3 periods" ([Clue](https://support.helloclue.com/hc/en-us/articles/29049246302237-My-current-tracker-knows-my-cycle-already-How-long-would-it-take-Clue-to-give-accurate-predictions)); Copilot Money finishes onboarding only after 1–2 years of bank history has imported ([Copilot](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/5542003-historical-transaction-data)) |
| Day 1 | Reflect the raw entry back; withhold any comparison to the user's own baseline | Tractive day-one activity and sleep; Garmin nightly HRV; WHOOP: "It's completely normal not to see much data on your first day" ([WHOOP Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/my-data-is-not-visible-in-the-app-i-got-the-band-yesterday-will-it-take-some-time-to-start-displaying-the-data-immediately/12047)) |
| Calibration | A named state defined by data sufficiency, a finite threshold in units the user controls, often a staged ramp | Garmin "No status… insufficient data"; Tractive "Learning" and "Pending"; Natural Cycles' Brown "needs more data" days ([NC](https://help.naturalcycles.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003313193-How-long-will-it-take-for-the-algorithm-to-get-to-know-my-cycle)); Bearable's "at least three entries with and without a Factor" ([Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/troubleshooting/my-correlations-are-wrong-dont-make-sense/)) |

| Stale or lapsed | Name the reason for the gap, keep any partial data, and raise uncertainty rather than carrying the last verdict forward | Oura puts an insight on the Readiness card "explaining why your sleep data is missing" ([Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/43396540667283-Troubleshooting-Gaps-in-Sleep-Data)); Flo widens the fertile window for irregular use ([Flo](https://flo.health/flo-accuracy)); Welltory hides charts after 12 days without data ([Welltory](https://help.welltory.com/en/articles/3377373-faq-about-data-sections)) |

Three details carry over to Culprit. First, the thresholds are stated in **concrete units the user controls**: "7 nights", "three weeks of consistent sleep data", "three entries with and without". None of them says "we're learning about your pet". Second, several products layer a **staged ramp** rather than one cliff. WHOOP calibrates over 4 days, then 2 weeks, then a month ([WHOOP trials](https://ww2.whoop.com/whoop-trials/); [WHOOP How it works](https://www.whoop.in/pages/how-it-works)). Oura uses up to two weeks for averages and about two months for long-term baselines ([Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057791533)). Third, the products that skip the gate and show a score on day one, such as Garmin Body Battery, attract confused forum threads about scores "starting at zero" ([Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation-archive/f/instinct-solar/256387/body-battery)). That argues against any number shaped like a pattern score before Culprit's bar clears.

Culprit cannot borrow one thing. Wearables tolerate empty days during calibration because the data arrives **passively**: the user only has to wear the device. Culprit's logging is active, so a calibration period with nothing on screen costs the owner real effort for no visible return. That asymmetry is why the techniques in the next section matter more for Culprit than for any wearable. A note on method: help centres describe thresholds precisely but almost never quote the drawn day-0 screen. The exact on-screen copy for Oura's, WHOOP's and Apple Vitals' pre-baseline states is unverified.

## Value before data comes from history, structure and the present

The products that feel instant are not producing instant analysis. They supply value from somewhere other than the user's future logs. The research identifies five sources, and they transfer to Culprit to different degrees.

**The record itself is the first value.** Letterboxd's diary is useful from the first film logged, and its yearly stats appear only for a year with at least ten films ([Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/welcome/)). MyFitnessPal's running total changes a meaningful number with every entry because the goal was set at setup ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/track-calories-myfitnesspal)). Symptom-tracker users say the same about health records: Migraine Buddy users value the app mainly as a record for their doctor, and some find the bare logged count "discouraging" ([migraine.com forum](https://migraine.com/forums/buddy-app)). In an IBS study, patients and providers reviewing interactive food-and-symptom visualisations together "helps improve data comprehension and build mutual trust" ([Schroeder et al. 2017](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jfogarty/publications/cscw2017-triggers.pdf)). Owners in an elimination trial otherwise rely on recall ([Tiffany et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380261)). The record is therefore a legitimate near-term payoff, and it needs no algorithm: the vet gets a usable diary at the next visit.

**Feedback at entry raises adherence even when it adds no new insight.** This is the strongest randomised result in the set. In the SMART trial, median adherence to diet self-monitoring over 12 months was **34% on paper, 58% on a PDA and 72% on a PDA with daily tailored feedback**, and the effect of feedback on weight loss "was fully explained by participants' adherence to self-monitoring" ([Wang et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3333811)). Epstein's participants described the act of logging itself as the point ("it's just about entering it in that moment") ([Epstein et al. 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Epstein2015.pdf)). The evidence supports giving the owner something back at every entry. It does not support the idea that the engine's eventual insight is what keeps people logging.

**Memory backfill shortens the cold start.** Flo and Clue ask at onboarding for the last period dates and treat the answer as a prior that logging will refine ([Flo](https://help.flo.health/hc/en-us/articles/360015106232-The-app-doesn-t-reflect-the-cycle-duration-I-ve-specified)). Finance apps import whole histories: Copilot by bank link, Monarch by CSV, which "cannot be undone" ([Monarch](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/41843105489300-Importing-Balance-and-Transaction-History)). Culprit has no data feed, but the owner arrives with memory and paperwork: when the vomiting started, roughly how often it happened last week, the food before the trial. *Inference:* a few structured questions at setup can give the engine a "before" period and give the strip something on day zero. Recalled data is weaker evidence than contemporaneous logs, so backfilled entries must be marked as owner-recalled, kept editable, and shown distinctly on the vet report. Monarch's irreversible import shows what goes wrong when backfill silently mixes with the real record.

**Present-tense value needs no history at all.** YNAB's first value is a decision about money the user holds now, not an insight about the past ([YNAB](https://www.ynab.com/frequently-asked-budgeting-questions)). *Inference:* Culprit's equivalent is the trial's own structure, meaning what day of the trial it is, what the pet should be eating, and when the vet expects a recheck, plus the always-available "Worried? When to call the vet" route. None of these needs a single log to be useful.

**Population priors give an answer with no personal data, but only in the cautious direction.** Tractive's scratch monitoring compares against "data from millions of other dogs" on day one ([Tractive](https://help.tractive.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011024119-What-health-features-does-Tractive-track)), and Strava's per-session score is meaningful before any personal history exists ([Strava](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000197364-Relative-Effort)). Priors are often wrong for the individual: in ~1.58M Flo users, only **16.32% had a 28-day median cycle** ([Grieger & Norman 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7381001/)). The safety-critical products resolve that uncertainty toward caution. *Inference:* a labelled clinical rule of thumb ("vomiting more than X times in a day is worth a call") is a legitimate day-one answer. "Most dogs on this diet are fine by week three" is not, because it reassures about this pet from population data.

**Sample data works only when segregated.** Mixpanel puts demo datasets in a separate project ([Mixpanel](https://docs.mixpanel.com/troubleshooting/general)). Vendors that mix demo rows into real workspaces need tagging, banners and "clear sample only" dialogs to undo the confusion ([KidKare](https://knowledge.kidkare.com/independent-center/clear-sample-data); [MangoApps](https://www.mangoapps.com/help/admin_sample_data)). In a pet-health record, a sample finding that could be read as real, or that could reach a vet report, is a clinical risk, not a cosmetic one. This is the least transferable technique for Culprit.

## Silence reads as an all-clear unless the screen says otherwise

This is the core health-specific risk, and the evidence on it is stronger than the evidence on empty-state aesthetics. Patients assume "no news is good news". Casalino et al. found **7.1% of abnormal test results were apparently not communicated** across 23 practices, and one recommended remedy was to communicate normal results too and tell patients to call if they hear nothing ([Weill Cornell VIVO](https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid19546413); [MDedge](https://www.mdedge.com/content/literature-25/page/0/2)). Automation-bias research shows that people make **omission errors** when an aid stays silent, that this happens to novices and experts alike, and that it "cannot be prevented by training or instructions" ([Parasuraman & Manzey 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055)). The most direct analogue to Culprit is TummyTrials. Most participants got "no evidence" results, and "many treated 'no evidence' as proof the food was safe" even though the design could only detect triggers ([Karkar et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5432136)). The risk peaks at the moment a long wait ends without a finding.

Two shipped pet products show the failure mode in their own copy. Tractive's resting heart-rate status pairs a blue smiley face with "No concerns detected". Its separation-anxiety monitor says "**No news means good news.** If you don't receive an update… we've not detected any significant changes" ([Tractive RHR](https://help.tractive.com/hc/en-us/articles/25432430590226-What-is-Resting-Heart-Rate-monitoring); [Tractive SAM](https://help.tractive.com/hc/en-us/articles/20205586806290-Separation-Anxiety-Monitoring-How-To-Guide)). Both violate Culprit's invariants. Tractive's persistence guidance is the better model: "If you see consistent changes for 2–3 days or more, consider contacting your vet." Clue offers a second warning. It infers that a period has ended after "1 complete day without this bleeding being tracked" ([Clue](https://support.helloclue.com/hc/en-us/articles/28389082270877-How-can-I-tell-the-app-that-my-period-has-ended)), so a missing log becomes a negative observation. Culprit's logging is active and owner-dependent, so a day with no vomit logged cannot be read as a day without vomiting.

Natural Cycles is the positive model. Its default is the cautious state, Red "Use protection". It gives the reassuring Green "Not fertile" only "if it has enough data to do so", and in Plan Pregnancy mode it adds a third, explicit Brown "needs more data" state so it never has to choose between a false reassurance and a false alarm ([NC](https://help.naturalcycles.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003313193-How-long-will-it-take-for-the-algorithm-to-get-to-know-my-cycle)). It even admits the cost of that caution, conceding that more Red Days "may reduce your satisfaction with the app". Correlation trackers apply the same logic on the data side. Bearable needs days with *and* without a factor ([Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/troubleshooting/my-correlations-are-wrong-dont-make-sense/)), and Cara Care tells users to log even when no symptoms occur ([Cara Care](https://cara-care.zammad.com/help/en-us/5-tracking)). *Inference:* Culprit's one-tap daily "look" is the affirmative "without" sample that makes a no-event day count. Silence is not a "without" sample. The look records the owner's observation, not the app's verdict, so it must never render as an all-clear on its own.

Visualisation research says how to draw this. Song and Szafir found that "visualizations that highlight missing values have higher perceived data quality while those that break visual continuity decrease these perceptions and can bias interpretation" ([CU VisuaLab](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/MissingData)). Practitioner guidance agrees: never bridge real gaps, and mark each measured point so isolated readings stay visible ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/patchy-data); [Bocoup](https://bocoup.com/blog/showing-missing-data-in-line-charts)). The implication is that **no data, zero and normal are three different states, and each needs its own mark**. "Not logged" is a deliberate empty slot, never a zero bar and never a bridged line. "Looked fine" is an affirmative mark. An event is a filled mark.

Stating limits honestly does not cost trust. In five experiments with about 5,780 people, van der Bles et al. found that communicating uncertainty lowered confidence in the *number* but not trust in the *source*, especially when the uncertainty was given as a numeric range rather than a verbal caveat ([Winton Centre](https://wintoncentre.maths.cam.ac.uk/news/being-upfront-about-your-uncertainty-doesnt-necessarily-mean-you-lose-your-audiences-trust-new-paper-published)). Daylio shows results with Low, Medium or High confidence labels rather than hiding weak ones ([Daylio](https://daylio.net/faq/?p=5703)). *Inference:* this resolves the apparent tension between the PM's low-copy Home and Culprit's honesty rule. A number ("9 of 56 days", "4 days with meals logged") is both shorter and more trusted than a paragraph of caveats, and the encoding of the strip does most of the honesty work without words. There is one caution in the other direction. Wearable users can develop preoccupation and anxiety around their data: 20% of AF patients using wearables always contacted their doctor after irregular-rhythm notifications ([UNC Health on Rosman et al. 2024](https://news.unchealthcare.org/2024/07/wearable-devices-can-increase-health-anxiety-could-they-adversely-affect-health/)), and "orthosomnia" describes sleep-tracker users who worsened from worry about their data ([Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-disorders/orthosomnia)). A Home that turns every gap into an alarm is a failure too. The "When to call the vet" line works because it gives a worried owner a concrete action rather than an open monitoring loop.

## Ghost charts are untested; a strip of real days is defensible

The evidence on placeholders is thin and mostly off-target. NN/g defines skeleton screens as a **loading** pattern "used exclusively for full-page loads" with waits under 10 seconds, and warns against ever showing a "no records" message while content is still loading ([NN/g skeletons](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/); [NN/g empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)). Even within that tested context the results conflict. Viget's n=136 test found skeletons produced the *longest* perceived wait ([Viget](https://viget.com/articles/a-bone-to-pick-with-skeleton-screens)), while a small n=21 study found the opposite ([SBC/IHC](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/ihc_estendido/article/view/37767)). **No study found tests a ghost of a future chart as a persistent, days-long insufficient-data state, or measures how often users mistake placeholders for real data.** IBM Carbon's rule that illustrations "should not carry informational content" ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/)) puts a ghost chart in an awkward category. If it looks like data, it is no longer decoration.

*Inference:* the risk of a ghost Signal chart is asymmetric in health. A grey trend line that looks flat or gently varying, shown for days, can be read as "loading", "broken" or, worst, "stable". When the wait ends without a pattern, the TummyTrials result predicts that owners will read the empty outcome as safety. The mock's ghost is therefore its weakest element. If it stays, it needs no plausible values (no y-axis numbers, no realistic curve), a visual treatment unlike real data ink (an outline or hatching), and a label saying what it is. A safer replacement is structural rather than speculative: the real trial timeline with today marked and the end date labelled, carrying no invented points.

The **record strip of the pet's recent days with every log marked** is the best-supported element of the current mock. Every mark on it is true. It follows the guidance on highlighting missing values instead of hiding them, it is the "data-shaped empty state" that sits between a blank page and fake data, and it makes Rooksby's same-day use possible from the first log. It needs the three-state encoding to be honest: not logged, looked fine, event. Collapsing "not logged" into "nothing happened" would repeat Clue's error.

**Progress toward a baseline** ("4 of 7 days") has industry precedent but no outcome evidence. The precedents include Fitbit's former countdown tile, Garmin's 3 weeks and Apple's 7 nights, and no study of their effect on comprehension or retention was found. *Inference:* a progress display differs from a streak because it has an end, measures how much data exists rather than how virtuous the user has been, and a missed day delays it instead of resetting it. That avoids the broken-streak mechanism described in the next section. Culprit has a hazard no wearable has, though. A wearable's calibration always ends in a score. **Culprit's bar may clear and still show nothing, because there may be no pattern.** A bar labelled "until your first Signal" promises a Signal and turns its absence into an implied all-clear. Progress should point at something guaranteed: days of the trial covered, or days the vet report will include. It should not point at a finding the engine may never make.

The design systems also support the PM's low-copy instinct. Pajamas limits empty-state titles to about five words ([Pajamas](https://design.gitlab.com/patterns/empty-states)). Atlassian makes the header the only required element ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/components/empty-state/usage)). Salesforce keeps body lines under 66 characters and allows at most two calls to action ([SLDS](https://v1.lightningdesignsystem.com/guidelines/empty-state/)). Carbon and Polaris call for one primary action ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/); [Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/components/empty-state)). Polaris adds that copy should never make the user feel unsuccessful. Notably, **none of these systems names an "insufficient data" state**, where some data exists but not enough for the derived view. Culprit has to design that state itself, and no published guidance covers it. Material Design's and Apple's HIG empty-state pages could not be retrieved, so their rules are not reported here.

## Streaks punish the missed day a diet-trial owner most needs to forgive

The gamification evidence argues against Culprit using it. A meta-analysis of 16 RCTs found that gamification raised physical activity by **g=0.42 during the intervention, falling to g=0.15 about 14 weeks after it ended**, and only g=0.23 against active non-gamified controls ([Mazeas et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/9754f1fd9dd84266b5e5a30336858ad8)). In the STEP UP trial, activity fell in every arm once the game ended ([Penn Medicine](https://pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2019/september/using-a-wearable-device-to-exercise-more-add-competition-to-improve-your-results)). On streaks specifically, Silverman and Barasch's seven studies show that intact streaks raise intent to continue "regardless of how much the person actually did". **Broken streaks reduce continuation, most of all when the break is self-attributed**, and the ability to repair a streak softens the drop ([JCR 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). Barasch adds that messages highlighting a recent failure "typically don't work" ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)). Duolingo, the streak's strongest advocate, reports small lifts (+1.7% day-7 retention from a milestone animation, +0.38% daily actives from Streak Freeze). It concedes that breaking a streak "can… actually feel quite demotivating" and that it deliberately uses loss aversion ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)).

Two further findings matter for a caregiving product. Etkin showed across six experiments that measuring an activity increases how much people do but **lowers enjoyment and later continuation**, because it makes the activity feel like work ([Wharton PDF](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Etkin-Jordan-11-12-15-Hidden-Cost.pdf)). Among people who lapsed from Fitbit, about half reported guilt. Nudges helped those who felt guilty but felt "judgmental" to those who considered themselves done ([UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/2016/09/08/life-after-fitbit-appealing-to-those-who-feel-guilty-vs-free/)). Epstein's work adds that on return, "seeing historical data (and potential failures surfaced) may be demotivating" ([Epstein et al. 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Epstein2015.pdf)).

*Inference:* in reactive pet tracking, the dog's condition sets the need to log, not a habit goal. A streak turns a missed day into the owner's failure, and that is the self-blamed break which drives the largest drop-off. The elimination-diet data show the clinically valuable entry is often the uncomfortable one, such as a treat, a scavenged item or a slip-up, with 75% of owners reporting indiscretions ([Tiffany et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380261)). Cordeiro found food journalers avoided logging things that made them feel guilty ([Cordeiro et al. 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Cordeiro2015.pdf)). Any framing that rewards a clean record discourages the entries the vet most needs. For the one daily nudge, the same evidence points to a **cue at the moment of the event** (meal time, the usual walk). Forgetting is the problem cues solve. A lapse reminder ("you haven't logged in 3 days") is the message type that fails.

## Patterns Culprit should consider, ranked

The ranking weighs the strength of the evidence against how well each pattern fits Culprit's fixed constraints. "Evidence" means a cited study or a shipped product behaviour. "Inference" is this brief's reasoning and has not been tested.

| # | Pattern | Evidence | Inference / untested |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frame the wedge user's tracking as a bounded trial** with a visible duration and endpoint ("Day 9 of 56"), and design a successful end state (report to the vet, "you can ease off now") | Bounded protocols reach high compliance ([TummyTrials](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5432136)); diagnostic tracking ends when answered ([Rooksby](https://pubweb.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Rooksby2014.pdf)); "happy abandonment" ([Epstein 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5428074)) | No study compares bounded and open-ended framing within one app; the retention lift is assumed, not measured |
| 2 | **Keep the record strip as the day-one value**, with three distinct marks: not logged (empty slot), looked fine (affirmative mark), event (filled mark); never bridge or zero-fill | Same-day use ([Rooksby](https://pubweb.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Rooksby2014.pdf)); highlight missing values ([Song & Szafir](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/MissingData)); the absence-as-negative hazard ([Clue](https://support.helloclue.com/hc/en-us/articles/28389082270877-How-can-I-tell-the-app-that-my-period-has-ended)) | Whether owners read three states correctly at a glance needs a device test |
| 3 | **Reflect every log back at once** as a fact about the record ("2nd vomit since 2 Oct", the mark landing on today's slot), never as a judgement | Daily feedback raised adherence from 58% to 72% ([SMART](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3333811)); the moment of entry is the point ([Epstein 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Epstein2015.pdf)) | SMART used tailored weight-loss feedback, so transfer to factual read-backs is assumed |
| 4 | **Keep an escalation route that never depends on how much has been logged** ("Worried? When to call the vet ›"), with copy built on persistence and route-to-vet logic | No news is not good news ([Casalino](https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid19546413)); omission errors ([Parasuraman & Manzey](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055)); Tractive's "2–3 days or more… contact your vet" ([Tractive](https://help.tractive.com/hc/en-us/articles/25432430590226-What-is-Resting-Heart-Rate-monitoring)) | The clinical thresholds need vet sign-off; no study tests this line's effect on anxiety |
| 5 | **Give "not enough yet" its own named state, measured in data sufficiency, with a number in owner units** (days with meals, days with a look), distinct from both concern and steady | Garmin "No status"; NC Brown days ([NC](https://help.naturalcycles.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003313193-How-long-will-it-take-for-the-algorithm-to-get-to-know-my-cycle)); numeric ranges keep trust ([van der Bles](https://wintoncentre.maths.cam.ac.uk/news/being-upfront-about-your-uncertainty-doesnt-necessarily-mean-you-lose-your-audiences-trust-new-paper-published)) | Point progress at guaranteed coverage, never at "your first Signal", since the bar can clear with no pattern |
| 6 | **Make the vet report available from log one** and say so where the owner logs | Owners rely on recall ([Tiffany](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380261)); the doctor record is valued ([Migraine Buddy forum](https://migraine.com/forums/buddy-app)); shared review builds trust ([Schroeder](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jfogarty/publications/cscw2017-triggers.pdf)) | A short report must not read as "nothing found"; that needs the cold-read review |
| 7 | **Short memory backfill at setup** (onset, rough frequency last week, pre-trial food), marked as owner-recalled and kept separate from contemporaneous logs | Flo and Clue onboarding priors ([Flo](https://help.flo.health/hc/en-us/articles/360015106232-The-app-doesn-t-reflect-the-cycle-duration-I-ve-specified)); backfill pays off immediately ([Copilot](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/5542003-historical-transaction-data)) | Completion rates and recall quality are unknown; it must not add decisions at the moment of an event |
| 8 | **Make the daily look the "without" sample**, so a calm day counts as observed evidence and silence never does | With/without symmetry ([Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/troubleshooting/my-correlations-are-wrong-dont-make-sense/); [Cara Care](https://cara-care.zammad.com/help/en-us/5-tracking)) | The look must never render as an all-clear by itself |
| 9 | **Spend the one nudge on a contextual cue** (meal time), never on a lapse reminder; after a gap, offer a clean slate and easy backfill without a "you missed N days" count | Forgetting persists despite lower effort ([DECAF](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jfogarty/publications/chi2015-decaf.pdf)); failure-highlighting messages fail ([Barasch](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)); a clean slate on return ([Epstein 2015](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Epstein2015.pdf)) | The best cue timing for pet owners is untested |

| 11 | **Consider surfacing early concern, labelled as early, while keeping reassurance hard-gated** | Daylio's labelled confidence ([Daylio](https://daylio.net/faq/?p=5703)); NC's cautious default | This is a clinical and product ruling for the PM, not a design detail |
| 12 | **If any ghost or preview stays**, make it structural (the real trial timeline, today marked, end labelled) with no plausible values, a distinct visual treatment and an explicit label | Decoration must not carry information ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/)); sample data is safe only when segregated ([Mixpanel](https://docs.mixpanel.com/troubleshooting/general)) | No evidence either way on persistent ghost charts; the risk that a flat ghost reads as "stable" is inferred |

The anti-patterns follow from the same evidence. Avoid **streaks, consecutive-day counts and badges**, because breaks cause self-blamed drop-off ([Silverman & Barasch](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb)) and gains decay ([Mazeas](https://doaj.org/article/9754f1fd9dd84266b5e5a30336858ad8)). Avoid **any quiet-state copy or icon that celebrates a null**, such as Tractive's smiley "No concerns detected" or "No news means good news", because owners treat no evidence as safety ([TummyTrials](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5432136)). Avoid **treating an unlogged day as an event-free day**, Clue's error. Avoid **a ghost chart with a realistic flat or gently varying line**, because no evidence supports it and the comprehension risk points toward reassurance. Avoid **sample or demo data inside the real pet's profile**, where it could reach the engine or the vet report ([KidKare](https://knowledge.kidkare.com/independent-center/clear-sample-data)). Avoid **a numeric pattern score before the bar clears**, given the confusion around ungated scores ([Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation-archive/f/instinct-solar/256387/body-battery)). Avoid **"we're learning" without units**, because every credible calibration state names a finite number. Avoid **paywalling the threshold-gated layer**: Guava gates auto-correlation behind Premium ([Guava](https://guavahealth.com/health-insights)), which would break Pets > $ if copied.

Several gaps limit confidence in these recommendations. There is **no retention or adherence study of any consumer pet-health app**. There is no study of time to first insight, and no controlled comparison of immediate reflection against delayed insight. There is no evaluation of persistent ghost previews, of progress-to-baseline indicators, or of how users misread placeholders as real data. No published outcome data exists for the calibration displays of Apple, Oura or WHOOP, and their day-0 screen copy is unverified. There is no study of confidence labels in pet-health UI. Strava's and Nike Run Club's first-activity flows were not documented. Every pet-specific recommendation above extrapolates from human self-tracking, clinical communication and wearables. Culprit's own instrumentation is where the evidence will have to come from: day-7 and day-30 logging by trial framing, and owner comprehension of the three-state strip in a device test.

## Conclusion

The PM's framing is right in a way that changes the design question. The problem is not how to make the wait for a Signal tolerable. **The Signal should never be the reason to log.** Two things are, and both pay from the first entry: the record the vet will read and the trial the owner is already committed to. Seen that way, the pattern engine is a bonus layer, as Apple's 180-day Trends sit on top of rings that carry day one, and Culprit's honesty constraints stop being a drag on engagement. The products that handle uncertainty best, Natural Cycles above all, make caution the default and admit its cost, and they still earn trust. Culprit can do the same mostly through encoding rather than copy, which is exactly what a low-copy Home needs.

One risk deserves a design response before launch. Culprit's evidence bar can clear with nothing to show, which is unlike any wearable's calibration. The weeks of logging end in a quiet card, and the evidence predicts owners will read it as "safe". Whatever the Home draws during calibration has to be designed backward from that moment: progress counts coverage rather than an approaching finding, the strip keeps "not logged" distinct from "fine", and the call-the-vet route never moves. Get that moment right and the first day takes care of itself.
