## 1. Executive summary — the eight findings that change the spec

**① §5.3's detection path is structurally blind to the contaminant class that clinical sources name most consistently: flavoured oral medications.** Every guideline-level source consulted — MSD, Tufts, CAVD, NWVDS, Bridgeport, Whole Dog Journal — independently names chewable heartworm/flea preventives, flavoured antibiotics, chewable joint supplements, gelatin capsules and flavoured toothpaste as trial-invalidating `[E]`; Nyx logs all of them as `medication` events (`constants/eventTypes.ts:31` routes to MedicationPicker with `hasFood: false`) and `grep -ri "flavo[u]r" supabase/migrations/` returns **zero hits** `[E]`. **Consequence:** §5.2 would render "No off-diet foods logged" with a beef-flavoured chewable sitting logged in the same window — the never-reassure invariant failing on the single most predictable case. This must be ruled before PR 5 and may touch PR 1's schema.

**② The sentence "No off-diet foods logged" cannot be made true at any coverage level, so G2 is asking the wrong question.** 25% of dogs in a 93-owner trial survey had access to unmonitored food sources — litter boxes, hunted prey, garbage `[E]` — and the medication channel (①) is invisible by construction. G2 as written ("below what coverage does §5.2 suppress the claim?") implies that *above* the floor the claim becomes sayable. **Consequence:** reframe G2 from a threshold to a rule — Nyx renders a positive-form description of the *record*, never a negative claim about the *world*. No mature human-health elimination app writes that sentence either (§3).

**③ GI = 28 days is a defensible *assessment* point and a wrong *trial length* — and §4.3's milestone copy would tell a GI owner to stop a diet their vet wanted continued for three months.** The ACVIM-endorsed 2026 consensus on canine chronic inflammatory enteropathy says response typically appears at 10–14 days, but the diet should continue "for at least 12 weeks before attempting to transition away," and PLE dogs stay on it long-term `[E]`. **Consequence:** `target_duration_days` conflates two clinically distinct milestones. Either split the concept or rewrite the milestone so it never reads as permission to stop.

**④ Day 1 is not the day the vet handed over the bag — it is the first day of exclusive feeding, after a ≥1-week transition, and the spec's "defaults today" wording gets it wrong for the canonical wedge user.** CAVD's client handout: "Take at least one week (or longer) to make the transition… **Start the 8-week countdown on the first day you feed only the elimination diet**" `[E]`. **Consequence:** two compounding failures — a completion milestone firing up to two weeks early, *and* 7–10 spurious off-diet exposures in week 1 from the transition's old-diet meals, arriving exactly when the owner is deciding whether to trust the flag.

**⑤ The profile card's progress bar is already driven by the mislabeled compliance number, and §12's PR 4 acceptance criterion would pass a card that still lies.** Verified in repo: `app/(tabs)/profile.tsx:764` renders `Day {daysElapsed} of {target_duration_days}`, `:766–772` renders a bar whose width is `${Math.min(100, dietTrial.compliance)}%`, `:775` renders the string `[E]`. The AC is "the string 'compliance' appears nowhere on the card" — delete the string, keep the bar, ship green. (The same pattern exists on the medication regimen card at `:716–721`.) **Consequence:** amend the AC to ban the blended metric *in any form*, including bar width, and add the bar to §1.3.

**⑥ §7's trial block has no concurrent-medication line, which makes a derm trial uninterpretable — and this is the one omission a specialist will notice first.** The standard shortened protocol deliberately co-administers prednisolone (2–3wk) or oclacitinib (3wk) during the elimination phase, with sensitivity 95% vs 63% and 100% specificity for both `[E]`; AAHA warns lokivetmab's long action forces the trial past 60 days `[E]`; Zoetis's own recheck checklist demands "Confirm the dog has not had Apoquel for 7 days prior to this recheck" `[E]`. Nyx already holds `medications` with dates and already renders dashed intervention verticals (`render.ts:1204`). **Consequence:** pull the drug overlap *into* the trial block — it is render work, not schema work, and it is what makes `vet-report-cold-read` return CLINIC-READY.

**⑦ D6's four-way outcome has nothing to be a change *from*, and the field has two owner-assessed instruments purpose-built for exactly this comparison.** COSCAD'18 — the consensus core outcome set for canine AD trials — agreed PVAS10 (owner-assessed 0–10, validated) and states plainly that OGATE, the global-efficacy item D6 resembles, "**is not a validated instrument**" `[E]`. Meanwhile `itch` is logged with `hasSeverity: false` `[E]`, so for the 8–12-week skin trial that *is* the wedge, the report can render itch **counts** and nothing else. **Consequence:** capture one owner-scored severity value at trial start and at completion. Owner-scored at both ends, so §6.1 is untouched.

**⑧ Nobody ships this. The real incumbent is a paper grid — but the diet manufacturer's handout asks the owner to record nothing at all, while the *clinic's* handout often ships a full weekly diary.** No consumer pet app surveyed carries a trial object with a start date, target duration and end `[E]`; the only structured elimination-diet-with-challenge app in any species is human (Monash FODMAP, $7.99) `[E]`; Royal Canin discontinued its one digital owner platform in the US on 2025-11-01 `[E]`. **Consequence:** B-417 faces zero feature-parity pressure and inherits zero user habits. The §0.4 mocks must be tested against an owner who has never tracked a trial in any tool — this is a first-run problem, not a differentiation problem.

## 2. Does anyone ship this?

**Verdict: no consumer pet app ships a diet-trial object.** Across two independent lanes we found generic meal/symptom logging, allergy fields in medical history, and ingredient scanners — but no start date + target duration + allowed set + end + outcome. The nearest thing markets an elimination-diet log and, on inspection, has no trial construct behind the copy.

Two honesty caveats on this negative, both from verification:
- The sweep did not survey the direct itch/allergy cluster (**ItchyPet**, **HotSpotter**, **Pet Allergy Log**, Doggo, FitFido) that an adversarial search surfaced. The most threatening of these, ItchyPet, was spot-checked: paid subscription, ingredient-level meal + itch logging, AI pattern-finding, no trial object, elimination diets mentioned only as context in its FAQ `[E]` (https://itchypet.app). The conclusion survives; state it as **"no app among those surveyed"**, never "no such product exists."
- **allergic.pet explicitly markets the capability** — verbatim: *"However you feed your dog or cat, you can track every ingredient and run an elimination diet"* — while shipping only free-text notes and retrospective correlation (*"See what lines up… Which meals came before the bad days? We'll show you"*) `[E]` (https://allergic.pet/). No named feature with a start date, duration, or allowed-food list exists behind that copy. Free signup, no App Store or Play link on the page; one search summary described it as waitlist-phase. **Marketed, not verified shipped.**

## 3. What the human-health category already solved

**Monash FODMAP is the only shipped, structured elimination-diet-with-challenge app in any species**, and it is a decade of iteration on exactly this problem. Four mechanics are worth stealing, and one non-mechanic is the most important finding in this section.

**The non-mechanic first: the gold standard computes no adherence number at all.** No adherence score, compliance %, or streak is documented in the public materials of Monash, mySymptoms, or Vyla `[E]` — stated as *not documented*, since we could not test in-app. This means D2's deletion of "% compliance" is not a downgrade; it is convergence with the entire mature category, and §13 should say so before a future session asks "but shouldn't we show progress?"

1. **The traffic-light lookup is indexed on serving size, and it never retro-scores what was eaten.** Monash's rating "is based on the serving size information found on a food when it is tested for FODMAPs" `[E]` (https://www.monashfodmap.com/blog/traffic-light-system/). The user consults it *before* eating; the diary — which is deliberately a plain note feature, unlinked to the food guide — passes no verdict at log time `[E]`. **Steal:** the register for §5.3 outcome 3. The card should read as a reference statement about the *food* ("not on this trial's list"), never a verdict about the *feeding*.
2. **mySymptoms renders the claim and its evidence as two adjacent bars, never one number.** Verbatim: "The orange bar represents the score of the item relative to the other items. The green bar represents the confidence of the result… based upon the number of events the item occurs in," with the on-surface disclaimer "**The algorithm used by the app can't identify cause – only correlation**" `[E]`. Notably it documents **no** insufficient-data state — low evidence renders as a short green bar, not a suppressed claim. **Steal:** offer this to Dr. Chen at G2 as the alternative to a suppression floor — co-render coverage permanently alongside the exposure count and let a visibly weak second channel do the work. Also steal the disclaimer *shape*: §6.1's "Nyx never scores the trial" currently has no owner-facing sentence, and this is what one looks like.
3. **Fig ships per-item explainability.** Verbatim: "You can even click on a ingredient to learn why it doesn't match your Fig!" `[E]` (https://foodisgood.com/app/). **Steal:** every off-diet flag must be tappable to its reason (not in the allowed set / derived protein match / no protein data). §6.3 says a flag is "disclosure, not blame," but a flag the owner cannot interrogate is an unfalsifiable accusation, and §12 currently has no check enforcing it.
4. **The owner's reported outcome changes what the app does next.** Monash's three-state per-FODMAP sliders re-colour the entire reference database going forward `[E]` (https://www.monashfodmap.com/blog/interpreting-reintroduction-challenges/). **Steal:** B-417's `outcome` is written at completion, rendered on the report, and then does nothing — which makes PR 6 a survey, not the close of a lifecycle. Give it a downstream job (even a minimal one: an `improved` trial's allowed set becomes a known-tolerated hint for the picker / B-357 shelf), or state explicitly that v1 deliberately does nothing with it.
5. **A challenge is a scheduled child object, not a phase flag.** Monash: the app supplies candidate foods and per-day quantities ("an amber serve of 1.5 tsp for the first day and 2 tsp for the second day"), one FODMAP subgroup at a time, over ~3 days, against a low-FODMAP background; the whole reintroduction runs 6–8 weeks with washouts `[E]` (https://www.monashfodmap.com/blog/reintroduction-using-diary-function/). VCA's canine equivalent: one ingredient at a time, watch for four weeks `[E]`. **Implication for D8:** `phase` reserves a lifecycle state, not a data model. Say so in §9 so a future session doesn't believe the space was held.

**Copy patterns worth stealing:**
- **ZOE labels its bands by action, not colour-moral:** "Enjoy occasionally," "Enjoy in moderation" `[E]`. Name the food and the fact; never a grade, a colour word, or a count-as-score.
- **The Celiac Disease Foundation's slip register:** "Accidental gluten-exposure is NOT a failure. When you have celiac disease, getting glutened can be scary, painful, and completely overwhelming" `[E]` — reframed as something that happens even when you're doing everything right. (Sourced from a CDF Facebook post, not a CDF web page — cite as such.)

**Copy patterns worth avoiding:**
- **Whole30's "back to Day 1"** on any slip — "No slips, no cheats, no special occasions" `[E]` (https://whole30.com/article/start-over/). It is the canonical anti-pattern *and* the strictest program in the category still refuses to enforce it: "no one is monitoring your Whole30 Day by Day to make sure you check the right box… **it's always up to you**" `[E]`. That final move is worth stealing; the restart rule is not. (The founder's concession that elimination-diet restriction "can actually create disordered eating habits where there were none before" is real but comes from a Katie Couric Media interview, not the Whole30 article. A Cleveland Clinic attribution circulating for the same point could not be verified and is **not** used here.)
- **Renaming the moral load instead of removing it.** Noom relabelled its "red" food category to orange in 2022 because the colour "purported a negative connotation" `[E]`. Renaming "% compliance" to "% adherence" or "trial score" would be the same move. D2's deletion must be a deletion.
- **Red/green per-log colour verdicts.** Eikey 2021 (BJPsych Open 7(5) e176) documents participants feeling "guilt, embarrassment and shame" at red visualisations after exceeding a budget, and ties streaks to "obsession with logging" and app dependency `[E]` (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/effects-of-diet-and-fitness-apps-on-eating-disorder-behaviours-qualitative-study/2D1EE739D97AB3EFC6573835E4C527BD). Nyx's owner is not the patient, so the transferable harm is narrower but sharper: **red feedback on today's log makes tomorrow's log less likely, and coverage is the denominator §5.2 depends on.**

## 4. The clinical protocol as a requirements checklist

### 4.1 Duration — verdict on G3

**Skin = 56 days: ratify, and stop calling it "the low end."** §0.3 characterises the defaults as the low end of the research ranges. That is wrong in a useful direction for skin. Olivry, Mueller & Prélaud's critically appraised topic (209 dogs, 40 cats) recommends verbatim: "To diagnose CAFR in at least 80% of dogs and cats, a restrictive (elimination) dietary trial should last a minimum of 5 weeks in dogs and 6 weeks in cats. **Increasing the duration of the restrictive diet to 8 weeks will increase the sensitivity of diagnosis to more than 90%** of cases in both species" `[E]` (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4551374/). AAHA 2023 concurs: 4–12 weeks, ">90% of food-allergic dogs" at 8 weeks, "a small subset… may require 12 weeks" `[E]`. CAVD, to owners: "A diet trial lasting 8 weeks will diagnose about 95% of food allergic dogs… a trial lasting 4 weeks will diagnose only about half" `[E]`. 56d **is** the >90% band.

**GI = 28 days: reframe the question.** Purina puts GI improvement at 1–4 weeks; the ACVIM 2026 consensus says complete dietary trials entail "exclusive feeding of a therapeutic diet… for at least 2 weeks," with responses "typically seen within 10-14 days," but the diet continued "for at least 12 weeks before attempting to transition away," and PLE dogs "long-term" `[E]` (https://academic.oup.com/jvim/article/40/1/aalaf017/8429723). So 28d is a fine *assessment* point and a wrong *trial length*. G3's real question is not "is 28 the right number" but "what does the number mean, and does the milestone read as permission to stop?"

**Add species to the key.** Cats reach ~50% cumulative remission at 4 weeks and ~80% at 6 (vs dogs >85% at 5) `[E]`, and AAHA states 90% of food-allergic cats resolve by 8 weeks vs 50% at 4 `[E]`. A cat on the 28-day GI default sits near the 50% point. `pets.species` already exists — this is a lookup-table change, not a schema change. Note the feline evidence base is 40 cats across 3 studies vs 209 dogs across 5, which raises the value of a prominent `Extend`.

**Extend should not re-prompt a blank number.** 12 weeks is the documented next step for the skin subset unresolved at 8 `[E]`. Make Extend a named option, not a blank field, and give it equal visual weight to `Complete trial` — at day 56, 5–10% of true CAFR patients have not yet remitted.

**What the food-centric allowed-set model structurally cannot represent — stated explicitly:**

- **Items 1, 4, 5, 6 (the oral-product lane).** This is the one the evidence supports most strongly and the one the spec must rule on. CAVD states the stakes bluntly: "the flavouring in a single monthly heartworm pill is enough to set off an allergy" `[E]`. Bridgeport names the drug classes and the substitutions: "If your pet receives a flavoured medication (chewable heartworm/flea pills; flavoured antibiotics; flavoured vitamin, mineral, joint or coat supplement), please have those changed to non-flavoured form" — and separately, "Do not use bread, cheese, Pill Pockets, sausages or meat to disguise oral medications" `[E]`. Tufts: "If you don't also control all of these other things, you can **completely invalidate** the elimination diet trial" `[E]`. Nyx's `medication_items` (migration 020) carries generic_name/brand_name/strength/form/route/is_prescription/is_critical and nothing about flavouring `[E]`.
- **Items 8–10 (the unobservable channels).** No app can see these. They belong in a **one-time teaching moment at setup and a named blind-spot line on the vet report** — not in a detector, and never as recurring alarms.
- **Item 12 (label truth).** Both arms of §5.3 are label-dependent, and the label is frequently wrong. Olivry & Mueller CAT(5): mislabeling in 0–83% (median 45%) of tested diets, rising to "between 33 and 83% in pet foods with 'novel/limited' ingredients proposed for elimination diets" `[E]` (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29357847/). Ricci et al. 2018: 6/11 (54.5%) limited-antigen wet diets contained undeclared animal protein; vegetarian and hydrolyzed products tested clean `[E]` (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30208880/). Kępińska-Pacelik 2023 (primary source): "65% of the dog foods contained undeclared chicken DNA, while 41% – undeclared pork DNA" across 29 OTC dry foods, affecting even mono-protein and hypoallergenic-marketed products `[E]`. **This is the strongest independent argument FOR D3**: an explicit allowed set is a record of *intent* that survives a wrong ingredient panel; derived-protein detection inherits the panel's errors directly.

### 4.3 Dose and lag — two numbers that constrain the render

**Dose is not binary.** A prospective double-blinded threshold study (11 dogs, 71 challenges) found mean eliciting dose **20.8 g** of protein (range 1–30 g; median ~0.86 g/kg), with 60% of reactions at 30 g, 20% at 10 g, 14.3% at 5 g and **5.7% at 1 g** `[E]` (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2026.1767167/full). **Two uses and one prohibition:** it licenses "the trial keeps running" as clinically honest rather than a comfort lie; it argues exposures must be itemized with amount where the meal log has it; and it **forbids quantifying the reassurance** — the same authors state "the threshold for such cross-contact-induced reactions remains unknown." Never write "a small treat probably won't matter."

**Lag is days, not hours.** Same study: mean time to flare **4.1 days**, with 82.9% of reactions falling on days 2–6 `[E]`. Olivry & Mueller CAT(9) on oral food challenge: dogs TTF50 5d / TTF80 7d / **TTF90 14d**; cats TTF50 4d / TTF80 and TTF90 both **7d** — "clinicians performing an OFC need to wait for 14 and 7 days for more than 90% of dogs and cats… to have a flare" `[E]` (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7247231/). **Hard consequence:** any exposure↔symptom juxtaposition uses a **1–14 day forward window**, species-dependent, never same-day, never "X hours after," and never a nearest-preceding-meal join. This repo shipped that exact attribution bug once under three ceremonial sign-offs (CLAUDE.md DoD, 2026-05-30); it must be a named counterexample for PR 5's mandatory adversarial pass.

### 4.4 Concurrent therapy is protocol, not violation

MSD: "Antipruritic medications are permitted throughout the trial," and a 2–3 week prednisolone course at anti-inflammatory doses can shorten the trial "to as few as 4 weeks," with a 2-week withdrawal ideally before challenge `[E]`. Fischer et al. 2021 (58 dogs): prednisolone protocol 95% sensitivity, oclacitinib 63%, both 100% specific `[E]`. AAHA on lokivetmab: the long-acting injection means "the diet trial must be extended more than 60 days… The exact length of time a diet trial must be extended is not known," and the task force "does not recommend using lokivetmab during diet trials unless absolutely necessary" `[E]`. **An app that flagged steroids as a compliance violation would be scolding an owner for following their vet's actual instructions.** Render the overlap; judge nothing.

**GI carries an asymmetry worth encoding:** ACVIM 2026 states "Empirical antimicrobial treatment is not recommended in CIE suspects" because "relapse rates after discontinuation are high and significant intestinal dysbiosis can remain long-term" `[E]`. A steroid's effect withdraws; a course of metronidazole's effect on the microbiome does not. An antibiotic course inside a GI-trial window is the single most report-worthy item on a confounder line.

### 4.5 The four escalations a specialty practice actually asks for

NWVDS (Nov 2025), verbatim, instruction 9 — "Please contact us and ask us for instructions if:"
> — The change of diet causes vomiting or diarrhea. — Your pet refuses to eat the new diet. — Your pet goes more than 3 days without a bowel movement. — Your pet appears to lose weight. `[E]`

§6.5 covers refusal only. Nyx can compute all four from data it already holds (`vomit`/`diarrhea` events, `detectIntakeDecline`, absence of stool events, `weight_check`/B-186). **The >3-days-no-stool case is the important one for G2's ruling text: it is an *absence-based escalation*, the exact mirror of §5.2 — which proves the coverage denominator gates alarms as well as reassurances.** At 40% coverage, "no stool for three days" is unknowable, not alarming. G2 should be written as a two-sided rule.

Separately, CAVD's cat handout escalates refusal outright: "cats can develop serious liver issues if they go without food for 1-2 days so contact your veterinarian immediately if your cat refuses the food" `[E]`. Merck names among hepatic-lipidosis triggers "forced weight loss with **unacceptable food substitutions**" `[E]` — adjacent to, though not identical to, a palatability refusal of a novel/hydrolyzed trial diet. Merck gives no day count, and the commonly quoted 3–4 day figure could not be verified this round; note that Nyx's own `analytics.ts` already encodes a **feline 48h hepatic-lipidosis window** in `detectIntakeDecline` `[E]`. The right build is *don't shadow the existing lane*, with the exact clock left to Dr. Chen.

## 5. Adherence: what closes the 25%→completion gap

**First, a correction to the premise.** §1.4's "20–30% adherence" figure could not be traced to any published veterinary source in two independent lanes `[E — negative]`. What exists: AAHA-derived ~64% general treatment adherence; therapeutic-diet compliance reported at 19% of dogs / 18% of cats vs 87% for vaccines (dvm360 — **403 on fetch, unverified**); and consistent qualitative statements from Jackson 2024, dermavet 2026 and AAHA ("a properly performed diet trial is difficult to conduct and client compliance can be challenging" `[E]`). The **argument** survives intact on qualitative evidence; the **number** should be re-sourced or downgraded before it appears in a clinical spec or investor-adjacent material.

**A credible external anchor exists.** Monash's own real-world data: 11,689 users tracked through FODMAP restriction (48% improved), 2,053 completed at least one food challenge `[E]` (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/fodmapspecific-mobile-application-impact-on-gut-symptoms-in-11689-people-and-dietary-triggers-in-2053-people/AD9C790FE51FAEF304C5109086FFE38C). The ~18% funnel is a *derivation*, not a stated result, and 11,689 is a symptom-severity-filtered count — so present it as **corroborating direction**, not a matched completion rate. It kills the assumption that better tooling alone lifts completion, which means PR 6's copy is doing the real work, not the detection engine.

### (a) Compatible with every ratified constraint — build these

### (b) Blocked by a ratified decision — do not propose

- **Push reminders / scheduled confirmations** — blocked on the push-provider open question **and** the live Principle-4 carve-out debate (B-288). Worth recording the effect size so a future session does not treat resolving push as the adherence unlock: electronic reminders pool at **d=0.29 (95% CI 0.18–0.41)**, described as "significant, yet small" `[E]` (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25147178/) — the *smallest* credible mechanic in this lane. (Compare cautiously: different outcomes, different literatures.)
- **Any paid or premium trial-adherence support** — Principle 7 forbids it independently, and the evidence would not justify it anyway: financial incentives moved medication adherence by **d=0.03** on percent-adherent, "statistically significant but possibly clinically irrelevant" `[E]` (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11868475/). Record it in §9 so it cannot resurface in a Track-3 session.
- **Streaks, freezes, perfect-week badges, owner scores** — barred by D2's "never blend" and by Principle 4's register. The specific reason is worth writing down: **on a diet trial the streak break and the real clinical slip fall on the same day**, so a streak stacks the abandonment mechanism onto the event most likely to end the trial. *(Note: circulating Duolingo streak-freeze retention statistics were checked and do not support this — see §9. The rule stands on its own logic.)*
- **Household/caregiver shared write** — §9 defers to B-292/B-040. The high-value piece is a **copy line**, not a feature.

### (c) Requires reopening a ratified decision — flag to the PM

- **A weekly owner-scored severity rating for skin trials.** This is a scope addition against D1's "guided trial mode is out," so it must be taken as an amendment, not silently. The argument: it is one slider a week, not a wizard; the field's own cadence is Zoetis's — "**WEEK 1:** For the first week, rate your dog's itching daily… **WEEKS 2–12:** Starting in the second week, rate your dog's itch only once a week, on the same day of the week" `[E]` (Zoetis UK Cytopoint owner brochure PDF). Configure it on the card after creation, never in the start modal.
- **A mid-trial lifecycle moment (D6).** D6 as written places exactly one moment, at day N = target — after the abandonment window has closed. Adding a weeks-3–6 state is an *addition* to D6, not a change to it, but it should be ruled rather than assumed.
- **A pause state (`trial_status`).** Today the enum is active/completed/abandoned. A vet saying "hold the trial, she's on antibiotics" has only one place to land: abandoned — which destroys a trial that will resume. Oura's Rest Mode is the shape: activity score and goal are *disabled* rather than failed, and "during each day on Rest Mode, a notification card will appear asking if selected tags are still applicable" `[E]` (https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057065433-Rest-Mode). Paused days leave the coverage denominator.

## 6. The vet-side artifact — §7 evaluated

**The recheck decision on trial validity is binary, not graded.** Zoetis AU's veterinarian guide (with Dr Dani Hoolahan, DACVD) prints literal 4-week and 8-week recheck checklists, both containing verbatim: "**Ensure the owner has not fed any foods other than the hypoallergenic diet. If they have then the food allergy trial will need to be restarted.**" The other items: obtain an itch score out of 10; physical exam to rule out infection or flea/mite infestation "which will interfere with assessing the results"; and at 8 weeks, "Confirm the dog has not had Apoquel for 7 days prior to this recheck." Interpretation is then binary — "If no itch" / "If the dog is still itchy" — with a response curve of 50%/85%/95% improved at 3/6/8 weeks `[E]` (https://www2.zoetis.com.au/content/en/pages/Campaign/Asset/Source-of-Comfort/Source-of-Comfort-Veterinarians-Guide-to-Food-Elimination-Trials.pdf).

**So §7's job is not to produce a better number than "% compliance." It is to produce an auditable record the vet can accept or reject.** And the value proposition is quantified: at a veterinary teaching hospital, diet was inquired about for 82% of GI-service dogs but only 53% of owners could name the specific diet; treats were documented in **10%** of GI and **8%** of internal-medicine consults `[E]` (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8909024/). The bar §7 must clear is not "perfect" — it is "better than a 90-second verbal recall that captures treats 8% of the time." **That sentence belongs on the artifact**, as one provenance line.

### What a specialist checks, versus what §7 renders

| What the specialist checks | Source | §7 as specified |
|---|---|---|
| Was the diet genuinely novel given the **full prior diet history**? | TVN mistake #1; Royal Canin ("include treats, medications, preventives and any table food") `[E]` | **Absent** — and Nyx uniquely holds a machine-readable version in its own meal log |
| Was feeding truly **exclusive** — everything by mouth? | universal | Partial — food events only (① the medication lane) |
| Was **duration** adequate for indication and species? | Olivry, AAHA `[E]` | Partial — renders the window, no band context |
| What **concurrent medication** ran, and was it withdrawn before judging? | Zoetis, MSD, AAHA, ACVIM `[E]` | **Absent — the single largest omission** (⑥) |
| Were **secondary infections** controlled? | MSD: they "complicate accurate pruritus evaluation" `[E]` | Absent (out of Nyx's reach; matters as a §6.1 rationale) |
| Was a **provocation challenge** performed? | Purina, MSD, Illinois, dermavet — "provocation is mandatory for diagnosis confirmation" `[E]` | **Absent** — and only 10% of owners ever do it `[E]` |
| Was the **outcome** measured against a baseline? | COSCAD'18, Sofou 2024 `[E]` | Partial — a terminal word, no baseline |
| **Data quality** — is this record interpretable at all? | AAEC: interference makes it "impossible to rule out a food allergy" `[E]` | Absent |

**The vocabulary to steal is "uninterpretable, not negative."** A contaminated trial does not rule anything out; it routes to "repeat it properly." A *failed* framing routes owners toward "food allergy ruled out" — a false negative on a patient who has the disease, which is exactly the harm Dr. Chen names at §1.3. §7 should carry a data-quality statement derived from coverage + exposures + any uncontrolled-access flag — framed strictly as a statement about the **log**, never about the pet or the outcome. That keeps §6.1 intact and gives G2's floor a natural copy home: below the floor the report says "not enough logging to interpret," not "0 exposures."

**Two instruments the report can speak in, without computing anything:**
- **PVAS10** for skin. COSCAD'18 agreed exactly three instruments — CADESI-4/CADLI (investigator), **PVAS10** (owner, 0.0–10.0, validated), and OGATE (owner) — and states "the subcommittee members do recognize, however, that **the OGATE is not a validated instrument**" `[E]` (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6097451/). PVAS traces to Hill et al. 2007 (116 then 166 owners; 98% found it easy and accurate; inter-owner R=0.8, P<0.0001) `[E]` (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17845617/); the feline VAScat is Colombo et al. 2022, Vet Dermatol 33(5):407–413 `[E]`. The threshold vets act on is a *delta*: "at least a 50% reduction in itch" before challenge `[E]`; Sofou 2024's predefined criteria use OGATE good-to-excellent and >100% CADESI-04/PVAS increase on provocation `[E]` (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38425024/).
- **CIBDAI inputs** for GI. Nyx already logs all six components — attitude/activity (`lethargy`), appetite (`intake_rating`), vomiting, stool consistency (Bristol), stool frequency, weight loss (`weight_check`) `[E]`. And a 2025 review of these indices asks for precisely this: "The integration of **machine-readable data, input daily by pet owners through specially designed digital applications**, could improve intra-observer repeatability," while reporting that single-timepoint clinician scoring is unreliable `[E]` (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12587788/). Render the inputs as a per-week grid; **never sum them, never threshold them**. This inverts the reproducibility criticism in Nyx's favour and is the strongest possible expression of §6.1 — so §6 should gain an explicit carve-out sentence, or a future reviewer reads the block as scoring by the back door.

**Two mechanical problems in §7 as written, both repo-verified:**
1. **The report's existing "off-diet exposures" is a treat/human-food heuristic, not an allowed-set computation.** `render.ts:2285` builds the appendix from `snap.provenance.confounders`, bucketed on `format === 'human_food'` and `foodType === 'treat' || format === 'treat'`; `report.ts:2246` filters the same way `[E]`. Under a hydrolyzed trial, a different-brand kibble fed as a **meal** cannot appear as off-diet, while a vet-permitted treat does. §7 describes this as "feeding the existing Appendix B confounder section" — which understates the work by an order of magnitude.
2. **The appendix letters drift between spec and code.** `docs/nyx-vet-report-requirements.md:88` lists "B — Off-diet exposures / C — Diet history"; `render.ts:1899` ships "B — diet history · C — off-diet exposures" `[E]`. B-417 §7 cites the spec's letter, so a builder following it targets the wrong appendix. File the drift back to the vet-report spec as its own correction.

**One design constraint for the cold read:** the only credible route to a competitor shipping a real trial object is vet-side software pushing a prescribed protocol into an owner app — the route Nyx explicitly cannot take (§9). So §7 must be legible to a vet who did **not** prescribe through Nyx and has no account. That belongs in §7 as a stated constraint and in `vet-report-cold-read`'s brief.

## 7. Lifecycle UX patterns worth adopting

**Start.** Progressive disclosure: Medisafe carries a "Days limit" choice "for medications you will take for a limited time; perhaps an antibiotic that you just take for a week or two" `[E]` (https://www.techenhancedlife.com/citizen-research/how-use-medisafe-app) — the finite-course duration is an *option*, not a gate. (Whether it sits behind a "more options" tap could not be verified; treat the navigation detail as unconfirmed.) §4.1 currently asks for six things and §12 allows 30 seconds for it. Recommended: primary screen = trial food + "What's it for" (which sets and *shows* the duration default); everything else behind one disclosure. Note "Also allowed" is the field owners are *least* able to answer at setup — the permitted treat is remembered the first time it flags — so a just-in-time "the vet allows this" path from the exposure surface is worth more than a setup field.

**In progress.** Three-state day vocabulary, borrowed from Way of Life's intentional-skip: green = done, red = missed, **yellow = "I chose not to today, and that's okay"** — "the only app on this list that treats intentional rest as a first-class state, not a failure" `[E]` (one reviewer's 30-day self-test on Medium — cite at that tier). This maps directly onto the coverage/exposure split §5.1 already has: a trial day is *logged-and-on-diet*, *logged-with-an-off-diet-feeding* (a recorded fact, never a break), or *not-logged* (honestly a gap). Writing the taxonomy once means the card copy and the report render derive from it rather than being invented per surface.

Progress framing can flip at the midpoint — Koo & Fishbach's small-area hypothesis: motivation is highest when attention rests on whichever quantity is smaller, so accumulated progress early and remaining progress late `[E]` (https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/04/120416113113.htm). `getDietTrialProgress` already returns both `dayCounter` and `daysRemaining`, so this is a render decision with no new math. Ship as a mock variant, not a locked requirement.

**Mid-protocol edit.** The gap: there is no pause, no restart, no owner-declared exposure. Oura's Rest Mode supplies the pause (§5c). The restart must exist as an owner action and must never be automatic — the clinical field genuinely refuses to give a rule, and encoding one would be the app scoring the protocol. The owner-declared exposure has direct paper precedent: Avacta's 8-week Food Trial Diary asks the owner to "write down any accidental intake of food that is not part of the trial diet" `[E]`, and PDSA asks them to note "if they have eaten anything they shouldn't have." **This is the only mechanism that converts the 25% unmonitored-access contamination into data.**

**Completion.** Anchor the question on the start. PGIC's stem — "Compared to your condition at the beginning of treatment, how much has your condition changed?" — is the pattern worth stealing, though the specific wording could not be verified from the cited source this round and should be re-checked before it enters copy. Zoetis's paper tracker frames the artifact as a hand-off: "**Please bring this Itch Tracker with you when you return to your vet**" `[E]` — the trial card should say what the vet will see, which makes the report feel earned rather than bolted on.

**Abandonment.** Whole30's own non-enforcement is the register (§3). And a structured stop-reason is the difference between one bit and a clinical fact: a vet reading "stopped at day 19 — wouldn't eat it" prescribes differently than "stopped — cost." Documented reasons owners quit: palatability/refusal, cost and convenience, duration, and unintentional feeding by people unfamiliar with the requirements `[E]` (Purina Institute / TVN). **"Wouldn't eat it" must route to §6.5's intake-decline health lane, not sit as a compliance note.** *(The clinical-trial disposition-coding precedent originally offered for this recommendation was miscited — see §9. The recommendation stands on the veterinary reasons alone; tag the precedent `[MK]` or drop it.)*

**Anti-pattern to name explicitly:** Apple Health models a finite medication course as ongoing, with manual swipe-left Archive and no automatic end `[E]` (https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254565730). That is what B-417 becomes if the milestone stays passive and the owner never opens the card on day 56 — which is also why the milestone should be sticky and never expire.

**And one disqualified mechanic:** endowed progress works (§5a rank 1) but must never be applied to the *trial window itself*. The day count feeds the vet report's scope-cascade rung 2 and a dermatologist's rule-in/rule-out. No head start, no rounding up, no "you're already 10% there."

## 8. Steal / Avoid

### Steal
1. **CAVD's lapse copy, near-verbatim** — "Don't panic! If you make a mistake, it's OK. Record it on the calendar and keep going with the diet trial." Acknowledge → record → continue.
2. **Royal Canin's "not approved by your veterinarian" framing** — model the allowed set as vet-authored *inclusion*, so the owner understands they are recording their vet's instruction, not Nyx's opinion.
3. **Royal Canin's tip that the trial kibble IS the treat** — "For treats, keep some ULTAMINO kibbles in a clean, sealed container." Resolves the most common permitted-treat case with zero data entry (and pre-empts the §3.2 constraint collision).
4. **CAVD's three-axis calendar** — "a calendar of your pet's symptoms, **medications**, and food." Nyx tracks two of three.
5. **Monash's traffic-light lookup register** — describe the food at a portion, never judge the eating.
6. **mySymptoms' two-bar grammar** — claim and confidence adjacent, always, never blended.
7. **Fig's per-item explainability** — tap the flag to learn why it fired.
8. **NWVDS's four-item "contact us if" list** — a free, specialty-authored escalation spec, all four computable from data Nyx holds.
9. **The AAEC "overlooked places" list** — food on walks, another pet's stool from yard or litter box, backwash from a shared water bowl. Perfect content for a one-time setup teaching moment about what the app *cannot* see.
10. **AAEC's "uninterpretable, not negative"** — the data-quality vocabulary for §7.
11. **PVAS10's behavioural anchors** — severity described by what the animal is *doing* ("No itching when eating, playing, exercising or being distracted"), never a number the owner must calibrate. That is confirmation-over-entry applied to a clinical scale.
12. **Zoetis's decaying cadence** — daily in week 1, then weekly on the same day. A front-loaded then decaying ask is the honest, non-nagging answer to "how often do we prompt."
13. **CAVD's pattern of pairing every prohibition with a permitted alternative** — "'Good dog!' Make the food into a treat by putting it into your dog's cookie jar… hide it in a food-dispensing toy."
14. **The care-first use of the manufacturer guarantee** — "Veterinary diets are guaranteed, so the food can be returned and replaced with another if your dog doesn't like it" (CAVD). Surfaceable on refusal, costs nothing, attacks completion directly, and is a clean Pets > $ move.
15. **Duolingo's one non-gamified half** — a back-dated log that closes a coverage gap should just close it, silently and retroactively, with no "you fixed it!" moment.
16. **Somryst's register** — 9-week prescription therapeutic, 6 cores, one core per 7 days, deliberately clinical `[E]` (https://dtxalliance.org/products/somryst/). Brief the §0.4 mock round with this so the trial card doesn't drift warm-and-celebratory.

### Avoid
1. **Whole30's mandatory Day-1 restart.** Never auto-restart on a detected exposure.
2. **Any implication that a slip voids or restarts the trial** — no clinical source instructs it, and implying it converts a recoverable slip into an abandoned trial.
3. **allergic.pet's core pitch** — "Which meals came before the bad days? We'll show you." That is precisely what §6.1 forbids, and it is the nearest competitor's entire value proposition. Nyx must not drift toward it under pressure to make the card feel smart.
4. **mySymptoms-style "Top Suspects" ranking inside an active trial.** During an elimination trial there is one dietary variable by design; a ranked suspect list invites the owner to conclude, and the conclusion is the vet's.
5. **Red/green/amber colour verdicts on a feeding** — Eikey 2021; and Nyx has a ratified colour rule for the vet report (§5.8) but none for the card.
6. **Streaks, freezes, perfect-week badges, owner scores.**
7. **Renaming rather than removing the moral load** — Noom's red→orange.
8. **A blended number in any form** — string, ring, meter, grade, or **bar width**. The paper grid has better information architecture than the shipped card, which is the strongest possible argument for D2.
9. **Absence-based reassurance** in any form, at any coverage level.
10. **Nearest-preceding-meal attribution** for exposures. The lag is 1–14 days, species-dependent.
11. **Flagging concurrent steroids or oclacitinib as a violation.** MSD explicitly permits them; a short prednisolone course is a documented protocol variant that *shortens* the trial.
12. **Deriving a trial verdict from the symptom trend** — secondary-infection treatment and any concurrent antipruritic produce the identical improving curve. Write the *mechanism* into §6.1, not just the principle.
13. **Treating the food label as ground truth** — 33–83% mislabeling in the diets marketed for these trials.
14. **The absolutist "one crumb ruins everything" register.** Pedagogically motivated, not literally true (60% of dogs needed ~30 g), and an app that alarms identically for a 5 g lick and a full bowl trains the owner to dismiss both.
15. **Purina Institute's page as a checklist model** — it omits toothpaste, other pets' food, coprophagy, hunting, outdoor foraging AND concurrent medications. A manufacturer page is not a guideline.
16. **Over-claiming professional endorsement in §13.** **AAHA 2023 does NOT recommend an owner diary** — a search of the full 101 KB guideline PDF returns zero occurrences of "diary" or "journal"; its only compliance tip is "have clients keep extra dog food bags on hand to avoid temporary backorder issues" `[E]`. The diary recommendation comes from CAVD, Tufts, Avacta and individual derm practices. Citing AAHA for it would be exactly the class of upgraded guess this project has been burned by.
17. **Royal Canin Individualis's architecture** — the only manufacturer-built, vet-gated digital nutrition platform in this space, discontinued in the US. Do not architect any part of B-417 to require a vet-side or manufacturer-side surface.

**One meta-observation for the review session.** Across all seven lanes the failures clustered in the *decorative* evidence — the second corroborating statistic, the vendor-blog flourish, the extra verbatim quote — while the load-bearing primary citations held. That is a lower-grade failure than the round that produced retractions, but it is the same mechanism, and a reader skimming for confidence cues would not distinguish them. Two practical rules fall out: **(1)** where a researcher self-flagged weak sourcing ("via search-result extraction, not a full fetch"), that flag was *accurate every time* — treat it as a reliable warning, not boilerplate; **(2)** four of the most load-bearing sources in this document (the Zoetis checklist, the WSAVA form, the Ultamino handout, the AAHA guidelines) were only verifiable by downloading the PDFs and extracting text locally, because WebFetch reported them unreadable. Any future round on this material should budget for that step rather than accepting a fetch failure as absence of evidence.
