The daily look — how a pet seems, captured in a breath and read back honestly

Nyx captures what a pet does and almost nothing about how a pet seems. The divergent round sketched one question answered with an observation word; this round converges it. Four rulings bind every frame here: the look is on for every account, always (a baseline that starts when the trouble starts is not a baseline); analytics means Patterns and the vet report; the vet and the team rule the words; naming waits. Two decisions differ visually, so both are drawn side by side — the shape of the answer, and where the door is.

This is the current-proposal page for the look; later rounds re-publish over this URL. The pencil sketch it converges stays where it is (docs/culprit-home-v2-divergent-mockups.html §01). The draft spec is docs/nyx-daily-look-requirements.md v0.1. Read: §00 the floor · §01 the shape of the answer, two options · §02 the door, four directions · §03 the resting state on a healthy pet · §04 the FAB tile, its confirm, its card · §05 the Home completion beat · §06 History and the day spine · §07 Patterns · §08 the report rows · §09 the briefs · §10 who ruled what.

How to read a frame: every device frame is the app's light surface on its real tokens. Rose marks the one safety element in a frame and the record's symptom pips, nothing else; teal is a door; a hollow ring is a look. The FAB is drawn in the same place on every Home frame so its presence is never mistaken for a proposal. Nothing on any frame carries a colour that could read as a verdict — print it in greyscale and it still reads.
00

The floor — what every frame here already obeys

Eleven isolated lenses reached these in #808 without being asked. They are not up for reaction; they are the ground the directions stand on. Re-open one only with a named counterexample.

The valence axis does not transfer. No pleasant side, no green corner. A "calm" cat is the early sickness-behaviour phenotype.
The owner is the informant, not the subject. Words are behaviours a vet could see on a video, anchored "than usual for her", species-keyed, a closed set. "Add your own" is a note.
The observed-absence day is the prize. "I looked; nothing unusual" is a denominator the record has never held. Under the every-account ruling it is also the baseline. Coverage, never a good day.
No colour on a day, a state or a run. A look is ink. A symptom-class word takes rose only on the leaf it proposes.
A look never enters a count, a floor, a verdict, a trial verdict or the coverage line. It may only raise — by proposing a leaf the owner taps. A "lively" never softens a safety card.
The ritual reads the same on a bad morning. The safety card leads; the question sits beneath it unchanged in wording; no register shift; no haptic.
The completion beat names what was written and stops. A symptom-class word is a symptom commit — a soft impact, never a success. No cheer.
The record lands in the event vocabulary, never in a mood table the engine cannot see. Consent is whole-boundary before a word leaves the account. The widget never writes.
The consequence of "every account, always on": the look must survive months on a healthy pet without a streak, a ring, a nag or a wellness verdict — and the baseline it accumulates has to be represented somewhere honest. §03 is that problem drawn; §07 and §08 are where the answer lives.
01

The shape of the answer — the field, or chips by family

One question, two answer surfaces. Option F is the divergent round's field: energy against her usual × settled / unsettled, one ink, four regions of words, hold to save, the centre writes "looked, nothing unusual", a hollow ring at her usual from the fifth look. Option C is chips grouped by family: the observed-absence chip first, then the species' words in a fixed order, a tap selects, hold or Done saves, "Something else ›" opens the full grid. Each is drawn at the same three moments. The question is the same on both — the instrument is the question; the shape is only how it is answered.

02

The door — four directions, each at three moments

The med strip's register rule allows one kind of one-tap write on Home: a control that writes a row the app could already describe (a confirmation). A look is neither a confirmation nor a form. Door A keeps Home a doorway and puts the write in the FAB. Door B writes on Home under a carve-out written for the look alone. Door C is B plus the FAB tile, one record from two doors — the shape the med strip already ships. Door D lets Home confirm only the usual, and routes every word through the FAB. All four are drawn on the chips shape; the field would sit in the same slot.

No average, no slope, no score, no heatmap, no positive-valence tally. Below floor, the calibration state — never an all-clear.

09

The briefs — seven rulings, each from the frames

Each is four lines; each is also posted on CUL-838. Where a brief continues one of #808's, it says which.

L-1 — The interaction shape

Deciding: how the one question is answered — the two-axis field that resolves to a word, or chips grouped by family.

Options: F the field (§01, top row) · C chips by family (§01, second row) — recommended: every clinical and scientific lens moved there; every ruled word fits it; it writes no unvalidated ordinal; and nothing on it accrues into a "usual" on Home.

Consequence: C writes word keys only (no scale, no version drift); F adds two ordinals and the ring, and drops the grooming / mouth / litter / drinking words to a door.

L-2 — The door (continues DV-2)

Deciding: whether a look is written on Home, in the FAB, or both.

Options: A FAB only, Home a doorway · B a Home write under the look's own carve-out · C both doors, one record — recommended: the one-gesture ritual and the 3pm look, the shape the med strip already ships · D Home confirms the usual only (fails Sam's morning, §02).

Consequence: B and C need the second clause to the register rule and guards/homeWrites.test.ts (a Tier-2 edit, flagged); A and D need nothing on the rule; D makes the reflex tap the only Home control.

L-3 — Cadence (continues DV-3)

Deciding: the cue for the look, and whether it may be scheduled.

Options: bowl-anchored on Home, no clock, plus one opt-in evening schedule (default off) shipping only with a local stand-down — recommended · Home only, no schedule · schedule only.

Consequence: the schedule PR waits on the stand-down (B-288's self-pruning is unshipped); the body is "How does Mochi seem this evening?" and never asserts the record.

L-4 — The resting state on a healthy pet

Deciding: what Home shows after months of looks with nothing wrong.

Options: Home never accrues the look; the baseline lives in Patterns and on the report (§03) — recommended: the instrument does not move, and no Home object can grow into a state · a coverage line on the card · the field's "usual" ring.

Consequence: the 200th open is byte-identical to the 40th; the return for a healthy month is §07's counts and §08's line, never a Home change.

L-5 — The record shape

Deciding: a check_in leaf with a looks child (one timeline, one reversal, History and the spine for free) or a standalone table (the exclusion structural, a second timeline).

Options: Shape A with the exhaustive-category guard — recommended: a fifth event category makes every counting consumer a compile error until it decides, which is Data's objection answered on its merits · Shape B.

Consequence: the enum add is irreversible; the schema is its own PR with the pre-flight; every explicit symptom list gets a pinned "no" for the look.

L-6 — The observed-absence row (continues DV-4; implied by the every-account ruling)

Deciding: whether "I looked and saw nothing unusual" is stored — a row, a spine mark, a report denominator.

Options: yes, a look row with no words — recommended (confirm, do not re-argue) · no. Sub-question: should "Haven't really looked yet" write a not observed row so the report can draw "didn't look" as its own glyph? v0.1 writes nothing.

Consequence: the baseline exists; the season strip and the trial's logged-day denominator never learn it.

L-7 — The report edit (Tier 2)

Deciding: what the vet report carries.

Options: the dated owner-observation line on page 1 beside the GI workup + Appendix G of every look (§08) — recommended · the appendix only · nothing.

Consequence: a Tier-2 edit to the report spec (three proposed lines in the draft §8) approved before build; the look joins no report lane; the render rides CUL-19.
