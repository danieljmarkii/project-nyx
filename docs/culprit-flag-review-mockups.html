When the app flags a photo, let the owner answer it

The PM has disagreed with three or four of the photo read's red flags, and the only way to say so today is an edit form two screens from the warning. This round asks where an owner should answer a flag, what yes, no and not sure should each do, and what the answers may be used for. Seven isolated persona interviews converged on one shape: answer on the record with the photo in view, treat the owner as a second witness rather than an editor of the AI, and never let a No or a Not sure be what quietly takes a warning away.

Sanity check, same day: build the small version. Asked whether this is worth building, eight fresh, isolated lenses all picked S1: make today's correction honest, without adding the question. The owner still corrects through Edit, now one tap from the warning. The app stops contradicting the correction, Home refreshes, Unclear can never clear a flag, a replaced photo's findings get through, and the vet report shows a disputed flag instead of dropping it. §0 draws S1. §2 to §6 (the question and its eight briefs) are parked and come back only on the triggers in §0. One ruling on CUL-1107 replaces eight.

§6

Eight decisions for the PM Parked · replaced by FR-0

Each brief can be ruled from the brief alone; the frames above show every visual choice side by side. Rule by number (FR-1 A, FR-3 B, FR-5 C, …) on CUL-1107, the ruling issue.

FR-1 · Where the owner answers

Deciding: whether the yes/no lives beside the photo or on the Home card.

Options: A, on the record with a door from Home (§3, H1) · B, on the Home card (H1-B) · C, both.

Recommend A: every lens that was asked chose it. The photo is the evidence, and the Home card pools photos, so a No there has no single target.

Consequence: A keeps Home at three write classes and needs the Signal finding to carry incident ids. B needs a Tier-2 amendment and one card per incident.

FR-2 · What a No does to the AI's finding

Deciding: whether an owner's No erases the read's observation or stands beside it.

Options: A, overwrite, as today · B, two witnesses: the No stands down the ask; the observation stays and prints beside the answer · C, a No clears nothing.

Recommend B: Dr. Chen, both owners, the Data Scientist and Trust & Safety converge. A silently drops true positives; C breeds alarm fatigue.

Consequence: an answer store keyed to the photo, and a report change (FR-6). Absorbs CUL-409 and fixes CUL-1104.

FR-3 · What is asked

Deciding: the question's wording and its answers (Q1 against Q1-A).

Options: A, "Is it blood?" Yes / No · B, name the read's claim, "Do you see it too?" Yes, I see it / No, it's something else / Not sure.

Recommend B: the owners asked to be asked what they know; four of five lenses require a Not sure that keeps the flag. The Designer drew A.

Consequence: B adds one reason tap after a No (Q2). A leaves "I can't tell" with nowhere to go but silence.

FR-4 · How long a flag the owner saw stands (CUL-208)

Deciding: what ends a flag the owner agreed with.

Options: A, 14 days, as today · B, an action: I've talked to my vet on the record, or a vet visit logged after it; folds meanwhile, never louder · C, until the owner hides it.

Recommend B: Dr. Chen and Sam. The app never asks whether they called (Jordan: "feels like being graded"); it is a control the owner can tap.

Consequence: a lifetime rule in generate-signal, tied to the vet-visit record. Closes CUL-208's open question.

FR-5 · A repeated dispute persona conflict

Dr. Chen: a second No on the same kind of flag within 14 days should become Show your vet these photos, closed only by a vet visit. The Lab on carprofen whose owner disputes coffee-ground vomit twice is the ulcer that perforates.

PM decision needed: no recommendation. C separates the two concerns (Home stays quiet; the vet still sees the pattern) but was not put to either lens.

FR-6 · What the vet report prints

FR-7 · "Improve our model"
