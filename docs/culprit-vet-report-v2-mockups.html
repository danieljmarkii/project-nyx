§00

The four answers

Can AI write it and keep the guardrails?

Not the clinical words. Let code write them and let AI read them.

An audit of every rule the report enforces found 81. Over AI prose a checker can reliably enforce 9. The 3 it cannot catch are the report's founding hazards: a trial read as clean, cause implied by sentence order, and a refusal read as preference. Eight of ten isolated reads ranked "no AI-written clinical words" first. AI's jobs: test every report change the way a clinic's AI will read it, find patterns offline that become rules, and help the owner write their own, labelled note.

Too wordy. Let the data tell the story.

One record strip carries the caveats the sentences carried.

Today the dog's page 1 is 1,736 words over 2 to 4 sheets. The strip puts care given, signs and food taken in on one date axis, as separate lanes, with every unlogged day hatched through each lane of daily marks. A medication timeline raised physicians' accuracy on past prescriptions from 17% to 91% in one study. A few plain sentences stay on top, because text beat graphs for choosing an action.

Two readers: the vet and the front desk

A filing strip on every sheet, and an email that names the flag.

The tech files on pet name, owner surname, species and window, triages on the subject line, and needs page X of Y when a fax drops a sheet. Today 6 of 12 sheets from the phone carry no patient line. The subject line is a fixed template and never AI: it is the urgency rating, read by whoever has the least context.

LLMs will read it first

Make every number carry its scope on its own line.

A simulated clinic summarizer kept 34% of the report's honesty clauses intact; 43% kept the number and lost its scope. "Apoquel, doses not logged" became "on Apoquel daily". What survived was any qualifier on the same line as every copy of its fact. On the redesign's first draw the same test kept 46%, with no distortions. No hidden JSON, no instructions to software: plain sentences for the vet, one claim per line.

And the print. The PDF the phone makes has no paper margin, so office printers clip its edges. Both simulations put ink within 4 mm of the edge on every page; a browser print of the same report kept 11 mm. The fix is a few lines and is filed (CUL-1864). One PDF exported from your phone decides which of two fixes ships.

What changed against today, and why

  • The patient leads, the brand follows. Today the wordmark is 20pt and the name 16.5pt, a sixth of the way down. The filing strip puts name, owner surname, species and window first, and the wordmark moves to the footer (Designer, vet tech).
  • One axis, separate lanes, a fixed order for every pet. Care given, signs, food taken in. A fixed order means placement never implies cause, and a vet finds the same thing in the same place on her twentieth report (Dr. Chen, Designer).
  • Absence is drawn as absence. Every unlogged day is a black hatch through every lane of daily marks (signs, meals, exposures, doses, the offered bowl), never a blank and never a tint, so it survives a mono printer, a 200 dpi scan and toner-save (vet tech, Data, criticalist). Only a recorded course draws through it, as recorded. Nyx's 40 empty days before her first record shrink behind an axis break and read "no entries", not "a good April".
  • No before/after split, no sparkline, no delta tile, no trend verb. Readers in one study saw a treatment effect in 16% to 84% of charts that had none, and the two-bin split read as the most causal. Counts sit on either side of each change line, each with its logged days, and no sentence says "fell" (D7, applied).
  • Exposure by protein, not by permission. Cooper's Dentastix carries chicken and cereal on 26 days; drawn as an outlined "allowed" box it understated the contamination (cold read). Every protein-day ticks the same, permitted or not, and the lane says a blank day is not a clean day.
  • Numbers in black, qualifiers in black. No qualifier is lighter than its number (vet tech). The weight is printed values, not a line, so 2% looks like 2%.

§02

Who writes the words: five ways AI could, tested

Your question meets a rule. The spec has said since July 2 that the report is "a deterministic assembly of already-true structured findings; there is no generative phrasing on clinical content (the cleanest way to guarantee these hold)." A better design is allowed to beat a rule, so the team tested whether this one still protects what it was written to protect. It does.

What a closed grammar would and would not close. If the model only picks finding IDs and sentence variants and code writes every word, invented numbers, paraphrase, minimizers, polarity and injection all close. Selection, order and adjacency stay open, and they carry both attribution and exoneration. At its limit, the safest AI writer is S4, and S4's choice is either a rule (so no model) or unreviewable.

The house has tried this three times already

  • The Signal's phrasing: 2 of 13 finding types still reach the model; its checker never compares numbers, and its "picky" screen is three words, so "choosy" and "lost interest" pass.
  • The Signal summary: the model path is switched off after two adversarial rounds; safety summaries are template-only by your ruling (CUL-1630).
  • The photo reads: hold best, because the guarantee is structural: deterministic flags run before the model, and the verdict list has no reassuring value. A word blocklist was measured missing about 86% of reassuring phrasings and dropped.

What AI does instead

1. Read the report the way a clinic will, on every change. A standing round-trip test: render the fixtures, extract the text as clinic software does, have an AI summarize it in clinic styles, and have a judge fail the change if a scope clause stops surviving. This is AI as the adversarial reader, before a clinic's AI is.

fixtures→PDF text layer→AI summaries, 3 clinic styles→judge, 33 clauses→red if a clause is lost

2. Find patterns offline that ship as rules. Sweeping the fixture corpus surfaced heaped timestamps (all 26 of the cat's vomits at one minute), a permitted treat carrying a trial-breaking protein every day, and coverage half made of the symptom itself. Each became a filed fix to the deterministic report (CUL-1865).

3. Help the owner say their own piece. Jordan wants "a line that's mine": "From Jordan: he gets Apoquel daily; I didn't log it." That line is the owner's words, attributed, quoted and outside the clinical sections. A model may help the owner draft it on the phone; it never writes the report's voice.

If you want AI on the page anyway: the narrowest shape option, not recommended

Shape: S4 first (the model orders fixed findings), then S2 only as a closed grammar (finding IDs and sentence variants; code writes every number, date and name). Below the record strip as its caption, never above it or in the flags; 8.5pt italic with a hairline rule; generated once, stored with the snapshot, never regenerated on open or for a share link.

Label, if it ships (the four lenses converged on honesty over comfort): "Drafted by AI from the figures on this page. Software confirmed each number appears in them; wording, emphasis and what it leaves out are not checked. Where it differs from a figure, the figure governs."

What it would take: about 4 PRs for S4 and 6 for S2; a stored-output schema before any model call; the consent gate plus a re-ask, because a report summary is a new purpose; privacy-policy text; a payload guard. What it would reverse: spec §5 and §7, the September critique's "nothing it says reaches the vet report", and the October positioning "written by templates, not a model". Cost per report is under two cents; determinism, not money, is the constraint.

Before any model runs, the text layer loses things

  • Numbers can vanish. In the Chromium proxy, 4 of 6 common extractors dropped every digit set in tabular figures: "Vomiting spans days (first logged May 14 episodes on days". Unproven on the iPhone's PDF; one exported PDF settles it.
  • Chart labels print twice. A white halo behind each value label doubles it, so Cooper's itch chart extracts as "33 55 33 11 33 11 00".
  • Tiles interleave and footers vanish. A tile's value lands beside the next tile's caption; two common converters drop headers and footers, where "not a diagnosis" lives today.

The fixes, in the order they matter

  1. The scope rides on the number's own line: "at least 4 of 154 feedings", "itching on 16 of 46 days (43 logged)". Badges on the same line survived 21 of 24 times.
  2. Each medication line carries its dose record in the same line: "Apoquel 16 mg once daily · no doses logged; the course as recorded, not evidence it was given". Where doses exist, Appendix D's split (given, refused, unconfirmed) in CUL-1209's words.
  3. Provenance badged, attribution limits inside the sentence a reader draws from. "Owner's note:" and quotes on every owner line; invisible characters stripped (CUL-1863).
  4. A text line under every chart with the same numbers, and chart labels that print once. The one-line version survived all six extractors.
  5. Sheet 2 is the record in lines (below): one self-contained claim per line, ISO dates, the scope repeated on every line, an ID in the margin like a lab accession number.

Not worth building. Embedded JSON (no AI ingestion path reads PDF attachments, and a layer the owner never saw is a consent problem), SNOMED or VeNom codes (no consumer), and any sentence addressed to "AI readers": clinic tools may strip or flag it as injection, and a vet's IT may block the file. Plain sentences written for the vet are the posture.

§05

Why the print clipped

The phone's PDF (modelled): ink within 4 mm of the edge on 44 of 44 pages. Office printers cannot print the outer quarter inch, so the edges clip.

The same report printed from a browser: about 11 mm on every side, 0 of 39 pages near the edge.

The app makes the PDF with expo-print and passes no margin, so its printable area is the whole sheet. WebKit then honours the report's CSS margin by shrinking the layout, and iOS scales the page back up to fill the paper: the margin turns into a 9% enlargement. Two fixes are filed on CUL-1864. A server CSS change (top and bottom from the page rule, sides from padding) reaches every installed app with no update; a client change uses expo-print's own margin option. Never both: they stack to about 15.5 mm and two extra sheets. One PDF exported from your phone decides, because a print fix measured only in Chromium is how the last two fixes missed this.

§08

The evidence

AI-written clinical documents

Omission is the commonest error in LLM clinical summaries (86% of errors in one study); invented and negated facts are the most harmful (44% of invented facts were major, and 30% were negations). Equal ratings hide extra errors: discharge narratives rated as good as physicians' carried 2.91 errors each against 1.82. Shortening strips qualifiers, and our honesty rules are mostly qualifiers. The best checkers reach about 75% balanced accuracy and cannot see what is missing, or what is true but misleading. In the data-to-text precedent, clinicians decided best from expert text, generated text tied graphs, and they preferred the graphs. Every hybrid with evidence behind it keeps the facts, numbers, selection and required clauses in code. One prompt at temperature 0 gave 80 different outputs in 1,000 runs, so any AI text would have to be generated once and stored. docs/research/2026-10-vet-report-ai-written-report.md

Overlaid clinical timelines

A medication timeline raised physicians' accuracy on past prescriptions from 17% to 91% (n=23). In two neonatal ICU trials, about 135 descriptive words beat trend graphs for choosing an action, though staff preferred the graphs. Reviewers saw a treatment effect in 16% to 84% of charts that had none (n=37). Separate graphs beat overlapping ones even for trained nurses; accuracy falls as lines go from 2 to 4 to 8. Readers interpret confidently over gaps unless the gap is marked. docs/research/2026-10-vet-report-overlaid-timelines.md

Machine readers

Claude, OpenAI and Gemini read PDF page images plus text; some pipelines read text only. AI summaries over-generalize about five times more than human ones and keep uncertainty cues less than half the time. No practice software imports a client's structured file. CoVet's free plan summarizes with page citations, so the round trip needs no clinic login. docs/research/2026-10-vet-report-machine-readers.md

The round trip

Two fixtures, three clinic summary styles each, 33 scope clauses, an isolated judge. Today's report: 34% kept, 43% number only, 5 distortions. The redesign's first draw: 46% kept, 39% number only, none distorted; the null food-association result was dropped in every dog summary in both rounds.
