Vet report v2: fewer words, more charts, the same honesty

The vet report works, and a real vet used it. Its first page runs 1,527 words for a diet-trial dog and 867 for a chronic-vomiting cat, against a sixty-second scan. The PM asked the team to explore a cleanly designed, chart-led report that a vet can consume inside a fifteen-minute appointment and would pass on. This round draws four directions for the same dog, the lead direction on the safety-led cat, the mono and phone proofs, and puts nine decisions to the PM.

How this round was made. Eleven isolated interviews, each reading the report exactly as the engine renders it today (Dr. Chen and four specialist lenses, a vet tech, Jordan, Sam, the Designer, Data + Trust & Safety, Engineering), plus two research sweeps. Every number on every sheet below is one the shipped assembly layer computes from the repo's fixtures; where a sheet shows a number the engine does not compute today, the caption says engine change. Nothing here changes the shipped report until the PM rules.
§00

Both pages below are the report as the app builds it this morning. The clinical lenses all reached the same answer in the first minute (the trial line, the weight, the weekly bars with their intervention marker) and all stopped in the same place: the diet-trial block, where one fact (no allowed-food list is recorded) is restated four times before the charts. The two numbers that decide the encounter, 8 → 3 vomits and 6 → 1 loose stools across the halves, sit about a thousand words in. The line every vet said they would act on, 8 of 8 timed episodes came six hours or more after eating, is the last line of page 1.

§01

What the room agreed without being asked

The interviews ran in isolation; no lens saw another. The rules below were named, in their own words, by at least four of the eleven. The dissent is at the end of the section, unresolved.

Fewer words, yes. The words are not the clinical caveats, they are bookkeeping and repeats.Dr. Chen · skeptical GP · GI · nutritionist · tech · Jordan · Sam · Designer · DataOne statement per fact on page 1; the long form once, at the back. Two caveats earn a place beside their number: "Intake not directly observed" and the assessed denominator on any AI read.
A chart is a number already lifted, so the caveat moves into the chart or dies.skeptical GP · GI · Data · Designer · Sam · JordanCoverage is the ground the marks stand on: a day strip under every time chart, hatched where nothing was logged; the unassessed drawn outside the proportion bar; every count labelled n / N days. "Repeat what the chart can carry; say once what it cannot" (GI). This is what CUL-480 stalled on.
The report is a timeline. Draw one axis and put everything on it.nutritionist · GI · skeptical GP · Data · Designer · Jordan · Dr. ChenSymptom lanes, intervention spans with start and stop dates, dated exposure ticks, coverage pips, weigh-ins as dots, sharing one week axis. It replaces the three prose blocks every reader skipped and carries the confound with no sentence.
Absence is named, never drawn.criticalist · Data · Sam · skeptical GP · Dr. ChenA drawn absence (an empty bar, a full square, a zero segment, a missing tile) reads as normal. A named one ("not observed", "no weigh-ins", a hatched day) reads as a gap. The band gets plainer as the page gets prettier, so the eye learns the plain box is the one to read (the Signal's S1 rule, on paper).
Four charts mislead an owner-logged record and are vetoed on page 1.every clinical lens · Data · Jordan · Sam · DesignerThe before/after dumbbell (draws a verdict through unequal coverage or a co-started drug: 3 → 20 is a logging onset). A sparkline through symptom counts (asserts continuity across unlogged weeks). A stacked protein bar that can exceed its feedings and sits under the vomit bars. Any tile with a delta arrow or verdict colour.
Beautiful means designed, not decorated. The reference is a lab sheet, not a wellness app.skeptical GP · tech · Dr. Chen · Designer · criticalist"Beautiful gets filed with the Instagram screenshots" (tech). What gets passed forward looks like it came from another clinic: plain, dated, one sheet, a header that survives the scanner. Type, rhythm and rules, nothing added.
The channel is sameness and time saved, never a footer.all elevenA vet recommends what makes the next appointment easier and never has to be corrected in front of a client. The small wordmark and a tokenless QR are all the brand that survives; "the moment the page asks for the referral itself, I'm handing my vet an ad" (Jordan). A vets link belongs on the legend sheet, not page 1 (tech, Dr. Chen).
One number per sign at the top.Dr. Chen · skeptical GP · criticalist · Data · Jordan · Sam · DesignerToday page 1 shows 8 (the chronicity flag's episodes), 11 (entries) and the chart's 11 for one sign, reconciled by a legend five pages away. The flag keeps its count with a dagger to the legend; the headline keeps one.

Sam (owner) + the current spec §3: the possible-foreign-material photo belongs beside the flag, "big enough to see the fragment across a desk", or a line saying the photo is not here. The spec already says safety-flagged photos lead the band.

Trust & Safety + the vet tech: no photo on page 1. Photos live on their own final sheet with their own header, so a front desk files page 1 without vomit at the counter and a forward can drop them deliberately; page 1 carries a pointer ("photo, sheet 7") and the retained / not-retained statement.

§05

Decision briefs

Each brief can be ruled from this page. Recommendations are the team's; where the lenses disagree the dissent is named instead.

D1 · The direction

Deciding: which page 1 the build targets.

Options: C, the record strip (recommended): one shared axis, every prose block the readers skipped becomes a lane, no verdict added, the most distinctive artifact for the channel. A, the lab sheet: the built report edited hard, fastest to ship, a tidier report rather than a new one. B, the dashboard page: the one the PM will like on sight and every lens trusts least. D, the cover sheet: additive, two homes per fact.

Consequence: C is the heaviest build (a lane component, day-grain data in the assembly layer, a split at the intervention); A is mostly CSS and copy cuts; D is a new sheet and nothing else.

D2 · One artifact or two

Deciding: whether the visual page replaces today's page 1, sits on top of it, or is an owner-selectable format.

Options: One artifact (recommended, Engineering + Designer): the visual summary is page 1, today's dense content becomes a "Clinical detail" page 2, appendices unchanged. Replace page 1 outright and drop the dense content. An owner toggle "classic / visual": a decision at the hand-off moment for a reader who is not the owner, and two artifacts to gate forever (vetoed by Engineering and the Designer).

Consequence: one artifact keeps one cold-read gate and one invariant suite; a toggle doubles both and adds a Principle 1 decision to the send flow.

D3 · Where the caveats live

Deciding: the rule that resolves CUL-358 and CUL-480.

Options: "Repeat what the chart can carry; say once what it cannot" (recommended, GI + Data + Designer): a caveat that protects a number from being misread moves into the chart (coverage strip, marker, hatched week, n / N labels); a caveat about what the record cannot see is said once in a fixed six-glyph legend strip identical on every report. Keep the prose caveats and cut only the repeats. Drop the caveats and trust the appendix.

Consequence: the first turns two stalled issues into design work inside this track; the third breaks §5 and is out.

D4 · The safety photo on page 1

Deciding: whether a safety-flagged photo renders beside its flag on page 1.

Sam + criticalist + the current spec §3: the photo beside the flag, big enough to see across a desk, or an explicit "photo not retained". Trust & Safety + the vet tech: no photo on page 1; a pointer and the retained statement; photos on their own final sheet with their own header. No recommendation: a genuine conflict.

Consequence: variant 1 keeps the spec's ratified rule and the can't-miss item in view; variant 2 makes page 1 fileable at a front desk and the forward controllable, and needs the pre-send photo review (CUL-77) built first either way.

D5 · Brand and QR on a channel artifact

Deciding: what the brand is allowed to do on the page a vet may pass on.

Options: Wordmark, a tokenless QR captioned for what it does, and a discreet "how this record is made" link on the legend sheet, never on page 1 (recommended, tech + Dr. Chen + T&S). The QR on page 1 pointing at a vets page (CUL-282). No QR at all.

Consequence: today the QR encodes the consumer landing page and is captioned "getculprit.app" while the spec calls it verify-only; the caption changes whichever option wins, and the vets page becomes the QR's target when it exists.

D6 · Colour on page 1

Deciding: whether the visual page may use colour at all.

Options: None on page 1 (recommended): ink, grey, hatch; the §5.8 texture carve-out stays for the appendix protein chart. The accent on the intervention marker only ("the thing that changed"). Colour as enhancement wherever a texture twin exists.

Consequence: mono is the only direction that survives a fax unchanged and cannot drift toward verdict colour; the accent-on-marker option needs a B&W proof on every report.

D7 · Split the comparison at the intervention

Deciding: whether the before/after read splits at the window midpoint (today: first 31 d / last 31 d) or at the intervention date.

Options: Split at the intervention, with logged days on both sides, and mark stops as well as starts (recommended, Jordan + GI + nutritionist; CUL-291). Keep the window halves. Show both.

Consequence: an assembly-layer change with an adversarial pass; today's 8 → 3 includes eleven days on the old kibble.

D8 · Sheet numbers

Deciding: whether every sheet carries "N of M" beside its section label.

Options: Both, if the on-device PDF path can count pages at print time (recommended; Engineering to verify). Section labels only (the 2026-07-02 ruling). Fixed numbers from the renderer, which print makes wrong.

Consequence: a fax that delivered six of eight sheets is detectable only with a count.

D9 · Where the track lives

Deciding: the Linear home for the build once D1 is ruled.

Options: A new project, "Vet report v2", carrying Engineering's PR 0–4 and the CUL-19 checkpoint deploy (recommended). Fold into Legacy Backlog beside CUL-358 / CUL-480. Hold until the App Store submission lands.

Consequence: PR 0 (the renderer module split) is direction-independent and can start now; everything after it is invisible in production until the generate-report redeploy.

§07

Never, on any direction

  • Verdict colour, a green anything, a trend arrow, a score, a grade, a percentage without both counts, a gauge, a donut, "improving" or "worsening" as a word or a chip.
  • A smoothed line through owner-logged points; a line across an unlogged week; a y-axis that hides zero; unequal y-scales across siblings; a bar drawn full width for a partial week.
  • A before/after read over unequal coverage or across a co-started drug without both stated on the read itself.
  • "0 of N" for blood, foreign material or mucus; a zero-width segment; an intake strip on a free-fed pet; a full square that says "ate" about a bowl nobody watched.
  • A flag as an icon, a colour, a badge or a chart annotation. A flag without its provenance tag. A photo flag whose photo is gone, unsaid.
  • The app addressing the owner on the vet's page; the app's name in the body; app vocabulary ("allowed list", "antigen check") that the vet must be taught in the room.
  • Two numbers for one sign at the top. A caption longer than its chart. A card inside a card. A footer that sells.
  • Photos of the owner's home inside a chart; an account identifier; a caregiver's name; a QR that encodes the token.

§10

What the evidence says

Two frozen research briefs were written for this round: docs/research/2026-09-vet-report-visual-design-sweep.md (the design of trusted, dense clinical one-pagers; a 22-device catalogue) and docs/research/2026-09-vet-report-distribution-channel.md (how a client-supplied document enters a clinic, what makes a vet recommend a tool, the competitor re-verification). Evidence, not decisions; the points below are the ones that bear on the briefs above.

Structured beats narrative by a factor of about three.Rosenbaum 2010, via PMCStructured summary tables cut a clinician's time to the critical fact from about four minutes to about ninety seconds and doubled comprehension against narrative. The place all reader groups fail is integrating across cells, which is what the shared axis is for.
Clinicians prefer charts, and their objections are honesty objections.Zikmund-Fisher 2019, n = 327 physicians85–89% prefer visual displays to tables; 19–32% expect more workload and 32–40% raise liability concerns. Bar graphs read in 31 s against 40 s for tables at equal accuracy (Brewer 2012). A reference or harm anchor changes what the reader does, so the report must never author one: the vet is the anchor.
The receiving clinic is a technician and a file picker.ezyVet, Vetspire, AVImark, Cornerstone, Digitail, Provet, Shepherd docsA person opens the patient and attaches one file, names it, picks a category (no "client summary" bucket exists), PDF or JPEG. AVImark's own staff document print-then-rescan. The first reader is the technician doing intake (AAHA 2023), and two practice-management tools now AI-summarise uploaded PDFs before the vet reads them: the page needs a text layer and plainly labelled counts.
Sameness is what saves the minutes.the AGP one-pager; the pet-insurance recommendation dataThe one standardised clinical one-pager with measured time savings (4–19 minutes per visit) earned them by being the same shape every time, not by carrying more. The two measured barriers to a vet recommending a client-side tool are "a hassle for staff" (35%) and "without increasing consultation time". A fixed layout and a fixed legend are the channel.
The appointment is shorter than fifteen minutes.Everitt; Shaw 2004UK first-opinion slots are most often 15 minutes (49.8%) with an observed median of 9 min 49 s; 48% run over. The vet's own data-gathering is about 9% of what is said, mostly closed questions. There is no veterinary study of whether a visual summary shortens history-taking: research debt.
Nobody ships this, as of September 2026.the competitor re-verificationVettie rebuilt (PDF and CSV now paid), Vet Record added an emergency QR, Petfetti's language converged on ours, hardware moved first (Sure Petcare ships a vet-addressed period chart; Fi builds a shareable record). No pet app ships a chart-led, vet-facing one-pager, and none uses the report as a vet-acquisition channel. Three June claims moved; the brief lists them.
Three anti-patterns most likely to trip a beautiful redesign.the design sweep §8Colour as verdict (dies on the photocopier; green is a reassurance an owner-logged record cannot give). A reference range or "impression" authored by the app. The compact variant that keeps the chart and drops the coverage line, which is exactly the trade to refuse.
Two devices from the catalogue to test in round 2.the design sweep §7, D9 and D10A limitations block placed immediately above the answer (the radiology order: findings, limitations, impression), which the cat sheet's line beneath the band approximates. And the clinician's ruled-off block the vet writes in, from the WSAVA form: the report stops before the impression and leaves room for it. Neither is drawn this round; both are cheap.
