D6 — where the door lives

Two acts must never be confused: changing the window keeps one continuous episode; replacing the trial ends it. The proposal gives them one door and two honest verbs.

03

D1 — the sheet: a total, or a delta

A vet says “take it to twelve weeks”, never “add twenty-eight days”. The proposal is denominated in what the owner was actually handed.

The milestone keeps its delta either way. There the owner has not been handed a number, and the named one-tap default is what stops them tapping done at day 56 (Jordan, §4.3). Two moments, two registers, one write.

(a) / (b) — the proposal, D4 box checked

Elimination diet trial — day 60 of 84. Window extended from 56 days on 19 Sep (day 56), owner reports at the vet’s direction. Primary sign logged: vomiting.

“owner reports” is load-bearing. The app cannot verify a vet instruction and must never assert one — the same attribution discipline §4.3 applies to the owner’s outcome verdict.

D2 (a) vs (b) has no visual difference — three columns on diet_trials versus a diet_trial_window_changes child table render the same sentence for a single extension. They differ only on a trial extended twice, where (a) keeps the first and last window and (b) keeps every step. The recommendation is (a), on this table’s own precedent: target_protein_set_at already ruled that “an edit is disclosed here, never versioned — one value, whole-trial” (TP-3).
06

D3 — why backward is a different act

The report’s completed-trial line is computed against the current window (render.ts:3948). Executed: a 56-day trial, shortened to 28 on day 28, then marked done.

executed   56-day trial → shortened to 28 → completed at day 28

Ran its course — the full window was completed.

An eight-week trial abandoned at four weeks, rendered to the clinician as one that completed its full course. Nothing on the document contradicts it. This is why shortening cannot ride the extension path, and why §5.1’s original-window record is what makes the report safe in either direction.

Not an argument against ever shortening. An owner who typed 56 when the vet said 28 has a real correction to make — and an honest existing path for it (Replace the trial, which records a stopped_reason). D3(a) keeps v1 forward-only and closes this with no new render logic.

08

D7 — one tap moves a reassurance gate

Round 1 of this page asserted that coverage is untouched by an extension. The adversarial-reviewer pass falsified that the same session, and the finding is serious enough to be the most consequential decision here. It is already reachable today at the milestone — the mid-trial door does not create it, it multiplies it.

On an un-ended trial past its target, the B-422 coverage tail clip bounds the coverage denominator at the current target (lib/dietTrial.ts:2201-2206). Extend, and the clip stops applying.

The record is too sparse to read that as a clean elimination.

↓  32 feedings — all 32 matched the trial diet or a permitted food.

Zero new evidence. No meal was logged, no exposure resolved; an owner tapped a button about a calendar. It runs both ways — on a trial logged daily then silent, one tap withdraws a clean claim. And nothing on any surface says the window moved.

The disclosure the clip’s own justification cites — and does not have

lib/dietTrial.ts:2223 — “not the B-422 tail clip (that is the trial’s own prescribed window closing, and closedByOverrun discloses it)”

Repo-wide, closedByOverrun is read by nothing in production — its definition, one comment, and its own test file. A comment writing a cheque the code does not cash (C-38), sitting inside the clip whose whole justification is the disclosure. D7(a) is to wire it up.

The precedent that settles it — one block away in the same report

Allowed list: Royal Canin Selected Protein PR primary diet. The allowed list changed after the trial started — the dates above are when each food was permitted, and feedings are scored against the list in force on the day.

The report already discloses the other mid-trial change to the comparator (render.ts:2721), because a mid-trial change to the comparator invalidates a reading. A mid-trial change to the window is the larger claim and has no equivalent anywhere. §5 of this page is that equivalent; D7 is the gate behind it.

D7’s option (d) is “do nothing”, and it is drawn here so that it is ruled rather than defaulted into. The recommendation is (a): wire the disclosure that already exists, is already computed, is already tested, and is read by nothing.
