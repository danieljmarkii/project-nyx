### §0.3 Ruled in-session by the team (labelled; each individually vetoable; three revised by the adversarial pass)

- **T-1** The look's question and chips **never change in response to the record** (the floor's item 6, held against the behaviourist's "the chips become the finding's ask" — Dr. Chen's "the owner is my instrument and the instrument does not move" wins). On a safety morning the finding's ask belongs to the safety card, the trial strip or the med strip, never to the look. *What may respond to the record is what Home draws of an answered look (§3.3) and the emergency door's conditionals (§4.6) — never the instrument.*
- **T-2 — VETOED by the PM (R8, 2026-09-09: "Who hasn't seen their pet lol.. what if it's afternoon").** There is no "Haven't really looked yet" chip. A skipped day writes nothing and Home says nothing; the question's time-word follows the clock band, so an afternoon open asks about *today*. What the vetoed row bought — a recorded exit for the reflex tapper (the round-1 adversarial pass, gap 8) — is carried by §7's always-printed denominator alone, and the report's "didn't look" glyph goes with it (a day with no look is a blank). The `not_observed` outcome leaves §5.2.

- **T-4 (revised)** Emergency signs are **never chips**; they live behind one quiet door on the look, as static species-keyed copy Dr. Chen signs (§4.6). A door is a chip with a chevron at the same weight as a word, never a dimmed or dashed chip (C-7: dimmed is the app's "unavailable" convention). *Struck in round 2:* the hold-to-save — a hidden gesture with no cue on a surface where every answer must cost the same, and the mock never drew it. *Said honestly in round 2:* a word behind the door costs one gesture more than a head word, so the head words are the named exceptions and a ruled list (T-13).

- **T-14 (new, round 3 — R9)** **The day model.** A look may be made at any hour, any number of times a day; each is its own `looks` row with its own `occurred_at`; the day (a local day, B-421's one counter) is the unit of every count. A word marked in two looks the same day counts **once** for that day; a day is *answered* when it holds ≥ 1 look; a day is an *observed-absence day* only when **every** look that day is `nothing_unusual` (a *nothing unusual* at 7 AM and an *off* at 6 PM is an *off* day, never an absence day — the later look does not overwrite the earlier one, the day's classification does). No surface says *morning*, *tonight* or *this evening* about a look; the hour prints on the entry. **Within one look the energy poles clear each other** (*Lively* clears *Off* and *Sleeping more*, and back): one entry cannot say both; a day can. **The day's classification decides every glyph and count** — on the report's strip a mixed day draws ● (an observation), never ○ (C-4: precedence is the only honest resolution, and the accusing branch wins).
- **T-15 (new, round 3 — R14; extended by the reviews)** **The card never closes the question, and its lines never mix.** After a look the card carries a **today list** — each look an *entry* (a hollow ring · the words in the serif · the hour · *Undo* for the completion dwell, then `›` to the record), newest first — a *receipt* on its own line under the entry that earned it (§3.3), the question **folded to one line** (*How does Mochi seem now? ▾*) that re-opens the chips cleared in place, and the *coverage line* as the footer (§3.3). An entry, a receipt and the footer are three kinds of thing and never share a line; the footer's numbers are tabular figures at the line's end with the door at the right edge so the line cannot wrap. Nothing collapses to an "answered row": the round-2 row and its bold verb are retired. **The list never becomes a feed** (Principle 3 names a log feed among the things Home is not): the card shows the two newest entries and folds the rest behind *N more today ›* (the med strip's §7 collapse rule, applied). **A quiet entry is quiet** (L-16): the absence renders in the sans, the secondary ink, a size down; a symptom-class word keeps the serif. **Undo fades for the last half-second of its dwell** before the chevron takes the slot, and a tap during the fade still undoes — the two controls never swap silently under a reaching thumb. **The footer is a line, not a door**: the card's one door is on its label, so no tappable control faces the ask row's caret (C-5).
- **T-16 (new, round 3 — both reviews; the brief is L-15)** **The coverage line has three forms and never renders a change.** The ratio (*Answered 24 of the last 28 days*) below full coverage; **the window alone at 28 of 28** (*Counted across the last 28 days*) — because a saturated rolling count of eligible days *is* a consecutive count, and the owner the dissent named lives at the saturation point; **nothing below the receipt's floor** (Q-13, fourteen answered days). It renders only under a today list (Q-16 resolved: the resting card never grows; a skip costs nothing on screen); Undo of the day's only look removes it. After a withheld period it returns as the window alone until the window has moved past the withheld days, so a hospitalisation is never read back as a lower number. R11's mitigation sentence ("the number floats") is corrected: it floats *below* the top of its range.
- **T-17 (new, round 3 — the adversarial pass, blocker 1)** **The pairing receipt lives on Patterns and the report, never on Home.** On Home it is an ungated claim about the same vomits a gated card may already be speaking to, chosen by the word the owner just tapped — an uncorrected comparison from a family of twenty-six words (`components/home/TrendZone.tsx`: *an ungated duplicate of a gated claim is a bypass*). Where it renders, **both sides are answered days**, the left denominator is stated as such (*of the 3 vomit days you answered*), a vomit day with no look is said in the same sentence (*· 1 vomit day not answered*) rather than silently scored as "nothing seen", the left numerator is **never zero and never one**, and it renders only once the window holds **≥ 3 answered vomit days and ≥ 10 answered other days**. Its unit is *days with ≥ 1 vomit*, never the report's episode count.
- **T-18 (new, round 3 — both reviews)** **Receipts are derived at render, earned by symptom-class words only, and absent below the floor.** Derived from the record as it stands (soft-deleted rows excluded, a backdated row placed by its local day), never stored on the row, never latched in state — so an Undo re-arms the first-day form and a backdated earlier *Off* moves the first day. **Activity words and the absence earn no receipt** (*First day Mochi has seemed lively* is a wellness receipt). Below Q-13's floor **no receipt renders at all** — a naked *First day Mochi has seemed off.* on four days of evidence implies he had been fine until now, which is reassurance by implication produced by removing the clause that was the claim's warrant. Precedence when one entry earns two: the first-day form, then the count form. The count form's window is **the footer's own** (the last four weeks — a fixed span, never a count of looks) and it renders only when that window holds ≥ 7 answered days, a floor on its own denominator distinct from Q-13's, so a month's first day never reads *1 of the 1*. The first-day form's denominator is the answered days **strictly before** the first-marked local day, and every surface says *before it*.
- **T-19 (new, round 3 — the adversarial pass, blocker 3e)** **One day, stored once.** The client (`lib/analytics.ts`, B-421's counter, the device zone) and the report (`generate-report/report.ts`, `user_profiles.timezone` with a UTC fallback) bucket days on **two clocks**, so "Answered 28 of the last 28 days" on Home and the report's answered-day count can differ over the same rows. The `looks` row therefore stores a **`local_day`** (`DATE NOT NULL`, computed on the device from `occurred_at` in the device zone at write, re-derived by "Change time" through `sourceAfterPointEdit`, C-10), and **every surface counts that key** — Home, Patterns, the report's line, the graph, Appendix G. A timezone change mid-window moves nothing already written. Pinned by a timezone-honest fixture (UTC+14 / −10) anchored to `Date.now()` (C-29, CUL-831), with a look at 11:58 PM and a "Change time" across midnight.
- **T-20 (new, round 3 — the adversarial pass, blocker 5)** **The withheld state is triggered by a positive intake fact, never by ignorance.** The shipped `isAnimalNotEating` is inert on a pet with no trial row (`lib/dietTrialFacts.ts` returns before `readIntakeDecline`), so the gate gains a record-local arm read from **rated meals** — two of the last three refused or picked. The first draft's second arm ("a cat with no meal row in the last 24 hours") is **struck**: it latched the withheld state on forever for every free-fed cat whose owner never logs meals, and its copy named meals that did not exist (`lib/analytics.ts`: *a logging gap is not anorexia*). A record that is merely blind is not withheld on Home; the report's line says the intake record is empty for the window (§8 rule 12). The withheld entry **says why in the owner's register and points at her record** (*Saved · While Pixel's eating needs attention, Home keeps quiet days off the card — an ordinary day isn't a sign she's well. ## §1 What this is, and what it is not

**What it is.** Nyx captures what a pet *does* well and almost nothing about how a pet *seems*. The look is a closed-set, species-keyed observation an owner makes in a breath, anchored "than usual for her", stored in the existing event vocabulary, and read back only as counts of days with denominators — on Patterns and on the vet report. Its single new datum is the **observed-absence day** ("I looked; nothing unusual"), which the record has never held and which, under R1, becomes the baseline.

**What it is not.** Not a mood. Not a rating of the pet's state. Not an input to any count, floor, verdict, trial verdict or coverage line. Not a wellness surface. Not a streak. Not a Home v2 layout decision beyond where the question sits.

**Out of scope, filed rather than folded:** the household track (CUL-194 / B-292 — the `observer` column is reserved, never populated by a shared credential); a Sunday review (DV-5 stays parked); the taxonomy's W2 waves (§9a not buildable); product telemetry (R2); naming (R4); a widget that writes (V2-1).

## §2 The floor — settled across the eleven isolated lenses of #808 (re-open only with a named counterexample)

### §3.3 The resting state on a healthy pet — L-10 **RULED** (R11 the coverage line, R12 the receipts; the mock's §03 and §04)

**What the PM asked for** (re-opening L-4 in round 1: *"day 40 and day 200 are the same … day 7 better than day 1"*) and **what round 3 rules:** two things grow on Home after a look, and one thing never does.

**1. The receipts (R12) — what grows when there is something to count.** A *receipt* is one line of plain ink under the entry that earned it, and it is **earned only by a symptom-class word — never by *nothing unusual*** (a run of quiet days is the one thing no surface may count aloud) **and never by an activity word** (*first day Mochi has seemed lively* is a wellness receipt — T-18). - **A word's count in the footer's window (Home):** *Off on 3 of the 24 days you've answered in the last four weeks · first Sep 3.* The window is the footer's own fixed span, never a count of looks ("the last 7 days you answered" is a duration wearing a count's clothes, C-19, and for a sparse looker spans a season) and never a calendar anchor that resets (*1 of the 1 days since Sep 1* on a month's first morning); it renders only when the window holds ≥ 7 answered days (T-18).

**The receipt's floors (the round-2 reviews, G8; unchanged in kind, restated in days):** (1) the window is bounded by a date, never by a count of looks; (2) **below a coverage floor** (provisional: **fourteen answered days** for the pet — Q-13) **no receipt renders** (T-18 — the first draft dropped the denominator and kept the fact, and the naked *First day Mochi has seemed off.* on four days of evidence implied he had been fine until now), so a worried owner on day four reads neither a coverage message nor a novelty claim the record cannot warrant; (3) **under the floor's item 12** a receipt is the bare first date (*first marked off Sep 2*), never a rate the withheld absence count could be read back out of by subtraction; (4) it inherits §6.10 — never a window that straddles a vocabulary change; (5) a receipt belongs to the entry that earned it — it stays under that entry when a later look arrives the same day (a good afternoon never removes a concern the card already said), and it goes when its entry is undone; (6) every receipt is derived at render from the record as it stands, never stored, never latched (T-18). **2. The coverage line (R11; its forms L-15 / T-16) — what grows on every answered day, and why it cannot accrue.** The footer of the card, under a today list: ***Answered 24 of the last 28 days***. ## §4 The vocabulary — RULED (Dr. Chen, over the veterinary behaviourist's §4 draft; Data, T&S, Sam, Jordan, `nyx-voice`, `clinical-guardrails` at the table; adversarial review recorded in §4.9)

### §4.1 The rules every word obeys

1. **The video test.** An owner could show a vet a phone video of it. Observable, never inferred: *sleeping more*, not *tired*; *hiding*, not *anxious*.
2. **Anchored.** Every word is "than usual for her"; the reference is the animal's own record, never a species norm. The chip label carries the anchor where it fits; the question carries it always.
3. **Species-keyed.** Two lists, declared per key with the taxonomy's `EventSpecies` mechanism; copy-level splits stay in labels, never keys (HR-23). A key both species share is one key.
4. **Grouped by the families that exist** (`EVENT_FAMILIES`) where one fits; where the word's family has not landed yet (Mobility W4, Urinary & litter W3, Mouth W5+, Ears & eyes W4), the look's chip group names it and the table says which wave lands the family. A look group is presentation metadata (D2), never schema.
5. **Both directions permitted as observations; neither rendered as a verdict.** *Lively* and *subdued*; *clingy* and *hiding*; *drinking more* and *drinking less*. A rise in an activity positive never reassures; a drop may raise (only through what a vet reads on the report — the engine does not read looks in v1).
6. **The positive half is activities done, in ink, never summed.** *Played · full walk · jumped up somewhere high.* Under R1 they are what a healthy month looks like in the record. They are never rendered as a two-half comparison (§6.6).
7. **A word that maps to a shipped leaf is an observation on the look's row and nothing more in v1 (R10).** *Off* is a look word; `lethargy` is a tile; neither writes the other, and the look never proposes, prompts or pips. The `Leaf` column in §4.2 / §4.3 records which words *would* map to a leaf so a vet reading the report can set the word beside the tile's count (§8), and so CUL-845 has the map when it rules. A word that maps to a **proposed** leaf (taxonomy §5) is the same observation until that leaf's wave ships (D5 stands; the word never mints the leaf).
8. **Intake is never a look word** (T-3; Dr. Chen: *let it live as a word and the word will be tapped instead of the meal logged*).
9. **Emergency signs are never chips** (T-4).
10. **"Add your own" is a note.** The vocabulary is a constant; a new word is a spec revision with Dr. Chen's sign-off, never an owner-minted key.
11. **The first row is the absence chip, the intake router and *Not herself ›*, at equal cost** (§3.1, T-8); the emergency door sits beneath it.
12. **Head word on Home, ruled label on the full grid.** A label is *head word, gloss* (*Restless, can't settle*); Home shows the head word, the full grid the whole label, and the gloss is the chip's accessibility hint. The gloss is never dropped from the grid (Sam: "Lip-licking" alone lost the findable half).

## §6 Baseline — defined operationally, because R1 rests on it (rewritten after the adversarial pass; the two contradictions and the eligibility hole are closed)

1. **The denominator is answered days:** local days (B-421's one counter) with ≥ 1 look row for the pet whose `outcome` is `observed` or `nothing_unusual` — any number of looks that day is one answered day (R9, T-14). A day with no look row is not an answered day (there is no `not_observed` row — R8). The verb everywhere is *answered* (R11): the act the app can vouch for, never *looked*.
2. **Per-word day counts:** days with ≥ 1 look holding word *w* — a word in two looks the same day counts once. **Observed-absence days:** answered days whose **every** look has `outcome = 'nothing_unusual'`; a day holding both a *nothing unusual* look and an *off* look is an *off* day and not an absence day (the day's classification, never the latest look, decides — T-14). The three counts partition an answered day exactly once (C-4): absence days + days with ≥ 1 symptom-class word + days with only activity-positive words.
3. **"Her usual" is not a computed value.** There is no score, average, median, slope or percentage anywhere. "Her usual" is the record's look history, spoken as counts with denominators.
4. **The comparison:** per-word day counts over **two four-week halves** (the CUL-787 chronicity shape, Shape C stacked compare), each with its own looked-day denominator and **its per-week looked-day placement**: *off on 6 of the 24 days you answered in the last 4 weeks (answered 6 · 5 · 7 · 6 by week) · 0 of 22 in the 4 before (5 · 6 · 5 · 6).* Time-ordered, direction-neutral, no arrows, no verdict word (the Change Contract v1.1, S5).
5. **The eligibility floors (provisional, anchored, adversarial-review-gated like SR-4).** A comparison *pair* renders only when **each half holds ≥ 8 answered days spread over ≥ 3 of its 4 weeks**. The spread is the guard the first draft lacked: under R1 looking is voluntary and the wedge is a worried owner, so eight answered days can be eight days the owner was already worried — and under R9 five looks a day for two days are two answered days, so a burst never reaches a floor on its own — days selected on the outcome (the adversarial pass, gap 9). Counting placement, not just count, and printing the per-week placement beside the pair is what keeps a count-anchored sentence from laundering a selected sample. Below the floor the pair is `notEnoughData` and the copy says what it needs (*Looks build up over time — 5 so far this month*), transparency never solicitation (G8).
6. **The direction rules (one clause, no contradiction):**
   - **A rising symptom-class word count is never withheld** — not by the density rule and **not by the floor**: below the floor the *current-window count* still renders as a count with its denominator (*off on 4 of the 7 days you answered this month*); only the *pair* waits for the floor.
   - **A falling symptom-class word pair is withheld** when looked-day density fell between the halves (the §3.3 asymmetry), and the detail says why (*You also answered on fewer days this month, so we can't tell yet whether there was less to see*).
   - **Activity positives never render as a two-half pair** (the adversarial pass, gap 10 — a positives pair is the caregiver-placebo render): the card prints the current-window count only (*played on 12 of the 24 days you answered*); the detail may print both halves only under the same density caption a symptom word gets, and never on the report's page 1.
7. **The regression-to-the-mean guard (falling pairs only):** owners start looking at a bad stretch. A **falling** symptom-class pair whose earlier half is the **first month of looks** is withheld with the reason (*Comparisons start from the second month of looks*). A rising pair is never subject to this rule (a first month of nothing followed by a month of *subdued* is exactly the onset R1 exists to catch), and the clause is direction-scoped on purpose — the first draft stated it unconditionally in one place and direction-scoped in another.
8. **The caregiver-placebo guard:** looks never enter the trial verdict, the trial-response line or any "improving" copy; Patterns renders the look card *beside* the trial card, never inside it, and below the intake cards (§7); the report prints the look line on the same page as the intake and vomit counts so the vet reads the pair and never the impression alone.
9. **Onset:** the first day a symptom-class word was marked, as a date, beside the **coverage** before it — never beside an absence count: *First day Mochi has seemed off — in the 118 days you've answered since May 3* (the report: *off on 6, first Sep 12; answered on 118 days before it since May 3*). The 118 are the answered days **before** the first-off date, never including it; if the first *off* entry is undone or deleted the receipt is not re-issued on that entry, and the next *off* look computes its own first date honestly from what the record then holds; a backdated look ("Change time") moves the first date with it. The first draft appended "nothing unusual on 116", the most reassuring string in the document rendered at the moment of onset (the adversarial pass, gap 6); the absence count lives in its own row with its own denominator, never on the onset line. A date is free; a duration inherits the engine's floors and is never re-derived at render (C-19).
10. **The vocabulary version:** rows compare only within one `vocab_version`. When a half straddles a version change the pair is withheld with the reason (*The words changed on Sep 1, so the two months can't be compared yet*), and the per-word counts render per version. A version bump is a spec revision (§4.1 rule 10), never a silent relabel.
11. **Same-day co-occurrence** is a receipt with both sides of the control margin (S2), **on Patterns and the report only** (T-17): *Lip-licking on 2 of the 3 vomit days you answered · and on 1 of the 20 other days you answered · 1 vomit day not answered.* Both denominators are **answered days** — the first draft's left side counted all vomit days while its right side counted answered days, so every unanswered vomit day pushed the association toward null ("didn't log" scored as "didn't happen", the reassuring direction); a vomit day with no look is outside both sides and is always said. **The left numerator is never zero and never one** — *0 of the 3 vomit days* is an absence claim at n=3 rendered on the day the owner was worried enough to tap the word (floor 5, n=1 never reassures), and *1 of the 1* is a pattern generator. **Floors:** ≥ 3 answered vomit days and ≥ 10 answered other days in the window. The unit is *days with ≥ 1 vomit*, never the report's episode count. Never "predicts", never "linked", never a tier; and never on Home, where it would sit one card below a gated finding about the same vomits, chosen by the word the owner just tapped — an uncorrected comparison from a twenty-six-word family beside a lane that pays a Bonferroni correction for the same shape.
12. **The floor's item 12 binds every render here:** while `isAnimalNotEating` or a live `intake_decline` holds for the pet, the absence and positive rows are withheld (fail-closed on unloaded facts) and the card says so; symptom-class rows still render.
13. **The instrument-change rule — the leaf half STRUCK (R10), the ascertainment half kept (the third adversarial pass).** The rule for a look-written symptom leaf left with the link it governed; it is in git at `117eb59` (v0.2 §6.13) and on CUL-845 in full. **What survives R10:** the look is an ascertainment intervention on *logging density*, not on rows — an owner who answers daily opens the app daily and logs more meals, and the engine's `loggingDaysInWindow` counts meal rows, so the current half of every density and comparison gate can gain logging days relative to the prior half across the look's ship date or an account's first-look date, and a falling comparison that was correctly withheld becomes publishable through behaviour rather than through rows (the CUL-787 shape).
