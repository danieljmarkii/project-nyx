When a medication course reaches its end

Nyx finished a 28-dose course of Motozol — all 28 logged as given — and the regimen still sits under Current medications, because the only thing that moves a course to past is the owner finding the small End control and tapping it. Even the PM didn't know it was required. A prescription has a defined end; the app should recognise it. The mechanism is ruled — confirm-in-the-loop — so this round is about the words and the states, not whether to build.

This is the wedge user's most-visited health record: the person sent home with a course to finish. When the course is over, the record should close cleanly — but nothing about the app admits the course can end except a red word in a utility row the owner had no reason to read. The fix is not a new mechanism. The card already ends a course; it just never says so at the moment it matters.

Mechanism · ruled
Confirm-in-the-loop
When a course reaches its planned end, surface a calm prompt to finish it. The owner always confirms.
Rejected
Auto-end
Both silent auto-end and auto-end-with-undo. Ending a course is a clinical judgement — the app must never make it for the owner.

01

The gap today

Here is the card Nyx's owner is actually looking at. B-642 already did something deliberate and Dr. Chen-reviewed here — under a full bar it added "When the course ends is your vet's call," precisely so a full progress bar could not be read as "stop now." That line is right, and this round keeps it. The gap is that the line describes the moment and then offers no way to act on it. When the vet does make the call, the owner is left hunting for the red word.

What's wrong

A finished course that can't tell you it's finished

The bar is full, the count says 28 of 28, and the compliance line says every dose went in. Everything reads done — except the app never lets the owner close it without knowing that the quiet, red, underlined End in the action row is the way.

Not the fix: auto-completing at 28. A course can genuinely run past its bottle, and a vet may extend it. The app deciding it's over is the exact early-stop error B-642 was written to avoid.

02

The proposal

Keep B-642's deferral to the vet, and give it a hand to act with. The card gains one calm inset at the moment a course reaches its planned end: the fact, the deferral, and a single confirming tap. It never asserts the course is over — it offers the affordance for when the vet says it is.

Anatomy

The fact, the deferral, the tap — in that order

asks, never asserts reuses handleEndRegimen no auto-end no “complete”

The fact — All 28 doses are logged. For a dose course this is always true at target, because refused and missed doses never count toward it. Stating the record, not judging the pet.

The deferral — B-642's blessed sentence, kept verbatim in spirit so no early-stop read is possible.

The tap — accent-light ground, accent-ink text: calm, not a loud “done” button. It reuses the shipped confirmEndRegimen → handleEndRegimen path; no new write. The utility-row End stays, because you can still end a course early — the prompt is that same action, surfaced at the right moment.

Which words

This is the decision the round exists for. Three directions, same mechanism. The clinical hinge is whether the prompt asks if the pet is done (which subtly implies they should be) or offers the affordance while leaving the call to the vet. Open sub-question for any variant: a second, quiet “Not yet” to dismiss the prompt for now? It reads considerate, but it needs a scrap of stored state and it risks letting an owner permanently silence the one cue that closes the record. My lean: no dismiss in v1 — the inset is calm and inline (not a nudge, so Principle 4 doesn't bite), and it simply stays until the course is ended.

05

What the tap does

The confirming tap reuses the shipped End flow verbatim — the same reassuring dialog, the same handleEndRegimen write. The course moves to Past medications as an owner-marked ending, which is the only kind of ending the record ever shows (H1).

06

What it must never say

The guardrails this surface inherits. Each is a rule the copy and the mechanism are held to, with the ruling it comes from — so the build (and its Dr. Chen / nyx-voice pass) has one place to check against.

NeverInsteadBecause
“Course complete.” / “All done!” / “Finished!” Offer to mark it finished; state the fact. D7 · B-618
Auto-complete the course at target The owner confirms, every time. H1 · B-422
“Great adherence!” / any praise Report the count; make no verdict. clinical-guardrails
“All 10 doses given” on a course with gaps Speak to the calendar; let the compliance line show misses. intake ≠ preference
Prompt an as-needed / open-ended course No defined end → no prompt. State 4
A push notification to “finish” the course Inline only in v1; notifications are their own track. B-661
  • One predicate. “Has this course reached its planned end?” is a single pure function, unit-tested, reused by both triggers — the diet-trial §5.3 lesson applied preemptively.
  • Out of scope, on purpose. Auto-superseding an old course when a new regimen for the same drug starts (needs a drug-identity match that can misfire), a reminder notification (B-661), and mirroring this onto the Home med strip (B-614) — each its own row if pursued.
