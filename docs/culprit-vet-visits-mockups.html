Culprit · design exploration · CUL-878 Vet visits · round 1

The vet visit — before, during, after

A pet sees a vet about every five months, and everything Culprit does is aimed at that room: the log exists so the visit goes better, and the visit is where the next directive comes from. Today the app has the pieces and no shape — a visit form nobody can find, a records library, and a pre-visit rundown two levels inside Ask. Round 1 draws one object with a lifecycle — the appointment becomes the visit, the visit becomes the plan, the plan becomes tracked records — and puts three placement directions side by side. Real content throughout: Nyx, the PM's own cat, with her real record shape (a GI history, a hydrolyzed trial, a PRN antiemetic) and an appointment on Tuesday.

What round 1 proposes — and the principle under each call
Shape
One object, three moments. "Tuesday at Riverside" is the same thing when Jordan books it, prepares for it, sits in it, and leaves with instructions. The screens follow that arc — before / during / after — instead of a form and a separate reminder. Under the hood the upcoming appointment is its own small record so the report's "since last visit" window (which keys off visits that happened) never sees a future date. Invisible complexity, visible calm.
Place
The Pet tab is the home; Home is the moment. A "Vet visits" section joins the Vet report and Vet Files as the vet-facing trio on the Pet tab (the G3 precedent). Home gets an appointment card only inside the window — the days before and the day of — and it leaves when the visit is logged. A permanent Home entry would be a feature menu (Principle 3); a time-bound state card is what Home is for.
Before
The rundown already exists — promote it, don't rebuild it. "Get ready" is the shipped deterministic rundown (counts with denominators, offline, no model) plus two things it lacks: a place for the owner's own questions, and the record's own questions — the open Signal findings and the trial/med state phrased as things to raise. No LLM in v1; the Signal pattern (deterministic first, phrasing later) applies.
After
The plan becomes records, never a second store. "Started a med", "changed the diet", "recheck in six weeks", "here's the paperwork" are doors into the flows the app already has (medication setup, diet-trial setup, the next appointment, Vet Files) with the visit pre-linked. Confirmation over entry, applied to the visit: the vet's directive is the wedge's origin event, and this is where it enters the record.
Record
Recording is drawn as an option, not as v1. A consent-first "ask your vet" moment, an always-visible recording state, exam room only, foreground only, transcription on the phone, audio that never leaves it. It is boxed because it waits on three things outside design: the consent-law read, the privacy-policy and App Privacy edits, and a microphone permission that should not enter the App Store submission binary.
Not drawn
Live suggestions in the room. Dr. Chen, Jordan and the Designer all decline it for v1 (see §F). The questions belong before the visit; the room is for the vet, the pet and the owner's hands.

A · Placement — three directions, side by side

Where does the visit live, and where does it show up?

Two different questions. A home is where the owner goes to browse and manage visits; a moment is where the app brings the visit to the owner when it matters. The current answer to both is an accent chip inside Ask, which only allowlisted accounts can open.

A3 · Status quo — the Ask door current

The only way to the rundown, and from its "No prior visit logged" tile, the only way to the visit form. Ask is allowlisted to one account, so for everyone else the whole vet-visit surface does not exist. Kept as a secondary door in A1+A2 — Ask stays the right place for "heading to the vet?" once it opens up — never the primary.

Recommendation: A1 + A2 together. They answer different questions and neither substitutes for the other. A1 without A2 leaves the prep surface eight cards deep on the day it matters; A2 without A1 leaves visits with no home once the card leaves Home.
Rejected: a fifth tab. A visit every five months is not a destination you return to and act within (the navigation rule); it is a doorway. The tab bar was also just re-cut (DP-1).
Open: the window length. The card appears N days before the appointment. Three days keeps Home calm; seven matches "get the report to the clinic ahead of time". Drawn at three; a reaction decides.

C · During the visit — capture

Notes in v1. The recording is one consent sheet away, when it's ruled in.

In the room Jordan is holding a dog and Sam has two carriers. Whatever the app asks for here has to survive one thumb and divided attention. v1 is a notes field that opens from the appointment card, with the owner's questions carried in as a checklist. The recording is drawn beside it as the v1.x option, with the consent moment it cannot ship without.

C2 · The consent moment v1.x · gated

Every recording begins here, every time — not a one-time onboarding toggle. The sheet does four things: says the law plainly without a lecture, makes asking the vet the default path (the vet-side scribes already ask the client; this is the mirror), states the data facts before the first byte, and confines the recording to the exam room — a waiting room is other people's conversations, and no state's consent rule covers a bystander. The primary button is a claim the owner makes about everyone in the room, deliberately un-pre-checked and stored with a timestamp. Whether the phone also says it is recording when it starts is an open decision (§F).

C3 · Recording, honestly v1.x · gated

The state is never hidden: a dark bar with the elapsed time, what is happening to the audio, and Stop; the screen stays awake and the recording runs in the foreground only — lock the phone or leave the app and it stops, and says so. The transcript grows live and reads as the vet's words, unattributed — no "Dr. Chen said / you said", because speaker identification is a voiceprint question under Illinois, Texas and Washington law and buys the owner little. Typing still works alongside. Nothing here calls a model.

D · After the visit — the plan becomes records

Finish the visit in the parking lot, and let the plan land where it belongs

The vet's instructions are the reason the app exists — "sent home with a directive" is the wedge. Today they would go into a 600-character notes box and stay there. The after-visit screen turns each instruction into a confirmation: the med course, the diet trial, the recheck and the paperwork each open the flow the app already has, with the visit pre-linked. Nothing is stored twice.

F · What is deliberately not drawn, and why

Four things the brainstorm named that round 1 leaves off the page

Live question suggestions in the room. Dr. Chen: a client reading prompts off a phone mid-exam degrades the consult and the relationship. Jordan: I'm holding the dog. Designer: a suggestion feed during the most stressful fifteen minutes is a firehose at the moment of event (Principle 1). Engineer: it needs network in a basement exam room and a model call every minute. The team's position is that the questions belong before the visit (B1) and a "did you ask about…" check belongs after (D3). Recorded as a dissent from the PM's prompt 7.2, not a silent drop — a v2 experiment if real visits show the pre-visit list is not enough.
Rich-text notes. Formatting is a decision at the moment of capture; the fields carry the structure instead (what the vet said · the plan · your questions). A bulleted, Markdown-lite field is the v1.x step if notes grow long; a WebView editor is not on the table on a screen used one-thumbed. The technical lane's read on the editor options is in the spec.
The AI visit summary. Drawn only as B2's labelled option. It is the first surface where a third party's speech (the vet's) would cross the LLM boundary, so it takes the same ruling Vet Files' AI phase takes, with T&S at the table, before any build. The v1 "last time" block is the owner's own recorded plan, verbatim.
The microphone in the submission binary. Adding a microphone permission string days before App Store review is a review risk and a privacy-policy edit (the current policy says "no microphone"), and Apple's guideline 5.1.1(ix) asks that apps handling sensitive information be submitted by a legal entity rather than an individual. Recording ships in a build after launch, or not at all — that is a PM call the spec's decision briefs put plainly.
The spoken announcement (open). Trust & Safety: the tap is the owner's claim; a short line the phone speaks at the start — captured in the recording — is the record that the room was told, and it is the statutory form of consent in Washington, Oregon's "specifically informed", and Apple's own call-recording precedent. Designer: a phone announcing itself in a small exam room is awkward and will make owners skip the feature. Dr. Chen: it is what makes the staff comfortable; the clinic's own scribes ask out loud too. Drawn as a line under the recording bar in C3; the PM rules default-on and locked, default-on with an opt-out, or tap-only.
