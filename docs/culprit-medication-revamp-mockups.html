A home for the course

Medication works in Culprit, but its parts don't agree with each other. A course is set up on the Pet tab, doses are logged from +, nothing on the new Home says a course is running, and the app has no screen for the course itself. This round draws one answer: every course gets its own screen, like the diet trial's, and the rest of the app points at it. The plumbing gets fixed first, because a screen that counts doses is only as honest as the rule that decides which course a dose belongs to.

§06

What a course screen can honestly show

The trial screen's vomiting line works because food does not suppress vomiting. A drug often does. So the answer to observation 7 is the record of the course itself, stated as facts, and never a verdict on whether it worked.

YesThe course as entered. Dose, route, how often, since when, and the length you entered as a date ("the 10 days you entered run to Sep 30"). A date answers how much longer without a countdown.
YesLast dose logged. "Last dose logged today, 7:40 AM." The household question: did someone already give it?
YesThe ledger, with counts. Every row carries its count; the rows add up to the caption. No percentage, no tick, no streak.
YesHow much she got. Counts of All of it / Some of it / None of it / Not given, and how each was given (tablet or gel, in food, in a pill pocket). "6 of the 7 went in a meal with no intake logged" is thin delivery evidence Dr. Chen wants to see.
YesWhere it came from. The visit it started at, the prescriber, and past courses of the same drug as dated facts. Motozol logged again from Sep 21 is the recurrence a vet looks for.
YesDoses after the end. Shown beside the course, never in its count, with a way to change the end date (brief D4).
SplitSymptom counts since the start. The Data Scientist: yes, quoted word for word from the Signal's EN-10 line, so its gate travels with it. Dr. Chen: no, under a drug's name "1 cough since Sep 21" becomes the drug's report card. Not in v1 either way (EN-10 has not shipped).
ReportDose times. A time-of-day view belongs on the vet report (8 AM and 11 AM is effectively once a day for a time-dependent antibiotic). On the screen, times appear on the dose list only, with no target band and no "late".
ReportThe overlap. "Vomiting logged on 6 of the 14 days of the Motozol course, 1 of the 14 before", as dated counts on the vet report (CUL-300), and only with at least 7 logged days on each side.
NeverA before-and-after compare. Drawn or in words. A drug often suppresses the symptom you would compare (Cerenia, a steroid), regression to the mean alone makes a fall after a peak, and a gated compare could only ever print rises, so its silence would read as good news.
NeverA zero beside a drug start. "0 coughs since the prednisone" is a claim the record cannot make on an owner surface.
NeverPills left in the bottle. "#28 dispensed" is a pill count, not therapy delivered: a spat pill leaves the bottle too. Never beside "Dose X of Y", and never a course length prefilled from a chronic or as-needed label (#60 methimazole is a refill, not a course).
NeverA grade. No adherence percentage, no "missed", no "due", no "helped", "working" or "settled".
§07

The Signal and medication (observation 3)

By design, medication enters the Signal as context and as a confounder: a course is a date span, a dose a point in time. When a drug is on board more on symptom days than on control days, that symptom's food findings are suppressed; when it is merely present, they are capped. A card can carry "During an active {drug} course".

In production, the dose half has read nothing since June 23. A June migration gave doses a second link to events, the query became ambiguous, and the code treats the error as "no doses". Deployed generate-signal v39 (this morning) failed 41 of 41 dose reads in the retained logs; the med-with-food pairing is dead with it, and Ask has the same query. CUL-1099 was marked Done on Sep 26 because an unrelated PR carried it as an attachment. It is reopened, and the Engines v3 project still lists it as shipped.

  • CUL-1355 · filedLive today: the Signal's "down from N episodes the week before" reads no medication. Mochi starts Cerenia on Monday and the card says "down from 5", naming no drug. An antiemetic can quiet the vomiting of an obstruction. The fix withholds the falling comparison when a drug's exposure differs between the two weeks.
  • CUL-417 · evidence addedAny drug present in any matched window blocks Established, and the vet report prints only Established findings. A cat on daily methimazole can never put a food finding on her report. A drug on board in both windows of a matched pair is controlled by the matching itself; the cap protects nothing there (a better-than-the-rule brief, §12).
  • An active course is on board foreverA course nobody ended counts as on board indefinitely, so Schrödinger's 98-day cetirizine caps every food finding in that span. The Data Scientist proposes spans bounded by the last logged dose plus one tail, as B-422 did for the diet trial.
  • Turning doses on needs a replayFixing CUL-1099 changes live findings for every pet with doses, mostly by capping food findings, so Established lines can drop off vet reports the morning it deploys. Its adversarial pass should be a per-pet replay diff, landed with the cap rule.
  • What comes next, and what never doesA refusal backs up another signal; on its own it escalates only for a critical drug. On a safety card the drug line goes under the ask ("mention it on the call"), never above it. A drug start never quietly ends a safety card. No card ever says a drug caused or cured anything.

§09

Reminders, the question every competitor answers

Every medication app leads with reminders, and the July landscape named them the category's largest parity gap. Culprit deliberately has none: the notification foundation's G4 (2026-08-02) bars any medication reminder, because "an owner who relies on a med reminder that doesn't fire is the worst-case hazard". The research supports the worry: reliable critical reminders depend on privileges the phone controls, iOS drops scheduled notifications past 64 per app, and Android battery savers kill alarm apps.

The team split. Engineering and T&S hold G4 for this track: a phone cannot see the other phone's log, so a 30-minute "log it?" follow-up on phone B after a dose logged on phone A can cause a double dose, the worst direction on insulin; and drug names on a lock screen reveal a controlled substance. Dr. Chen argues that forgotten doses fail more courses than notifications ever could, and names a safe shape: Apple's (a time you set per course, off by default, one follow-up that asks you to log, escalation only by your choice) plus three additions: fail loudly on the course screen when notifications are off, stop only when you end the course, and send household follow-ups from the server, which sees every phone. That last part reopens the push-provider question. Jordan: "No pill notifications I didn't set up. I have a phone alarm."

Recommendation: hold G4 for this track, and give B-227 (CUL-359) its own spec built on Dr. Chen's shape, with the household question answered first. Brief D7.

§10

What the leaders do

Full brief, every claim tagged by strength and sourced: docs/research/2026-09-medication-competitive-landscape.md. Four load-bearing claims were re-checked against their sources before they went on this page.

No pet app records a refused or partial doseGiven, skipped, early and late are the most any offers; yet a resistant pet is the top owner-reported barrier, and 78.7% of cat owners report spat-out tablets. Culprit's given / partial / refused, and the dose in doubt, are already ahead of the category.
Self-report overstates adherencePill caps that log every opening measured a median 64% of antibiotic doses on time while owners reported 100% (Adams 2005, n=90), and vets could not predict who complied. Counted records, not attestations.
A third of pets are dosed by more than one person38% of dogs and 32% of cats. Pet double-dosing is unmeasured; the small pet apps guard it with a "given at 13:04 · Tony" stamp. That needs the household primitive (B-292), an open question.
The big players treat medication as refillsChewy, PetDesk and Banfield frame medication as refills and due dates. Doses, courses and the vet-facing record are open ground.
Human leaders assume the first reminder can failApple re-asks 30 minutes later if nothing was logged, and lets you turn on Critical Alerts for each medication. The user, never the app, decides which drug is loud.
Streaks and adherence scores ship, and fail as predictedMyTherapy has streaks, CareClinic an adherence score. The research on broken streaks says they hurt most when people blame themselves.
"Did it help?" done well is a correlation with a warningBearable: "because they're correlative and not causal we recommend not making any decisions about your medication using Effect On reports." No pet app analyses a drug's effect.
One pet app auto-completes a course on its end datePetfetti does. The 2025 veterinary guideline for skin infections sets an initial course "followed by re-examination", and 27.8% of cat owners who didn't finish stopped near the end. D7 is on solid ground.
The best delight is an honest object with a countmyPill's pack reads "You have 18 active pills left in your pack". Apple lets you design each pill's look. No medication app celebrates a finished course.
Pets > $ is not the category normFree tiers cap history at 30 or 7 days, and vet PDFs and family sync sit behind paywalls. Keeping the record, the report and the pill face free is a position.
