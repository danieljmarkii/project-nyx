And your question

How sure are we that AI can pull this out of your notes

Tested, not guessed: four runs across three model sizes on Nyx's two real visit notes, scored by hand, beside four runs from the visit summary test a day earlier. It bears on one direction above, "Tell Culprit", and on suggestions from a visit.

Sure

It finds what typed notes hold and quotes it word for word. Across eight runs: no invented diagnosis, no invented date. Every run found the vomiting, the cough, the ear infection, chicken as your own untested suspicion, and all three medicines given with no course.

Sure, and it matters most

It leaves out what it must. Every run set aside "wasn't very concerned about this loss", the normal exam findings and the seasonal cause, and said so.

The best result

Every run noticed your Nov 14 notes say Nyx is off prednisolone while a dose is logged that day, and asked you to confirm. A count from the logs can't do that.

Less sure

Completeness. The smallest model missed the weight loss twice and two instructions each time. You can't see what is missing from a list you are checking.

One dangerous misread

A small-model run turned "If we need to start Monday re meds we can" into "restart them on Monday". The words are in the notes, so a quote check passes it.

Not tested at all

Any document. No invoice photo, PDF, discharge sheet, lab result or handwriting has been read in either test. Nyx's two stored files, dropped into a session, would close that.

The runs went through tooling that loads this project's safety rules, so they leaned cautious. Treat this as the shape of what goes right and wrong, not a rate. Detail: docs/research/2026-11-pet-memory-extraction-test.md.
